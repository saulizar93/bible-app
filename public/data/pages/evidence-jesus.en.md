## The question

Did Jesus of Nazareth really live, die on a Roman cross, and rise from the dead? This page weighs the evidence the way Lee Strobel, a former atheist and legal journalist, did in _The Case for Christ_ (1998): by calling witnesses — friendly and hostile — and asking which explanation best fits all the facts.

> **Luke 1:1-4:** "Forasmuch as many have taken in hand to set forth in order a declaration of those things which are most surely believed among us, Even as they delivered them unto us, which from the beginning were eyewitnesses, and ministers of the word; It seemed good to me also, having had perfect understanding of all things from the very first, to write unto thee in order, most excellent Theophilus, That thou mightest know the certainty of those things, wherein thou hast been instructed."

> **2 Peter 1:16:** "For we have not followed cunningly devised fables, when we made known unto you the power and coming of our Lord Jesus Christ, but were eyewitnesses of his majesty."

> **1 John 1:1-3:** "That which was from the beginning, which we have heard, which we have seen with our eyes, which we have looked upon, and our hands have handled, of the Word of life; (For the life was manifested, and we have seen it, and bear witness, and shew unto you that eternal life, which was with the Father, and was manifested unto us;) That which we have seen and heard declare we unto you, that ye also may have fellowship with us: and truly our fellowship is with the Father, and with his Son Jesus Christ."

It takes more faith **not** to believe than to believe. If Jesus did not rise, then some Jewish teacher from a small village, with no army, no money, no writings of his own and a ministry of about three years, managed — after being publicly executed as a criminal — to:

1. **Transform Jewish faith and practice** that had been held for 1,500 years: animal sacrifices for sin ceased among his followers, the day of worship moved to Sunday, Gentiles were welcomed without circumcision, and the long-awaited Messiah turned out to be not a general who would drive out Rome but a crucified man.
2. **Convince his own followers to die** rather than deny that they had seen him alive — with nothing to gain: no money, no power, no pleasure, only prison and death.
3. **Conquer the Roman Empire** — about 60 million people — without a sword, so that within three centuries the empire that crucified him bowed to his name.

What could explain all of that? Let us look at the evidence.

# 1. Jesus existed: the non-Christian witnesses

Some people on the internet claim Jesus never existed. Almost no historian agrees — including those who are not Christians. The agnostic New Testament scholar **Bart Ehrman**, a critic of Christianity, wrote a whole book to answer that claim and concluded that Jesus certainly existed (_Did Jesus Exist?_, 2012). Here is what writers who were **not** his followers said about him within about 150 years of his death:

### Flavius Josephus (Jewish historian, c. 93)

Josephus, a Jewish priest and general who wrote for the Romans, mentions Jesus twice. In the first passage (the _Testimonium Flavianum_), later Christian copyists added some phrases, but most scholars accept that Josephus wrote a core like this:

> "At this time there appeared Jesus, a wise man. For he was a doer of startling deeds, a teacher of people who receive the truth with pleasure. And he gained a following both among many Jews and among many of Greek origin… And when Pilate, because of an accusation made by the leading men among us, condemned him to the cross, those who had loved him previously did not cease to do so… And up until this very day the tribe of Christians, named after him, has not died out." (_Antiquities of the Jews_ 18.63-64)

An Arabic version of this passage, preserved by the tenth-century bishop Agapius, adds: "**They reported that he had appeared to them three days after his crucifixion and that he was alive.**"

In the second passage, which nearly all scholars accept as genuine, Josephus describes the execution of "**James, the brother of Jesus who was called Christ**" in A.D. 62 (_Antiquities_ 20.200).

### Tacitus (Roman historian, c. 116)

Tacitus, the greatest Roman historian and no friend of Christians, describes how Nero blamed them for the great fire of Rome:

> "**Christus**, from whom the name had its origin, **suffered the extreme penalty during the reign of Tiberius at the hands of one of our procurators, Pontius Pilatus**, and a most mischievous superstition, thus checked for the moment, again broke out not only in Judaea, the first source of the evil, but even in Rome… Accordingly, an arrest was first made of all who pleaded guilty; then, upon their information, an immense multitude was convicted… Mockery of every sort was added to their deaths. Covered with the skins of beasts, they were torn by dogs and perished, or were nailed to crosses, or were doomed to the flames and burnt, to serve as a nightly illumination." (_Annals_ 15.44)

### Pliny the Younger (Roman governor, c. 112)

Pliny, governor of Bithynia, wrote to Emperor Trajan asking what to do with Christians. He executed those who would not deny Christ, and reported that they met on a fixed day before dawn and "**sang in alternate verses a hymn to Christ, as to a god**" (_Letters_ 10.96).

### Suetonius (Roman historian, c. 121)

"Since the Jews constantly made disturbances at the instigation of **Chrestus**, he [Emperor Claudius] expelled them from Rome" (_Life of Claudius_ 25.4) — the same expulsion mentioned in Acts 18:2. Suetonius also records that under Nero "punishment was inflicted on the Christians" (_Life of Nero_ 16).

### Mara bar Serapion (Syrian philosopher, after 73)

In a letter to his son, this non-Christian Stoic compares the deaths of Socrates, Pythagoras and Jesus: "What advantage did the Jews gain from **executing their wise King**? It was just after that that their kingdom was abolished."

### Lucian of Samosata (Greek satirist, c. 165)

Lucian mocked Christians, but confirmed that they worship "**the man who was crucified in Palestine** because he introduced this new cult into the world," and that they despise death and willingly give themselves up (_The Passing of Peregrinus_ 11-13).

### Celsus (pagan philosopher, c. 175)

Celsus wrote the first book-length attack on Christianity. He did not deny that Jesus did miracles; he claimed Jesus learned **sorcery** in Egypt (quoted in Origen, _Against Celsus_ 1.28, 1.38).

### The Babylonian Talmud (Jewish tradition)

"**On the eve of the Passover Yeshu was hanged** [crucified]… because he has practiced sorcery and enticed Israel to apostasy" (_Sanhedrin_ 43a). His enemies did not deny that he did wonders; they called them sorcery. They did not deny his death; they confirm it, on the eve of Passover — exactly as John 19:14 says.

### Thallus and Phlegon (historians, 1st–2nd century)

According to the Christian writer Julius Africanus (c. 220), the historian Thallus tried to explain the **darkness at the crucifixion** as a solar eclipse — which is impossible at Passover, when the moon is full. Africanus also cites Phlegon for a great darkness in the reign of Tiberius. Their works are lost, but even their attempts to explain the darkness away show that it was being discussed.

### The Qur'an (7th century)

Islam honors Jesus as the Messiah, born of a virgin, who healed the blind and the lepers and raised the dead (Qur'an 3:45-49; 5:110). (The Qur'an, written six centuries later, denies that he was crucified, 4:157 — against every first- and second-century source, friendly and hostile.)

### What these sources establish

From non-Christian sources alone we know that Jesus lived in Judea in the first century; that he was called the Christ and a wise man; that he was known for amazing deeds (his enemies called them sorcery); that he was crucified under Pontius Pilate during the reign of Tiberius, on the eve of Passover; that his followers believed he had risen and worshipped him "as a god"; and that they spread rapidly to Rome and were willing to die for it.

# 2. The New Testament is the best-preserved book of the ancient world

- More than **5,800 Greek manuscripts** of the New Testament survive, plus over 10,000 in Latin and thousands in Syriac, Coptic and other languages. The more copies you have from different places, the more precisely you can reconstruct the original.
- The earliest fragment, P52 (John 18), is commonly dated to the first half of the second century — within a few decades of the original.
- For comparison, the _Annals_ of Tacitus survive in a handful of manuscripts, the earliest about 800 years after he wrote; for most classical authors we have a few dozen copies, centuries later.
- Even without any Greek manuscripts, the New Testament could be almost entirely reconstructed from quotations in the early Christian writers.
- The books were written **within the lifetime of eyewitnesses**: Paul's letters from about A.D. 50–60, the Gospels by about 70–95. Hostile eyewitnesses could have refuted them. Paul could say to a king: "this thing was not done in a corner" (Acts 26:26).

The small differences between the Gospels (was Jesus' robe scarlet or purple? how many angels at the tomb?) are what we expect from **independent** witnesses telling the same story from different angles. If the four accounts were identical word for word, we would suspect collusion.

# 3. The facts that almost all historians accept

Gary Habermas surveyed more than 2,000 scholarly works on the resurrection written since 1975, by believers and skeptics alike. A small set of facts is accepted by nearly all of them (the "minimal facts"):

**1. Jesus died by crucifixion.** Even the atheist scholar Gerd Lüdemann wrote: "**Jesus' death as a consequence of crucifixion is indisputable**" (_The Resurrection of Christ_, 2004).

**2. His disciples sincerely believed that he rose and appeared to them.** Lüdemann again: "It may be taken as historically certain that Peter and the disciples had experiences after Jesus' death in which Jesus appeared to them as the risen Christ" (_What Really Happened to Jesus_, 1995). The agnostic E. P. Sanders: "That Jesus' followers (and later Paul) had resurrection experiences is, in my judgment, a fact" (_The Historical Figure of Jesus_, 1993).

**3. The earliest report is very early.** In 1 Corinthians 15 (about A.D. 55), Paul passes on a creed he had "received," which most scholars date to within a few years of the crucifixion — Paul met Peter and James in Jerusalem about three years after his conversion (Galatians 1:18-19):

> **1 Corinthians 15:3-8:** "For I delivered unto you first of all that which I also received, how that Christ died for our sins according to the scriptures; And that he was buried, and that he rose again the third day according to the scriptures: And that he was seen of Cephas, then of the twelve: After that, he was seen of above five hundred brethren at once; of whom the greater part remain unto this present, but some are fallen asleep. After that, he was seen of James; then of all the apostles. And last of all he was seen of me also, as of one born out of due time."

More than five hundred people at once, "of whom the greater part remain unto this present" — in other words: go and ask them.

**4. Paul, the persecutor, was converted.** He was destroying the church until, he says, the risen Jesus appeared to him (Acts 9; Galatians 1:13-16).

**5. James, the skeptical brother, was converted.** During Jesus' ministry his brothers did not believe in him (John 7:5; Mark 3:21). After the resurrection James became leader of the Jerusalem church and was martyred for it (Josephus, _Antiquities_ 20.200). What would it take to convince you that your brother was the Lord?

**6. The tomb was empty.** The resurrection was preached in Jerusalem, the very city where Jesus was buried; if the body had been there, the authorities could have ended Christianity by producing it. Instead, the earliest Jewish response was that "his disciples came by night, and stole him away" (Matthew 28:11-15; also Justin Martyr, _Dialogue_ 108) — an admission that the tomb was empty.

> **Matthew 28:11-15:** "Now when they were going, behold, some of the watch came into the city, and shewed unto the chief priests all the things that were done. And when they were assembled with the elders, and had taken counsel, they gave large money unto the soldiers, Saying, Say ye, His disciples came by night, and stole him away while we slept. And if this come to the governor’s ears, we will persuade him, and secure you. So they took the money, and did as they were taught: and this saying is commonly reported among the Jews until this day."

# 4. Details no one would invent

Historians trust details that are **embarrassing** to the people who report them, because no one invents material that hurts their own cause:

- **Women were the first witnesses of the empty tomb** (Matthew 28:1-10; Mark 16:1-8; Luke 24:1-12; John 20:1-18). In that culture a woman's testimony was not accepted in court; Josephus records the rule, "Let not the testimony of women be admitted" (_Antiquities_ 4.219). If the story had been invented, the inventors would have made men — Peter and John — the first witnesses.
- **The disciples look bad:** they argue about who is greatest, fall asleep in Gethsemane, flee at the arrest; Peter denies Jesus three times; Jesus calls Peter "Satan" (Matthew 16:23).
- **Jesus' own family** thought he was "beside himself" (Mark 3:21).
- **Jesus cries on the cross**, "My God, my God, why hast thou forsaken me?" (Matthew 27:46).
- **He is executed by crucifixion**, the most shameful death in the Roman world; Deuteronomy 21:23 said, "he that is hanged is accursed of God." No Jew inventing a Messiah would have him crucified.

# 5. They died for it

People will die for something they **believe** is true, even if it is false. But the apostles were in a unique position: they were not dying for something they had been told; they were dying for something they claimed to have **seen with their own eyes**. Liars make poor martyrs. No one dies for what he knows is a lie, with nothing to gain.

- **James son of Zebedee** was killed with the sword by Herod Agrippa I (Acts 12:2).
- **Peter** and **Paul** were executed in Rome under Nero (_1 Clement_ 5; Ignatius, _Romans_ 4; Tertullian, _Prescription against Heretics_ 36).
- **James the Lord's brother** was stoned in Jerusalem in 62 (Josephus, _Antiquities_ 20.200).
- Ancient tradition says that most of the other apostles also died as martyrs, and that John died old after exile on Patmos. (Sean McDowell's _The Fate of the Apostles_, 2015, examines each tradition and its reliability.)
- And Tacitus and Pliny describe ordinary Christians who chose to be burned, crucified or torn by dogs rather than deny Christ.

# 6. The alternative explanations fail

- **"The disciples stole the body."** Then they died for what they knew was a lie — and the Roman guard and seal (Matthew 27:62-66) stand in the way.
- **"Jesus did not really die; he fainted and revived."** Roman executioners were experts in death; a spear was thrust into his side (John 19:34). A half-dead man, whipped and crucified, could not move a great stone, overpower guards, and convince his followers that he had **conquered** death.
- **"They went to the wrong tomb."** Then the authorities would simply have gone to the right one and produced the body.
- **"They hallucinated."** Hallucinations happen in individual minds; they are not shared by groups — but Jesus appeared to the Twelve together and to more than five hundred at once, and he ate with them (Luke 24:42-43). And hallucinations would not empty a tomb, or convert an enemy like Paul or a skeptic like James.
- **"It is a legend that grew over time."** Legends need generations to grow. The creed in 1 Corinthians 15 goes back to within a few years of the events, while the eyewitnesses were alive.

Only one explanation accounts for **all** the facts: Jesus rose from the dead.

# 7. An effect that demands a cause

Think of the size of the change:

- Jews, for whom the Law of Moses was life and death, **stopped offering sacrifices** for sin, because "Christ died for our sins" once for all.
- Jews, who had kept the Sabbath for fifteen centuries, began to gather on **the first day of the week**, the day of the resurrection (Acts 20:7; 1 Corinthians 16:2).
- Strict monotheists, who would die rather than worship a man, worshipped Jesus as God manifest in the flesh — and still confessed that "the Lord our God is one Lord."
- A small group of fishermen and tax collectors, hiding behind locked doors after the crucifixion (John 20:19), within weeks were preaching openly in the same city that had killed their master (Acts 2).
- From about a thousand believers in A.D. 40, the church grew — by the estimate of the sociologist Rodney Stark — to some six million by A.D. 300, without armies, while being persecuted (_The Rise of Christianity_, 1996).

Something enormous happened in Jerusalem around A.D. 30. The disciples said what it was:

> **Acts 2:32:** "This Jesus hath God raised up, whereof we all are witnesses."

> **1 Corinthians 15:14:** "And if Christ be not risen, then is our preaching vain, and your faith is also vain."

> **1 Corinthians 15:17:** "And if Christ be not raised, your faith is vain; ye are yet in your sins."

> **1 Corinthians 15:20:** "But now is Christ risen from the dead, and become the firstfruits of them that slept."

# 8. Prophecy

Centuries before Jesus, the Hebrew Scriptures described the Messiah's birthplace, his suffering and death for the sins of others, and his resurrection:

> **Micah 5:2:** "But thou, Beth-lehem Ephratah, though thou be little among the thousands of Judah, yet out of thee shall he come forth unto me that is to be ruler in Israel; whose goings forth have been from of old, from everlasting."

> **Isaiah 53:5-6:** "But he was wounded for our transgressions, he was bruised for our iniquities: the chastisement of our peace was upon him; and with his stripes we are healed. All we like sheep have gone astray; we have turned every one to his own way; and the LORD hath laid on him the iniquity of us all."

> **Psalm 22:16-18:** "For dogs have compassed me: the assembly of the wicked have inclosed me: they pierced my hands and my feet. I may tell all my bones: they look and stare upon me. They part my garments among them, and cast lots upon my vesture."

> **Psalm 16:10:** "For thou wilt not leave my soul in hell; neither wilt thou suffer thine Holy One to see corruption."

The Great Isaiah Scroll found among the Dead Sea Scrolls, copied more than a century before Jesus was born, contains Isaiah 53 — so it cannot have been written after the fact.

# Conclusion

Jesus is the best-attested figure of the ancient world: friends and enemies, Jews, Romans and Greeks agree that he lived, that he was a wise teacher known for amazing deeds, that he was crucified under Pontius Pilate, and that his followers proclaimed — and died proclaiming — that he had risen. The tomb was empty; his enemies admitted it. His persecutor and his skeptical brother became his servants. And a movement with no power but its message overcame the Roman Empire. The only explanation big enough for all of it is the one the witnesses gave.

"Thomas answered and said unto him, My Lord and my God. Jesus saith unto him, Thomas, because thou hast seen me, thou hast believed: blessed are they that have not seen, and yet have believed." (John 20:28-29)
