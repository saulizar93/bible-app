**Sola Scriptura** ("Scripture alone") means that the written Word of God is the **only infallible rule** of faith and practice: every teaching, tradition and church must be judged by it, and it contains everything we need to know to be saved. It does not mean that teachers, history or the early Christians are useless — it means that none of them stands **above** the Bible or **beside** it as an equal authority.

The Roman Catholic Church teaches the opposite. "The Church… does not derive her certainty about all revealed truths from the holy Scriptures alone. Both Scripture and Tradition must be accepted and honored with equal sentiments of devotion and reverence" (_Catechism of the Catholic Church_ 82; Vatican II, _Dei Verbum_ 9; Council of Trent, Session 4, 1546). This page lets Scripture speak for itself.

# 9. Catholic tradition against the commandments of God

These are not abuses of a few priests; they are official teachings that Scripture contradicts — exactly what Jesus condemned in Mark 7.

- **Images.** God said, "Thou shalt not make unto thee any graven image… Thou shalt not bow down thyself to them" (Exodus 20:4-5). Rome teaches that "the Christian veneration of images is not contrary to the first commandment" (CCC 2132), following the Second Council of Nicaea (787), which anathematized those who refuse to venerate them (see **Image veneration**).
- **Praying to others than God.** "There is one God, and one mediator between God and men, the man Christ Jesus" (1 Timothy 2:5). Rome calls Mary "Advocate, Helper, Benefactress, and Mediatrix" (CCC 969) and teaches the faithful to ask her and the saints to intercede.
- **"Call no man your father"** (Matthew 23:9) — the Pope is called "Holy Father," a title Jesus gave only to God (John 17:11).
- **Forbidding to marry and commanding to abstain from meats** (1 Timothy 4:1-3) — mandatory celibacy for priests (Lateran II, 1139) and days of obligatory abstinence from meat.
- **Heavy burdens.** Missing Sunday Mass without a serious reason is "a grave sin" (CCC 2181); sins must be confessed to a priest; purgatory must be shortened by Masses and indulgences. None of these burdens is laid on believers in the New Testament (Acts 15:28-29; Galatians 5:1).

> **Exodus 20:4-5:** "Thou shalt not make unto thee any graven image, or any likeness of any thing that is in heaven above, or that is in the earth beneath, or that is in the water under the earth: Thou shalt not bow down thyself to them, nor serve them: for I the LORD thy God am a jealous God, visiting the iniquity of the fathers upon the children unto the third and fourth generation of them that hate me;"

> **1 Timothy 2:5:** "For there is one God, and one mediator between God and men, the man Christ Jesus;"

> **Matthew 23:9:** "And call no man your father upon the earth: for one is your Father, which is in heaven."

> **1 Timothy 4:1-3:** "Now the Spirit speaketh expressly, that in the latter times some shall depart from the faith, giving heed to seducing spirits, and doctrines of devils; Speaking lies in hypocrisy; having their conscience seared with a hot iron; Forbidding to marry, and commanding to abstain from meats, which God hath created to be received with thanksgiving of them which believe and know the truth."

# 10. Not "Scripture and tradition" — "the Church alone"

Rome says it holds Scripture **and** Tradition. But who decides what Tradition is, and what Scripture means? "The task of interpreting the Word of God authentically has been entrusted **solely** to the Magisterium of the Church, that is, to the Pope and to the bishops in communion with him" (CCC 100; _Dei Verbum_ 10). _Dei Verbum_ adds that this teaching office "is not above the word of God, but serves it" — yet only the same office may say what the word of God means, so in practice there is no appeal beyond it.

That is why, when Scripture and tradition collide, the Church always wins. The Immaculate Conception (1854) and the Assumption of Mary (1950) are not in Scripture, and they are not in the earliest Fathers; they became dogmas because the Pope defined them. Pope Pius IX, when told at the First Vatican Council that papal infallibility was not part of the tradition, reportedly answered: "**I am the tradition**" (_La tradizione sono io_, 1870). That is not _Sola Scriptura_ versus "Scripture plus tradition" — it is **_Sola Ecclesia_**: the Church alone.

The Bible's way is the Bereans' way: every teacher, every church and every tradition is tested by what is written.

# 11. Common objections

- **"The Bible does not list its own canon."** God's people recognized His Word before any council in Rome; see **Who gave us the Bible?**
- **"Hold the traditions… whether by word, or our epistle" (2 Thessalonians 2:15).** Notice Paul's two categories: "By word"- the apostolic teaching Paul delivered orally while present with the Thessalonians; and by "our epistle": the same apostolic teaching Paul delivered in writing. The contrast is between two modes of delivering the same teaching, not between two **different** complementary sources of information. Both forms came from the apostles themselves. Paul never appeals to a tradition that contradicts or adds to what he wrote; he instructs the church to distance itself from anyone who does not follow it (2 Thessalonians 3:6, 14).
- **"Not everything Jesus did is written" (John 21:25).** True — but John says what **is** written is enough to believe and have life (John 20:31).
- **"The church is the pillar and ground of the truth" (1 Timothy 3:15).** A pillar holds up the truth; it does not create it or sit above it. See **The One True Church**.

> **2 Thessalonians 2:15:** "Therefore, brethren, stand fast, and hold the traditions which ye have been taught, whether by word, or our epistle."

> **2 Thessalonians 3:6:** "Now we command you, brethren, in the name of our Lord Jesus Christ, that ye withdraw yourselves from every brother that walketh disorderly, and not after the tradition which he received of us."

> **2 Thessalonians 3:14:** "And if any man obey not our word by this epistle, note that man, and have no company with him, that he may be ashamed."

> **John 21:25:** "And there are also many other things which Jesus did, the which, if they should be written every one, I suppose that even the world itself could not contain the books that should be written. Amen."

> **John 20:31:** "But these are written, that ye might believe that Jesus is the Christ, the Son of God; and that believing ye might have life through his name."

# Conclusion

Traditions change, councils contradict each other, and popes reverse their predecessors. The written Word does not change.

> **Isaiah 40:8:** "The grass withereth, the flower fadeth: but the word of our God shall stand for ever."

> **Psalm 119:89:** "For ever, O LORD, thy word is settled in heaven."

> **Matthew 24:35:** "Heaven and earth shall pass away, but my words shall not pass away."

> **Mark 7:13:** "Making the word of God of none effect through your tradition, which ye have delivered: and many such like things do ye."
