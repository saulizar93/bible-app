## The claim

The Roman Catholic Church teaches four dogmas about Mary that every Catholic must believe: that she is the **Mother of God** (Ephesus, 431), that she remained a **virgin forever**, before, during and after the birth of Jesus (Lateran Synod, 649), that she was conceived **without original sin** (Pius IX, 1854), and that she was **assumed body and soul into heaven** (Pius XII, 1950). It also calls her "Queen over all things" (_Catechism_ 966) and teaches that "taken up to heaven she did not lay aside this saving office but by her manifold intercession continues to bring us the gifts of eternal salvation" (969).

This page compares that Mary with the Mary of the Bible. In short:

- The Mary of Scripture was a **humble, faithful believer**, greatly favored by God, who called God her **Saviour**, brought the offering the Law required, had other children with Joseph, and was last seen **praying with the church**, not being prayed to.
- Every "Marian" proof text, the "full of grace," the Ark of the Covenant, the queen mother, the wedding at Cana, says something else when it is read in context.
- The Marian dogmas appear **centuries after the apostles**, grow step by step, and rest on apocryphal books, forgeries, speculation and politics. Some were rejected by Rome's own greatest teachers.

Mary herself pointed away from herself:

> **John 2:5:** "His mother saith unto the servants, Whatsoever he saith unto you, do it."

# 1. "Highly favoured," like every believer

The angel greeted Mary with the Greek word **κεχαριτωμένη** (_kecharitōmenē_), "highly favoured" or "graced." The Latin Vulgate rendered it _gratia plena_, "full of grace," and on that rendering Rome builds the idea that Mary was filled with grace from her conception and never sinned.

But the word is a form of the verb **χαριτόω** (_charitoō_), "to grace, to show favour," and it is used only one other time in the New Testament, of **every believer**:

> **Luke 1:28:** "And the angel came in unto her, and said, Hail, thou that art highly favoured, the Lord is with thee: blessed art thou among women."

> **Ephesians 1:6:** "To the praise of the glory of his grace, wherein he hath made us accepted in the beloved."

Paul says God "hath made us accepted" (ἐχαρίτωσεν, _echaritōsen_) in the Beloved: the same grace, given to the whole church. And the exact same participle, κεχαριτωμένος, appears in the Greek of Sirach, a book Catholics themselves accept as Scripture:

> **Sirach 18:17:** "Lo, is not a word better than a gift? but both are with a **gracious** man." (KJV 1611 Apocrypha; Greek: ἀνδρὶ **κεχαριτωμένῳ**)

Nobody concludes that this "graced man" was conceived without sin or never sinned. The word means that God has shown favour; it says nothing about sinlessness. Mary was favoured because God chose her for an immense privilege, not because she was different in nature from other believers. When the angel explains the greeting, he says: "thou hast found **favour** with God" (Luke 1:30), the same thing said of Noah (Genesis 6:8).

# 2. Mary needed a Saviour

Scripture says "all have sinned" and makes no exception. Mary herself rejoiced in God "my **Saviour**." A sinless person does not need a Saviour.

> **Romans 3:23:** "For all have sinned, and come short of the glory of God;"

> **Romans 5:12:** "Wherefore, as by one man sin entered into the world, and death by sin; and so death passed upon all men, for that all have sinned:"

> **Luke 1:46-48:** "And Mary said, My soul doth magnify the Lord, And my spirit hath rejoiced in God my Saviour. For he hath regarded the low estate of his handmaiden: for, behold, from henceforth all generations shall call me blessed."

Catholic theology answers that God saved Mary in advance, by preserving her from sin. But that idea is nowhere in Scripture, and it was rejected by Rome's own greatest theologian. **Thomas Aquinas** taught that Mary did contract original sin and was cleansed of it before birth, because "if the soul of the Blessed Virgin had never incurred the stain of original sin, this would be derogatory to the dignity of Christ, by reason of His being the universal Saviour of all" (_Summa Theologiae_ III, q. 27, a. 2, ad 2). **Bernard of Clairvaux**, the most Marian writer of the Middle Ages, wrote to the canons of Lyon to protest against the new feast of the Immaculate Conception (Letter 174). The doctrine was finally defined in 1854.

Some of the early Fathers even spoke openly of Mary's faults. **Origen** said that at the cross Mary too was shaken in faith: "If she did not suffer scandal at the Lord's passion, then Jesus did not die for her sins" (_Homilies on Luke_ 17). **Basil** read Simeon's "sword" (Luke 2:35) as the doubt that pierced her at the cross (Letter 260). **John Chrysostom** said that when Mary came to call Jesus away (Matthew 12:46), she acted out of vanity, wishing to show the people that she had authority over her Son (_Homilies on Matthew_ 44). These Fathers were not trying to dishonor her; they simply did not yet believe in a sinless Mary.

## Mary brought a sin offering

Forty days after the birth, Mary went to the temple "according to the law of Moses." That law required a mother to bring a lamb for a burnt offering and a pigeon or turtledove **for a sin offering**; a poor mother could bring two birds, "the one for the burnt offering, and the other for a sin offering." Mary brought the two birds.

> **Luke 2:22-24:** "And when the days of her purification according to the law of Moses were accomplished, they brought him to Jerusalem, to present him to the Lord; (As it is written in the law of the Lord, Every male that openeth the womb shall be called holy to the Lord;) And to offer a sacrifice according to that which is said in the law of the Lord, A pair of turtledoves, or two young pigeons."

> **Leviticus 12:6-8:** "And when the days of her purifying are fulfilled, for a son, or for a daughter, she shall bring a lamb of the first year for a burnt offering, and a young pigeon, or a turtledove, for a sin offering, unto the door of the tabernacle of the congregation, unto the priest: Who shall offer it before the LORD, and make an atonement for her; and she shall be cleansed from the issue of her blood. This is the law for her that hath born a male or a female. And if she be not able to bring a lamb, then she shall bring two turtles, or two young pigeons; the one for the burnt offering, and the other for a sin offering: and the priest shall make an atonement for her, and she shall be clean."

Catholics answer that this was only ritual purification. It is true that the law dealt with the impurity of childbirth. But the offering the law prescribes is called a **sin offering** (_chattath_), and Mary brought it without claiming any exemption. Luke calls it "**their** purification," as he would of any Jewish family. Nothing in the account suggests she saw herself as different from other mothers.

# 3. Mary and Joseph lived as husband and wife

Matthew says Joseph "knew her not **till** she had brought forth her firstborn son." He also says the conception happened "before they came together." Both phrases look forward to a normal married life afterwards. If Mary and Joseph never came together, Matthew chose the most misleading words possible.

> **Matthew 1:18:** "Now the birth of Jesus Christ was on this wise: When as his mother Mary was espoused to Joseph, before they came together, she was found with child of the Holy Ghost."

> **Matthew 1:24-25:** "Then Joseph being raised from sleep did as the angel of the Lord had bidden him, and took unto him his wife: And knew her not till she had brought forth her firstborn son: and he called his name JESUS."

The Gospels name Jesus' brothers and mention His sisters, and always place them with His mother:

> **Matthew 13:55-56:** "Is not this the carpenter’s son? is not his mother called Mary? and his brethren, James, and Joses, and Simon, and Judas? And his sisters, are they not all with us? Whence then hath this man all these things?"

> **Mark 3:31-32:** "There came then his brethren and his mother, and, standing without, sent unto him, calling him. And the multitude sat about him, and they said unto him, Behold, thy mother and thy brethren without seek for thee."

> **John 7:3-5:** "His brethren therefore said unto him, Depart hence, and go into Judæa, that thy disciples also may see the works that thou doest. For there is no man that doeth any thing in secret, and he himself seeketh to be known openly. If thou do these things, shew thyself to the world. For neither did his brethren believe in him."

> **Galatians 1:19:** "But other of the apostles saw I none, save James the Lord’s brother."

Even a messianic Psalm, quoted of Jesus in John 2:17, says He would be "an alien unto **my mother's children**":

> **Psalm 69:8-9:** "I am become a stranger unto my brethren, and an alien unto my mother’s children. For the zeal of thine house hath eaten me up; and the reproaches of them that reproached thee are fallen upon me."

## They were not Joseph's sons from an earlier marriage

The first explanation for the brothers, from the apocryphal _Protoevangelium of James_ (about 150), is that they were sons of Joseph by an earlier wife. But Jesus' **legal right to David's throne** came through Joseph, the "son of David" (Matthew 1:16, 20). Under the Law, the birthright belonged to the **firstborn** son (Deuteronomy 21:15-17), and Luke calls Jesus Mary's "firstborn." If Joseph already had older sons, they, not Jesus, would have been the firstborn heirs of Joseph's royal line, and Jesus' claim through Joseph would collapse.

> **Luke 1:32-33:** "He shall be great, and shall be called the Son of the Highest: and the Lord God shall give unto him the throne of his father David: And he shall reign over the house of Jacob for ever; and of his kingdom there shall be no end."

> **Luke 2:7:** "And she brought forth her firstborn son, and wrapped him in swaddling clothes, and laid him in a manger; because there was no room for them in the inn."

## They were not cousins

**Jerome** (383) proposed instead that the "brothers" were cousins. But Greek has a word for cousin, ἀνεψιός (_anepsios_), and the New Testament uses it (Colossians 4:10). It never uses it of Jesus' brothers. **Hegesippus**, a second-century Jewish Christian, kept the two words apart: he calls James "**the brother of the Lord**," but calls Symeon, son of Clopas, "**a cousin** of the Lord" (Eusebius, _Church History_ 2.23; 4.22). **Tertullian** (about 207) simply took the brothers as Mary's sons (_On the Flesh of Christ_ 7). In the Gospels, they come with Mary, live with Mary, and are named with Mary as "his mother, and his brethren."

## No vow of virginity

Augustine argued that Mary's question, "How shall this be, seeing I know not a man?" (Luke 1:34), showed she had vowed perpetual virginity. But she was **betrothed**, about to be married. First-century Jews regarded marriage and children as a duty from Genesis 1:28 ("A man shall not abstain from procreation unless he already has children," Mishnah _Yevamot_ 6:6). Some Essene men lived celibate, but they did not marry. There is no example in Jewish life of a woman who vowed virginity and then got married; under the Law, a husband could cancel such a vow on the day he heard it (Numbers 30:6-8). Mary's question was about the present: she was still a virgin, and the angel spoke of a conception about to happen.

## A sexless marriage would have disobeyed God's word

Paul teaches that husband and wife must not "defraud" one another, except "with consent for a time," and then come together again "that Satan tempt you not." A lifelong refusal is exactly what he forbids. If the Catholic claim were true, Mary and Joseph would have lived their whole marriage in disobedience to the teaching of Scripture. The Bible does not say that; it says Joseph "knew her not **till**."

> **1 Corinthians 7:3-5:** "Let the husband render unto the wife due benevolence: and likewise also the wife unto the husband. The wife hath not power of her own body, but the husband: and likewise also the husband hath not power of his own body, but the wife. Defraud ye not one the other, except it be with consent for a time, that ye may give yourselves to fasting and prayer; and come together again, that Satan tempt you not for your incontinency."

# 4. Nobody in the Bible prayed to Mary

Mary appears in the Gospels and once in Acts. She is never prayed to, never asked to intercede, never venerated, and never mentioned by name in a single epistle. Paul refers to her once, as "a woman" (Galatians 4:4). Her last appearance is in Acts 1:14, where she is **praying with** the disciples, one believer among about 120, waiting like everyone else to be filled with the Holy Spirit:

> **Acts 1:14:** "These all continued with one accord in prayer and supplication, with the women, and Mary the mother of Jesus, and with his brethren."

> **Acts 2:1-4:** "And when the day of Pentecost was fully come, they were all with one accord in one place. And suddenly there came a sound from heaven as of a rushing mighty wind, and it filled all the house where they were sitting. And there appeared unto them cloven tongues like as of fire, and it sat upon each of them. And they were all filled with the Holy Ghost, and began to speak with other tongues, as the Spirit gave them utterance."

If Mary is the Mediatrix of all graces, it is strange that she had to wait in the upper room to receive the Holy Ghost, just like Peter and the others.

## When a woman venerated Mary, Jesus corrected her

The only person in the Gospels who praised Mary for being Jesus' mother was a woman in the crowd. Jesus did not accept the praise; He redirected it at once:

> **Luke 11:27-28:** "And it came to pass, as he spake these things, a certain woman of the company lifted up her voice, and said unto him, Blessed is the womb that bare thee, and the paps which thou hast sucked. But he said, Yea rather, blessed are they that hear the word of God, and keep it."

## Jesus put His spiritual family first

When His mother and brothers came to take Him home, believing He was "beside himself," Jesus did not leave the crowd to go to them. He pointed to His disciples:

> **Mark 3:21:** "And when his friends heard of it, they went out to lay hold on him: for they said, He is beside himself."

> **Mark 3:31-35:** "There came then his brethren and his mother, and, standing without, sent unto him, calling him. And the multitude sat about him, and they said unto him, Behold, thy mother and thy brethren without seek for thee. And he answered them, saying, Who is my mother, or my brethren? And he looked round about on them which sat about him, and said, Behold my mother and my brethren! For whosoever shall do the will of God, the same is my brother, and my sister, and mother."

At Cana and again in the temple, Jesus made clear that His mother did not direct His mission: "Wist ye not that I must be about my Father's business?" (Luke 2:49), "Woman, what have I to do with thee? mine hour is not yet come" (John 2:4). On the cross He gave her into the care of John, a believing disciple, because at that time His brothers did not believe in Him (John 7:5; 19:26-27).

## Cana is not heavenly intercession

Catholics point to the wedding at Cana as proof that Mary intercedes: she told Jesus "They have no wine," and He acted. But this was a living woman, at a real wedding, speaking to her Son in the same room about a practical need. That is what any believer does when he asks a brother to pray. It says nothing about a woman in heaven hearing millions of prayers. And Mary's own instruction to the servants was not "come to me," but "**Whatsoever he saith unto you, do it.**"

> **John 2:3-5:** "And when they wanted wine, the mother of Jesus saith unto him, They have no wine. Jesus saith unto her, Woman, what have I to do with thee? mine hour is not yet come. His mother saith unto the servants, Whatsoever he saith unto you, do it."

# 5. God has no mother

Ephesus called Mary _Theotokos_, "God-bearer," and the West rendered it "Mother of God." The council's concern was right: the child born of Mary was not a mere man but **God manifest in the flesh**. But "Mother of God," taken as it sounds, says that God had a beginning from a woman. God is an eternal **Spirit**, without beginning; He did not come from Mary. What Mary conceived and bore was the **Son**, the holy human child "called the Son of God," in whom the fullness of the Godhead dwelt bodily:

> **John 4:24:** "God is a Spirit: and they that worship him must worship him in spirit and in truth."

> **Luke 1:35:** "And the angel answered and said unto her, The Holy Ghost shall come upon thee, and the power of the Highest shall overshadow thee: therefore also that holy thing which shall be born of thee shall be called the Son of God."

> **Colossians 2:9-10:** "For in him dwelleth all the fulness of the Godhead bodily. And ye are complete in him, which is the head of all principality and power:"

> **2 Corinthians 5:19:** "To wit, that God was in Christ, reconciling the world unto himself, not imputing their trespasses unto them; and hath committed unto us the word of reconciliation."

> **1 Timothy 3:16:** "And without controversy great is the mystery of godliness: God was manifest in the flesh, justified in the Spirit, seen of angels, preached unto the Gentiles, believed on in the world, received up into glory."

Mary is the mother of the **Son**, the humanity in which God dwelt and through which He reconciled the world to Himself. She is not the mother of the eternal Spirit, who existed before her and who created her. As the Fathers liked to say: as man, Jesus had no father; as God, He had no mother. Melchizedek, the figure of Christ's priesthood, is described as "without father, without mother" (Hebrews 7:3).

# 6. Mary is not the Ark of the Covenant

Catholic apologists say Mary is the "new Ark of the Covenant," because she carried Jesus as the ark carried the tablets of the Law, the manna and Aaron's rod. They point to parallels between David bringing up the ark (2 Samuel 6) and Mary visiting Elizabeth (Luke 1:39-56): David asked, "How shall the ark of the LORD come to me?"; Elizabeth asked, "Whence is this to me, that the mother of my Lord should come to me?"; the ark stayed three months in the house of Obed-edom, and Mary stayed about three months with Elizabeth.

## In the New Testament, the Ark is Christ

Everything in and on the ark points to **Jesus**:

- The ark held the **tablets of the Law**; Jesus is the **Word** made flesh.
- It held the **manna**; Jesus is the **bread of life**.
- It held **Aaron's rod** that budded; Jesus is our **High Priest**, raised from the dead.
- Its lid was the **mercy seat** (ἱλαστήριον, _hilastērion_), where the blood was sprinkled; Paul uses the very same word for **Christ**, "whom God hath set forth to be a **propitiation**" (ἱλαστήριον).
- Above it dwelt the **glory of God**; Jesus is the true **temple**, "the temple of his body," in whom "dwelleth all the fulness of the Godhead bodily."

> **Hebrews 9:4-5:** "Which had the golden censer, and the ark of the covenant overlaid round about with gold, wherein was the golden pot that had manna, and Aaron’s rod that budded, and the tables of the covenant; And over it the cherubims of glory shadowing the mercyseat; of which we can not now speak particularly."

> **Romans 3:25:** "Whom God hath set forth to be a propitiation through faith in his blood, to declare his righteousness for the remission of sins that are past, through the forbearance of God;"

> **John 1:14:** "And the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us, (and we beheld his glory, the glory as of the only begotten of the Father,) full of grace and truth."

> **John 6:35:** "And Jesus said unto them, I am the bread of life: he that cometh to me shall never hunger; and he that believeth on me shall never thirst."

> **Hebrews 4:14:** "Seeing then that we have a great high priest, that is passed into the heavens, Jesus the Son of God, let us hold fast our profession."

> **John 2:19-21:** "Jesus answered and said unto them, Destroy this temple, and in three days I will raise it up. Then said the Jews, Forty and six years was this temple in building, and wilt thou rear it up in three days? But he spake of the temple of his body."

Mary carried Jesus for nine months. Jesus carries the presence of God forever. God even said the day would come when the ark would no longer be remembered:

> **Jeremiah 3:16:** "And it shall come to pass, when ye be multiplied and increased in the land, in those days, saith the LORD, they shall say no more, The ark of the covenant of the LORD: neither shall it come to mind: neither shall they remember it; neither shall they visit it; neither shall that be done any more."

## The verse they quote is about the ark killing a man

Read 2 Samuel 6 in full, and David's question looks very different. David asked "How shall the ark of the LORD come to me?" **because the ark had just killed Uzzah**, who put out his hand to steady it. David was not rejoicing; he was **afraid**. The ark went to the house of Obed-edom because David did not dare take it home.

> **2 Samuel 6:6-10:** "And when they came to Nachon’s threshingfloor, Uzzah put forth his hand to the ark of God, and took hold of it; for the oxen shook it. And the anger of the LORD was kindled against Uzzah; and God smote him there for his error; and there he died by the ark of God. And David was displeased, because the LORD had made a breach upon Uzzah: and he called the name of the place Perez-uzzah to this day. And David was afraid of the LORD that day, and said, How shall the ark of the LORD come to me? So David would not remove the ark of the LORD unto him into the city of David: but David carried it aside into the house of Obed-edom the Gittite."

If Luke really meant to echo this passage, the point would be that Elizabeth was terrified that Mary might strike her dead. Nobody believes that. The verbal parallels are loose (a woman greeting her cousin naturally says "Why has this happened to me?") and Luke never calls Mary an ark.

## Revelation 12 is not Mary

The other text is Revelation 11:19-12:1, where the ark is seen in heaven and then "a woman clothed with the sun." The chapter break is a later addition, but even read together, the ark in heaven is the heavenly original of the earthly one (Hebrews 8:5; 9:23-24), not a woman. The woman of Revelation 12 wears the sun, the moon and **twelve stars**, the picture of **Israel** in Joseph's dream (Genesis 37:9). She flees into the wilderness for 1,260 days and has "the remnant of her seed" persecuted. And she cries out "**travailing in birth, and pained** to be delivered" (12:2), while Catholic tradition teaches that Mary gave birth **without pain** (the _Catechism of the Council of Trent_ says she was exempt from the curse of Eve, "in sorrow thou shalt bring forth children").

# 7. The "queen mother" got a man killed

Catholics argue that in the kingdom of David the king's mother, the _gebirah_, sat at the king's right hand and interceded for the people, and that Mary, mother of the Son of David, now does the same. The proof text is Bathsheba before Solomon:

> **1 Kings 2:19-20:** "Bath-sheba therefore went unto king Solomon, to speak unto him for Adonijah. And the king rose up to meet her, and bowed himself unto her, and sat down on his throne, and caused a seat to be set for the king’s mother; and she sat on his right hand. Then she said, I desire one small petition of thee; I pray thee, say me not nay. And the king said unto her, Ask on, my mother: for I will not say thee nay."

Read the next verses. Adonijah, Solomon's rival for the throne, had asked Bathsheba to request Abishag for him. Solomon refused her outright, saw the request as a plot to take the kingdom, and **had Adonijah executed the same day**. If this passage teaches anything about going through the queen mother, it teaches that it can get you killed.

> **1 Kings 2:21-25:** "And she said, Let Abishag the Shunammite be given to Adonijah thy brother to wife. And king Solomon answered and said unto his mother, And why dost thou ask Abishag the Shunammite for Adonijah? ask for him the kingdom also; for he is mine elder brother; even for him, and for Abiathar the priest, and for Joab the son of Zeruiah. Then king Solomon sware by the LORD, saying, God do so to me, and more also, if Adonijah have not spoken this word against his own life. Now therefore, as the LORD liveth, which hath established me, and set me on the throne of David my father, and who hath made me an house, as he promised, Adonijah shall be put to death this day. And king Solomon sent by the hand of Benaiah the son of Jehoiada; and he fell upon him that he died."

The other queen mothers of the Bible are no better models. **Maachah** was removed from being queen mother because she made an idol, an _asherah_, the image of a mother goddess (1 Kings 15:13). **Jezebel** was the queen mother of Israel (2 Kings 10:13). And the only "**queen of heaven**" in Scripture is a goddess whom the women of Judah honored with cakes and incense, and whom God condemned:

> **1 Kings 15:13:** "And also Maachah his mother, even her he removed from being queen, because she had made an idol in a grove; and Asa destroyed her idol, and burnt it by the brook Kidron."

> **Jeremiah 7:18:** "The children gather wood, and the fathers kindle the fire, and the women knead their dough, to make cakes to the queen of heaven, and to pour out drink offerings unto other gods, that they may provoke me to anger."

> **Jeremiah 44:17-19:** "But we will certainly do whatsoever thing goeth forth out of our own mouth, to burn incense unto the queen of heaven, and to pour out drink offerings unto her, as we have done, we, and our fathers, our kings, and our princes, in the cities of Judah, and in the streets of Jerusalem: for then had we plenty of victuals, and were well, and saw no evil. But since we left off to burn incense to the queen of heaven, and to pour out drink offerings unto her, we have wanted all things, and have been consumed by the sword and by the famine. And when we burned incense to the queen of heaven, and poured out drink offerings unto her, did we make her cakes to worship her, and pour out drink offerings unto her, without our men?"

# 8. How the devotion began

## The first century: nothing

The New Testament, the _Didache_, Clement of Rome, Ignatius, Polycarp and the _Epistle of Barnabas_ contain no prayer to Mary, no veneration of Mary, and no mention of her perpetual virginity, immaculate conception or assumption. In the second century, Justin (_Dialogue_ 100) and Irenaeus (_Against Heresies_ 3.22; 5.19) compare Mary's obedience with Eve's disobedience, a fair illustration, but still no prayers to her.

## The second century: an apocryphal novel

Almost every later Marian belief first appears in the **Protoevangelium of James** (about 150), an anonymous book falsely claiming James as its author: Mary's birth to Joachim and Anna, her childhood in the temple, Joseph as an old widower with sons, and a midwife who examines Mary after the birth and finds her still a virgin. The church never accepted it as Scripture, and the _Gelasian Decree_ listed it among the rejected apocrypha.

## The fourth century: the first Marian worshippers were called heretics

Around 375, **Epiphanius of Salamis** described a group of women in Arabia, the **Collyridians**, who offered cakes to Mary as to a goddess. He condemned them in words that could be written over every Marian shrine today: "Let Mary be held in honour, but let the Father, the Son and the Holy Spirit be worshipped; let no one worship Mary" (_Panarion_ 79.7). Two hundred years later, Muhammad grew up in Arabia believing that Christians worshipped Mary as a god:

> "O Jesus, son of Mary! Didst thou say unto mankind: Take me and my mother for two gods beside Allah?" (Qur'an 5:116)

Muhammad was wrong about the Trinity. But he was describing what he saw Christians doing.

## Ephesus, 431: politics, gold and the city of Artemis

The title _Theotokos_ was made dogma in one of the most disgraceful councils in church history. The quarrel was between two schools: **Antioch**, which stressed the full humanity of Christ and was represented by **Nestorius**, bishop of Constantinople, and **Alexandria**, which stressed His divinity and was led by **Cyril**. Nestorius objected to _Theotokos_ and proposed _Christotokos_, "Christ-bearer."

- Cyril opened the council on 22 June 431 **without waiting** for John of Antioch and the eastern bishops, over the protest of 68 bishops and of the emperor's own representative, the count Candidian. He deposed Nestorius the same day, before his supporters had arrived.
- When John of Antioch arrived four days later, he held his own council and **deposed Cyril**. Each side excommunicated the other.
- Emperor **Theodosius II** at first confirmed **both** depositions and put Cyril and Nestorius under arrest.
- Cyril then won the court. A letter from his own church (Cyril, _Letter_ 96) lists the "blessings" sent to the officials of the palace in Constantinople: rugs, curtains, tablecloths, ivory chairs and stools, and **over a thousand pounds of gold**, to the prefects, the chamberlains and the quaestor, and to the chambermaids of the empress **Pulcheria**, the emperor's sister, so that they would "persuade" her. Pulcheria, a vowed virgin devoted to Mary, was Nestorius' enemy; a Syriac source says he had barred her from the sanctuary and questioned her virginity.
- In the end the **emperor** decided. Nestorius was sent back to his monastery and later exiled to the Egyptian desert, and Cyril's term became dogma.

The council met in **Ephesus**, the city of the "great goddess Diana" (Artemis), whose image "fell down from Jupiter" and whom "all Asia and the world worshippeth" (Acts 19:27, 35). When the people heard that Nestorius was condemned, they escorted the bishops with torches and incense. The modern "House of Mary" outside Ephesus was identified in 1891 from the visions of a German nun, Anne Catherine Emmerich.

## Veneration that looks exactly like worship

Rome says it does not worship Mary; it gives her _hyperdulia_, a "higher veneration," above the _dulia_ given to the saints but below the _latria_ given to God (Aquinas, _Summa_ II-II, q. 103, a. 4). But **δουλεία** (_douleia_) means **the service of a slave**: a δοῦλος is a slave. "Hyperdulia" literally means "super-slavery." And Scripture uses the verb **δουλεύω** for the service owed to God, and for the service the pagans gave their idols:

> **Matthew 6:24:** "No man can serve two masters: for either he will hate the one, and love the other; or else he will hold to the one, and despise the other. Ye cannot serve God and mammon."

> **Galatians 4:8-9:** "Howbeit then, when ye knew not God, ye did service unto them which by nature are no gods. But now, after that ye have known God, or rather are known of God, how turn ye again to the weak and beggarly elements, whereunto ye desire again to be in bondage?"

> **1 Thessalonians 1:9:** "For they themselves shew of us what manner of entering in we had unto you, and how ye turned to God from idols to serve the living and true God;"

Louis de Montfort, whose _True Devotion to Mary_ inspired John Paul II's motto _Totus tuus_ ("entirely yours"), teaches believers to consecrate themselves to Mary as her "slaves of love." Catholics pray to Mary, kneel before her images, burn candles and incense to them, crown them, carry them in procession, make vows to them, and call her "our life, our sweetness and our hope" (_Salve Regina_). No outside observer can tell the difference between that and worship, and Muhammad did not.

## "No man goeth to Christ but by His Mother"

Jesus said: "No man cometh unto the Father, but by me." In 1891 **Pope Leo XIII** wrote: "Thus as no man goeth to the Father but by the Son, so no man goeth to Christ but by His Mother," and called Mary "the intermediary through whom is distributed unto us this immense treasure of mercies" (_Octobri Mense_ 4). Jesus said "**Come unto me**." Rome says go through Mary.

> **John 14:6:** "Jesus saith unto him, I am the way, the truth, and the life: no man cometh unto the Father, but by me."

> **Matthew 11:28:** "Come unto me, all ye that labour and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest."

> **1 Timothy 2:5:** "For there is one God, and one mediator between God and men, the man Christ Jesus;"

> **Hebrews 4:16:** "Let us therefore come boldly unto the throne of grace, that we may obtain mercy, and find grace to help in time of need."

Even the Vatican now admits that some of these titles went too far. In November 2025 the Dicastery for the Doctrine of the Faith declared it "always inappropriate" to call Mary "Co-redemptrix," and warned that "Mediatrix of All Graces" has "limits that do not favour a correct understanding" (_Mater Populi Fidelis_). But popes had been using those titles for more than a century.

# 9. Every nation makes its own Mary

Men have always wanted a god they can see, in their own image (Romans 1:22-23; Acts 17:29). So every nation has its own Mary, with its own face, dress and miracles: the dark-skinned **Guadalupe** of Mexico; the Spanish **Guadalupe** of Extremadura, a black statue, from which the Mexican one took its name; the **Virgen del Pilar** of Spain; the Black Madonna of **Częstochowa** in Poland; **Lourdes** in France; **Fátima** in Portugal; **Aparecida**, a dark statue, in Brazil; **La Negrita** of Costa Rica; **Caridad del Cobre** in Cuba; **Luján** in Argentina; **La Vang** in Vietnam; **Akita** in Japan; **Kibeho** in Rwanda. Each is "Our Lady," each is the patroness of her nation, and each has her own shrine, feast and promises.

> **Exodus 20:4-5:** "Thou shalt not make unto thee any graven image, or any likeness of any thing that is in heaven above, or that is in the earth beneath, or that is in the water under the earth: Thou shalt not bow down thyself to them, nor serve them: for I the LORD thy God am a jealous God, visiting the iniquity of the fathers upon the children unto the third and fourth generation of them that hate me;"

> **Isaiah 44:15-17:** "Then shall it be for a man to burn: for he will take thereof, and warm himself; yea, he kindleth it, and baketh bread; yea, he maketh a god, and worshippeth it; he maketh it a graven image, and falleth down thereto. He burneth part thereof in the fire; with part thereof he eateth flesh; he roasteth roast, and is satisfied: yea, he warmeth himself, and saith, Aha, I am warm, I have seen the fire: And the residue thereof he maketh a god, even his graven image: he falleth down unto it, and worshippeth it, and prayeth unto it, and saith, Deliver me; for thou art my god."

Scripture has one Mary, a Jewish woman of Nazareth. These are not her.

# 10. Our Lady of Guadalupe

According to the official story, the Virgin appeared four times in December 1531 on the hill of Tepeyac, near Mexico City, to an Indian named **Juan Diego**, and sent him to Bishop **Juan de Zumárraga** to ask for a church. As a sign, she filled his cloak (_tilma_) with roses, and when he opened it before the bishop, her image was printed on it.

The historian and Catholic priest **Stafford Poole**, in _Our Lady of Guadalupe: The Origins and Sources of a Mexican National Symbol, 1531-1797_ (1995), examined every source and showed that this story cannot be traced to 1531.

## The story first appears 117 years later

The first published account of the apparitions is **Miguel Sánchez**, _Imagen de la Virgen María Madre de Dios de Guadalupe_, printed in **1648**. The Nahuatl version, the _Huei tlamahuiçoltica_ with the narrative called _Nican mopohua_, was published by **Luis Laso de la Vega** in 1649. Poole argues that Laso de la Vega and his assistants composed it, not the sixteenth-century Indian scholar Antonio Valeriano to whom it was later attributed.

## The people who should have known say nothing

- **Zumárraga**, the bishop who supposedly received the miraculous cloak, wrote letters, books and reports until his death in 1548. He **never mentions** the apparition, Juan Diego, the roses, or the image. In 1547 he even wrote that the age of miracles was over and was no longer needed.
- **Juan Diego** does not appear in any document before 1648.
- No sixteenth-century chronicler mentions the apparition: not Motolinía, not Mendieta, not Bernal Díaz, not the Franciscan **Bernardino de Sahagún**, the great student of Indian culture. Sahagún did mention the shrine at Tepeyac, and he was suspicious of it: the place had been dedicated to the Aztec mother-goddess **Tonantzin**, "our mother," and the Indians still came there calling the Virgin by that name, which he thought a cover for idolatry (_General History_, book 11).
- In **1556**, the archbishop **Alonso de Montúfar** promoted the shrine. The Franciscan provincial **Francisco de Bustamante** preached against it publicly, saying the devotion had no foundation and that the image had been **painted by an Indian**, the painter **Marcos**. In the official inquiry that followed, no one, on either side, said a word about an apparition or a miraculous origin.

## A criollo symbol

Poole shows that the devotion was promoted not by the Indians but by the **criollos**, Spaniards born in Mexico, who were treated as inferior by the **peninsulares** born in Spain. Sánchez's book is a patriotic work: it presents the Mexican Virgin as the woman of Revelation 12 and Mexico as a chosen nation. The Spaniards had brought their Marys with them: **Hernán Cortés**, from Extremadura, was a devotee of the Guadalupe of Extremadura, and he and his men put images of the Virgin in the Aztec temples in the very places where the idols had stood. The conquistadors' Virgin of **Los Remedios** became the Virgin of the peninsulares. The criollos needed a Virgin of their own, born in Mexico like them.

## Two Marys at war

When the War of Independence began on 16 September 1810, the priest **Miguel Hidalgo** took a banner of the **Virgin of Guadalupe** from the sanctuary of Atotonilco, and his army marched under it, shouting "Long live the Virgin of Guadalupe, death to bad government." In response, the Spanish viceroy had the **Virgin of Los Remedios** brought to the cathedral, **dressed as a general**, and proclaimed her **"Generala"** of the royal army. Mexicans died under the banner of one Mary, killing Spaniards fighting under the banner of another. Which one was the real Mary? The real Mary of Nazareth was in neither.

## The image and the cloth

- The cloth is about **1.7 metres** tall and just over a metre wide, made of two pieces sewn together. A _tilma_ was a cloak tied at the shoulder; on an Indian man of the sixteenth century, typically about 1.6 metres tall, a cloak of that length would have dragged on the ground.
- The image has been **retouched**. Infrared photographs taken in 1979 by the biologist **Philip Callahan**, who believed the face was inexplicable, showed that the **moon, the angel, the gold rays, the stars and the border** were painted on later by human hands, and that this added paint had cracked and flaked. The **crown** visible in older copies has disappeared; it was removed at the end of the nineteenth century.
- In 1996 the abbot of the Basilica of Guadalupe himself, **Guillermo Schulenburg**, who had been in charge of the shrine for over thirty years, said in an interview that Juan Diego was "a symbol, not a reality," and that canonizing him would be "recognition of a cult," not of "the physical, real existence of a person." He was forced to resign. Juan Diego was canonized anyway in 2002.

## How she took God's place

In **1629** Mexico City was flooded, and stayed under water for about five years. The image was carried from Tepeyac to the cathedral, and when the waters finally went down in 1634, the city credited Guadalupe. In **1736-1737** an epidemic (_matlazahuatl_) killed tens of thousands in the city. The city turned to one image after another, including the Virgin of Los Remedios. In 1737 the city council and the church swore the Virgin of Guadalupe as **patroness of Mexico City**, and when the epidemic declined, she received the credit; in 1746 she was made patroness of all New Spain. Scripture says:

> **Psalm 46:1:** "God is our refuge and strength, a very present help in trouble."

> **Psalm 121:1-2:** "I will lift up mine eyes unto the hills, from whence cometh my help. My help cometh from the LORD, which made heaven and earth."

> **Isaiah 42:8:** "I am the LORD: that is my name: and my glory will I not give to another, neither my praise to graven images."

The guardian, refuge and protector of a nation is God. Guadalupe was given His place.

# 11. Dogmas without tradition

Rome claims its dogmas are part of the apostolic tradition, handed down from the beginning. The Marian dogmas show the opposite.

## Nobody knew how Mary's life ended

As **Gavin Ortlund** shows in _What It Means to Be Protestant_ (2024), the early church did not know what happened to Mary. Around 377, **Epiphanius of Salamis**, who was born in Palestine and wrote a long treatise on Mary to settle the disputes about her, wrote that Scripture does not say whether Mary died or did not die, whether she was buried or not, and concluded: "**No one knows her end**" (_Panarion_ 78.11; 78.23). He listed the possibilities, that she died naturally, that she was martyred, or that she remained alive, and refused to choose. If the apostles had taught that Mary was taken bodily to heaven, a bishop of Palestine looking into the question in the fourth century would have known it.

## The assumption stories contradict each other

The first assumption stories, the _Transitus Mariae_ ("Passing of Mary") texts, appear only at the end of the fifth century, in many conflicting versions: Mary died in Jerusalem, or in Ephesus; her body was taken to paradise, or raised and taken to heaven, or left under the tree of life; the apostles were carried to her on clouds, or not. The _Gelasian Decree_, attributed to a pope, listed the book "called the Passing, that is the Assumption of Saint Mary" among the **apocrypha to be rejected**. The feast was established in the East around 600 by the emperor Maurice, and the story slowly became stable over the next centuries. It was made dogma only in **1950**, by Pius XII, who rested it chiefly on "the universal agreement of the Church's ordinary teaching authority" in his own day, not on any testimony from the apostles.

## The Fathers name the people taken to heaven, but not Mary

The early writers often speak of the people God took up bodily: **Enoch** and **Elijah** (Irenaeus, _Against Heresies_ 5.5.1; Tertullian, _On the Soul_ 50; Hippolytus). They never add Mary. A dogma that every Catholic must believe under pain of losing the faith has no basis in Scripture and none in the first four centuries of tradition.

# 12. Why the Fathers believed in perpetual virginity

Catholics answer that many Fathers held Mary's perpetual virginity. They did, from the fourth century on. But **believing what other people believed is not evidence**. The question is _why_ they believed it, and when we look at their reasons, there is no evidence at all, only speculation:

- **Origen** (about 245) admits the source: "some say, basing it on a tradition in the Gospel according to Peter, as it is entitled, or the Book of James, that the brethren of Jesus were sons of Joseph by a former wife." Both are apocryphal books. And he gives the motive: those who say this "wish to preserve the honour of Mary in virginity to the end, so that that body of hers which was appointed to minister to the Word… might not know intercourse with a man after that the Holy Ghost came into her" (_Commentary on Matthew_ 10.17). The reasoning is that Joseph would not touch what the Holy Spirit had touched.
- **Jerome** (_Against Helvidius_, 383) argued that the brothers were cousins, and that even Joseph was a virgin, because virginity is holier than marriage. He offers no historical source.
- **Ambrose** and **Jerome** applied to Mary the gate of Ezekiel's temple: "This gate shall be shut, it shall not be opened, and no man shall enter in by it; because the LORD… hath entered in by it." In Ezekiel, that is the **east gate of the temple** through which the glory of God returned (43:1-4), shut so that no prince would walk where God had walked. It has nothing to do with Mary.
- Later tradition called Mary the "**spouse of the Holy Spirit**," so that a union with Joseph would seem like unfaithfulness to her divine husband. Scripture never calls her that; it calls her "Mary thy wife," Joseph's wife (Matthew 1:20).

> **Ezekiel 44:1-3:** "Then he brought me back the way of the gate of the outward sanctuary which looketh toward the east; and it was shut. Then said the LORD unto me; This gate shall be shut, it shall not be opened, and no man shall enter in by it; because the LORD, the God of Israel, hath entered in by it, therefore it shall be shut. It is for the prince; the prince, he shall sit in it to eat bread before the LORD; he shall enter by the way of the porch of that gate, and shall go out by the way of the same."

And the earlier witnesses held the opposite. **Tertullian** took the brothers as Mary's own sons. **Hegesippus** called James the Lord's brother and Symeon His cousin. In the fourth century, Helvidius, Jovinian and Bonosus still taught that Mary had other children. Even **Basil** admitted that the belief that Mary remained a virgin afterwards was not necessary to the faith, though Christians did not like to hear otherwise (_Homily on the Holy Generation of Christ_).

The real driving force was the **ascetic movement** of the fourth century, which exalted virginity above marriage. As Marina Warner shows in _Alone of All Her Sex: The Myth and the Cult of the Virgin Mary_ (1976), Mary was remade into the ideal of the celibate monk and nun. The book's title comes from the fifth-century poet Sedulius, who said that Mary "had no peer, before or since: alone of all her sex she pleased the Lord." Mary became a model no real woman could follow, a virgin mother. Scripture's Mary was a wife and the mother of a large family.

# 13. Forgeries and corrupted Fathers

Much of the support for the Marian cult came from documents written under false names:

- **The letters of Ignatius to the Virgin Mary and to John.** Medieval Latin forgeries in which Ignatius writes to Mary, Mary answers him, and Ignatius tells John that many women in Antioch want to come and see her. They were circulated as genuine works of the disciple of the apostles. Every scholar, Catholic included, now recognizes them as spurious; the _Ante-Nicene Fathers_ prints them among the "Spurious Epistles."
- **The _Euthymian History_.** A story inserted into a sermon by John of Damascus (8th century), claiming that at the Council of Chalcedon (451) the bishop Juvenal of Jerusalem told the emperor Marcian and the empress Pulcheria that Mary's tomb had been found empty. Nothing of the sort appears in the acts of Chalcedon. It was an interpolation.
- **Pseudo-Dionysius the Areopagite**, a sixth-century writer pretending to be Paul's convert in Athens (Acts 17:34), whose vague passage about gathering around a "life-giving body" was later read as an eyewitness account of Mary's death.
- **Pseudo-Melito**, a _Transitus_ falsely attributed to Melito of Sardis (2nd century).
- **Pseudo-Jerome**, the letter _Cogitis me_ on the Assumption, written by Paschasius Radbertus in the ninth century under Jerome's name, and sermons on the Assumption falsely attributed to Augustine.
- **Prayers to Mary attributed to Ephrem the Syrian**, most of which are later Greek compositions.

When a doctrine has to be supported by forged letters from the apostolic age, it is a sign that the genuine letters did not support it.

# 14. A devotion that keeps growing

The Marian cult did not come from the apostles fully formed. It grew, century by century:

| Date | Development |
|---|---|
| c. 150 | _Protoevangelium of James_: Mary's birth, childhood, and perpetual virginity invented |
| c. 375 | Epiphanius condemns the Collyridians for offering worship to Mary |
| 3rd-6th cent. | _Sub tuum praesidium_, the first known prayer to Mary (the papyrus is dated anywhere from the 3rd to the 6th century) |
| 431 | Ephesus: _Theotokos_ |
| late 5th cent. | First _Transitus_ (assumption) stories |
| 6th cent. | "Maria Regina": Mary painted as a crowned queen in Santa Maria Antiqua, Rome |
| 553 | Constantinople II calls Mary "ever-virgin" |
| c. 600 | Emperor Maurice fixes the feast of the Dormition on 15 August |
| 649 | Lateran Synod defines perpetual virginity |
| 11th cent. | _Salve Regina_: "Hail, holy Queen… our life, our sweetness and our hope" |
| 12th cent. | Bernard of Clairvaux calls Mary the "aqueduct" of grace, yet opposes the Immaculate Conception |
| 13th cent. | Legend that the rosary was given to St. Dominic |
| 1476 | Sixtus IV approves the feast of the Immaculate Conception |
| 1568 | The second half of the Hail Mary ("Holy Mary, Mother of God, pray for us sinners…") made official in the Roman Breviary |
| c. 1712 | Louis de Montfort, _True Devotion_: consecration as Mary's "slaves" |
| 1854 | Pius IX defines the Immaculate Conception |
| 1891 | Leo XIII: "no man goeth to Christ but by His Mother" |
| 1917 | Fátima: "God wishes to establish in the world devotion to my Immaculate Heart" |
| 1950 | Pius XII defines the Assumption |
| 1954 | Pius XII institutes the feast of Mary's Queenship (_Ad Caeli Reginam_) |
| 1964 | Paul VI proclaims Mary "Mother of the Church" |
| 2025 | The Vatican has to restrain the push to make her "Co-redemptrix" |

Warner also notes how Mary absorbed the titles of the goddesses she replaced. "Queen of Heaven" was a title of Ishtar, Astarte and Isis. "Star of the Sea" (_Stella Maris_), a title of Isis, came to Mary by a copying error: Jerome had explained the name Miriam as _stilla maris_, "a drop of the sea," and later scribes wrote _stella maris_, "star of the sea." The images of Isis nursing Horus became images of the Madonna and Child.

# Conclusion

The Mary of the Bible is a woman to admire and imitate: she believed the word of God, said "be it unto me according to thy word," magnified the Lord and not herself, called God her Saviour, kept the Law, raised a family, followed her Son to the cross, and was filled with the Holy Ghost with the rest of the church. She never asked anyone to pray to her. Her last recorded words to others were "Whatsoever he saith unto you, do it."

The Mary of Rome is a different person: sinless, ever-virgin, crowned Queen of Heaven, dispenser of graces, the one through whom we must go to reach Christ. Every step of that picture was added after the apostles: from an apocryphal novel, a council won with gold, forged letters, the visions of monks and nuns, and the needs of nations and armies. The real Mary would be grieved to see her name used to take glory from her Son. When Peter was offered worship, he said "Stand up; I myself also am a man" (Acts 10:26). When John fell at an angel's feet, the angel said "See thou do it not… worship God" (Revelation 19:10). Mary would say the same.

> **Luke 1:46-47:** "And Mary said, My soul doth magnify the Lord, And my spirit hath rejoiced in God my Saviour."

> **Isaiah 42:8:** "I am the LORD: that is my name: and my glory will I not give to another, neither my praise to graven images."

> **Acts 4:12:** "Neither is there salvation in any other: for there is none other name under heaven given among men, whereby we must be saved."
