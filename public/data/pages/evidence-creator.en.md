## The question

> **Romans 1:20:** "For the invisible things of him from the creation of the world are clearly seen, being understood by the things that are made, even his eternal power and Godhead; so that they are without excuse:"

> **Psalm 19:1:** "The heavens declare the glory of God; and the firmament sheweth his handywork."

Does the universe point to a Creator, or did everything come from nothing, by chance? This page sets out the evidence the way a detective or a jury would weigh it: not one proof that ends the discussion, but many independent lines of evidence that all point the same way. Several of them are made famous by Lee Strobel's _The Case for a Creator_ (2004), in which a former atheist journalist interviews scientists and philosophers.

Ask yourself honestly, at each step: which explanation fits the evidence better — **a mind**, or **nothing plus time plus chance**?

# 1. Something cannot come from nothing

You have never come home and found a random elephant standing in your living room, and if you did, you would never say, "It just appeared, from nothing, for no reason." Nothing has no properties, no energy, no laws, no potential. Nothing produces nothing.

Yet that is exactly what an atheist must believe about the whole universe: that all the matter, energy, space and time that exist came from nothing, with no cause.

The argument is simple (philosophers call it the _kalam_ argument):

1. Whatever **begins** to exist has a cause.
2. The universe **began** to exist.
3. Therefore the universe has a cause.

That cause must be outside space, time and matter (because it created them), immensely powerful, and personal — able to **choose** to create. That is a description of God.

> **Genesis 1:1:** "In the beginning God created the heaven and the earth."

> **Hebrews 11:3:** "Through faith we understand that the worlds were framed by the word of God, so that things which are seen were not made of things which do appear."

> **John 1:3:** "All things were made by him; and without him was not any thing made that was made."

# 2. The universe had a beginning

For most of history, many scientists assumed the universe was eternal — it had always existed, so it needed no Creator. Twentieth-century science overturned that:

- **The universe is expanding.** Georges Lemaître (1927) and Edwin Hubble (1929) showed that galaxies are moving apart. Run the film backwards, and everything converges to a beginning: the "Big Bang," a starting point of space, time, matter and energy.
- **The echo of that beginning** — the cosmic microwave background radiation — was discovered in 1965.
- **The Borde–Guth–Vilenkin theorem** (2003) showed that any universe that has, on average, been expanding cannot be eternal in the past; it must have a beginning. Alexander Vilenkin wrote: "**There is no escape: they have to face the problem of a cosmic beginning**" (_Many Worlds in One_, 2006).

The astronomer Robert Jastrow, an agnostic, described the scientists' surprise:

> "For the scientist who has lived by his faith in the power of reason, the story ends like a bad dream. He has scaled the mountains of ignorance; he is about to conquer the highest peak; as he pulls himself over the final rock, he is greeted by a band of theologians who have been sitting there for centuries." (_God and the Astronomers_, 1978)

The Bible said it in its first sentence: "In the beginning God created the heaven and the earth."

# 3. The laws of thermodynamics

- **The First Law:** energy is neither created nor destroyed by natural processes. Nature can only rearrange what already exists; it cannot create the energy in the first place.
- **The Second Law:** in a closed system, **entropy** — disorder, the spreading out of usable energy — always increases. Stars burn out, heat spreads evenly, ordered things run down. The universe is like a clock that is unwinding.

If the universe were eternal, it would have run down an infinite time ago, and everything would already be at the same cold temperature, with no usable energy left ("heat death"). Yet the universe is still full of usable energy and order. So it was **wound up** at a finite time in the past. A clock that is running down had to be wound.

The physicist Roger Penrose calculated that the odds of the universe beginning in such a low-entropy, ordered state by chance are about **1 in 10^10^123** — a number with more zeros than there are particles in the universe (_The Emperor's New Mind_, 1989).

> **Psalm 102:25-27:** "Of old hast thou laid the foundation of the earth: and the heavens are the work of thy hands. They shall perish, but thou shalt endure: yea, all of them shall wax old like a garment; as a vesture shalt thou change them, and they shall be changed: But thou art the same, and thy years shall have no end."

# 4. The fine-tuning of the universe

The laws of physics contain constants — numbers that could, as far as we know, have had different values. Physicists of every belief have discovered that dozens of them are set within extremely narrow limits that allow life to exist:

- **The cosmological constant** (the energy of empty space): if it were larger by even a tiny fraction — estimates are as small as one part in 10^120 — the universe would have flown apart before stars and galaxies could form.
- **The strength of gravity:** slightly stronger and stars would burn out too fast; slightly weaker and stars would never ignite.
- **The strong nuclear force:** a few percent weaker, and no element but hydrogen could exist; a few percent stronger, and hydrogen would be used up and there would be no water and no long-lived stars.
- **The mass of the neutron** compared to the proton: a fraction of a percent different, and atoms as we know them could not form.
- **The electromagnetic force**, the ratio of electrons to protons, the expansion rate of the early universe, and many more.

And on our planet: Earth is at the right distance from the right kind of star, with the right size, the right atmosphere, liquid water, a large moon that stabilizes its tilt, and a magnetic field that shields it from solar wind and cosmic radiation.

The non-Christian astronomer Sir Fred Hoyle wrote: "A common sense interpretation of the facts suggests that **a superintellect has monkeyed with physics**, as well as with chemistry and biology, and that there are no blind forces worth speaking about in nature" (_Engineering and Science_, 1981). The cosmologist Martin Rees wrote a whole book, _Just Six Numbers_ (1999), on how finely set the universe is.

**Think about coins.** If you throw 50 coins and every one lands heads, the odds are about 1 in 10^15 (a thousand trillion). Nobody would believe it happened by chance; they would check the coins. The universe has not 50 but dozens of "coins," many tuned far more finely than heads or tails — and all of them landed right.

The usual reply is that there might be an infinite number of other universes we cannot see (a "multiverse"), and we just happen to be in a lucky one. But there is no evidence for those universes; the theory was invented to escape the conclusion. It takes more faith to believe in infinite invisible universes than in one Creator, which would still be needed to create the "multiverse."

# 5. Design: more time does not help

Imagine these:

- **Lightning strikes a beach.** How many years would you need before the lightning and the wind and the waves produce a sandcastle with walls, doors, windows, door handles, a kitchen, and plumbing?
- **Ink spills on a white page.** How many years would you need before the spills produce letters, words, sentences, ideas and paragraphs — a page of a novel?
- **A tornado passes through a junkyard.** How many tornadoes before one assembles a working Boeing 747? (Fred Hoyle's famous comparison for the chance origin of life.)

We all know the answer: not a million years, not a billion. And here is the point many people miss: **giving it more time does not make it more likely; it makes it less likely.** Time is not a builder. Left to itself, time **destroys** order. Waves wash castles away. Ink smears and fades. Machines rust. Chemistry is the same: in water, the building blocks of life — amino acids, proteins, RNA — **break apart** faster than they join together. The Second Law of Thermodynamics says that without a guiding intelligence, things move from order to disorder, not the reverse. Time, plus chance, plus nothing, gives you ruins.

Whenever we see **specified complexity** — a pattern that is both highly improbable and carries a meaning or a function, like a sentence, a computer program or a machine — we know from all our experience that a mind produced it. No one has ever observed it arise any other way.

> **Psalm 139:14:** "I will praise thee; for I am fearfully and wonderfully made: marvellous are thy works; and that my soul knoweth right well."

> **Isaiah 40:26:** "Lift up your eyes on high, and behold who hath created these things, that bringeth out their host by number: he calleth them all by names by the greatness of his might, for that he is strong in power; not one faileth."

# 6. DNA: a language written in our cells

Every cell in your body carries a library of information written in a four-letter chemical alphabet — **A, T, C, G**.

- One copy of the human genome is about **3 billion letters**, roughly 750 megabytes of information — and almost every cell carries two copies.
- The DNA in a single cell is about two meters long, folded into a nucleus a few thousandths of a millimeter wide. Laid end to end, the DNA in all the roughly 30 trillion cells of one human body would reach from the Earth to the Sun and back **hundreds of times**.
- The cell **copies** this information with astonishing accuracy, **proofreads** it, and **repairs** it when it is damaged.
- It is **read** like code: the cell's machinery translates sequences of letters into proteins, using a genetic **code** that maps three-letter "words" to amino acids.

Bill Gates, who knows something about software, wrote: "**DNA is like a computer program but far, far more advanced than any software ever created**" (_The Road Ahead_, 1995).

And what is information? A flash drive weighs the same whether it is empty or full. Information is not matter or energy; it is a message, and every message we know of comes from a mind. The philosopher **Antony Flew**, for decades the world's most famous atheist, changed his mind in 2004 largely because of DNA: the complexity of the arrangements needed to produce life, he wrote, convinced him that "intelligence must have been involved" (_There Is a God_, 2007).

# 7. A single protein

Life needs proteins — the machines of the cell — and proteins are chains of amino acids that must be in a precise order and fold into a precise shape to work.

- A modest protein is about 150 amino acids long, chosen from 20 different kinds. The number of possible sequences is 20^150 — about 10^195.
- The molecular biologist Douglas Axe estimated experimentally that only about **1 in 10^77** sequences of that length would fold into a working shape (_Journal of Molecular Biology_, 2004).
- For comparison, there are about 10^80 atoms in the whole observable universe.
- And the simplest living cell needs **hundreds** of different proteins working together at once, plus DNA to store the instructions, plus RNA and ribosomes to read them — and the ribosomes are themselves made of proteins. Which came first?

> **Psalm 104:24:** "O LORD, how manifold are thy works! in wisdom hast thou made them all: the earth is full of thy riches."

# 8. The origin-of-life experiments

In 1952–1953, Stanley **Miller** and Harold Urey passed electric sparks through a mixture of gases in a sealed glass apparatus and produced some amino acids. It was hailed as proof that life could arise by itself. It proved much less:

- It used a gas mixture (methane, ammonia, hydrogen) that most geochemists today do not think matches Earth's early atmosphere; with more realistic mixtures the results are far poorer.
- It produced a sticky tar and a **50/50 mixture** of left- and right-handed amino acids, while living things use only left-handed ones.
- The products had to be **trapped** and removed from the sparks, or the same energy would have destroyed them — an intelligent intervention by the experimenters.
- Amino acids are not life. Getting from a few building blocks to a single working protein, let alone a self-copying cell with a genetic code, is a gap that seventy years of research has not closed.

Every experiment since has had the same hidden ingredient: **a scientist** — choosing the chemicals, purifying them, controlling the conditions. Even the experiments show that it takes intelligence to make the ingredients of life.

# 9. The Cambrian explosion

In rocks about 530 million years old (by the standard dating), most of the major body plans of animals (phyla) appear in a geologically brief window, with no clear ancestors in the rocks below. Darwin knew it was a problem for his theory and wrote that "**the case at present must remain inexplicable; and may be truly urged as a valid argument against the views here entertained**" (_On the Origin of Species_, chapter 10). More than 160 years of searching later, the sudden appearance remains. Building a new body plan requires huge amounts of **new genetic information** — and as Stephen Meyer argues in _Darwin's Doubt_ (2013), the only known source of new information is a mind.

# 10. Common design, not just common descent

Living things share similar features — humans and apes share much of their DNA, and many animals share the same basic bone structure in their limbs. Is that proof that one turned into the other?

Not necessarily. Designers reuse good designs. A car company uses the same engine, wheels and chassis across many models; a programmer reuses the same code in different programs. The similarities do not mean one car **gave birth** to the other — they mean **the same engineer** made both. Similarity is exactly what we would expect if one Creator designed all living things, adapting a common plan to different purposes.

Living things do **adapt**: finch beaks change size, bacteria develop resistance, dog breeders produce new breeds. Those are real changes within limits. But adaptation is the rearrangement and loss of information that already exists; it does not explain where the information came from in the first place. Evolution describes **variation**, not **creation**.

# 11. The moral law

Every human being knows that some things are really wrong — torturing a child for fun, betraying a friend, cruelty to the weak — and not just because we dislike them. We feel the pull of "ought," and we expect others to feel it too. Even someone who says there is no right or wrong will complain the moment someone steals from him.

C. S. Lewis, a former atheist, made this one of his central arguments: a law implies a lawgiver. If there is no God, then morality is only the opinion of a species of animal, and the Holocaust was not really evil — only unpopular. But we know it was evil. So there is a moral law above us, and a Moral Lawgiver above it (_Mere Christianity_, 1952).

> **Romans 2:14-15:** "For when the Gentiles, which have not the law, do by nature the things contained in the law, these, having not the law, are a law unto themselves: Which shew the work of the law written in their hearts, their conscience also bearing witness, and their thoughts the mean while accusing or else excusing one another;)"

And every person also knows the other half of the story: we break that law. A little child learns to lie without being taught. Every human institution, sooner or later, is corrupted. We are not only creatures who know right from wrong; we are sinners who need forgiveness.

# 12. The mind that reasons

If the universe is only matter in motion, then your thoughts are only chemical reactions in a brain that evolved to survive, not to find truth. But then why should you trust **any** of your thoughts — including the thought that atheism is true?

Darwin himself felt this: "With me **the horrid doubt** always arises whether the convictions of man's mind, which has been developed from the mind of the lower animals, are of any value or at all trustworthy" (letter to William Graham, 1881).

The fact that we can reason, do mathematics, discover the laws of nature, and ask whether God exists makes sense if we were made by a rational Mind, in His image. A rational being requires a rational source. Mindless matter does not explain minds.

> **Genesis 1:27:** "So God created man in his own image, in the image of God created he him; male and female created he them."

> **Acts 17:24-28:** "God that made the world and all things therein, seeing that he is Lord of heaven and earth, dwelleth not in temples made with hands; Neither is worshipped with men’s hands, as though he needed any thing, seeing he giveth to all life, and breath, and all things; And hath made of one blood all nations of men for to dwell on all the face of the earth, and hath determined the times before appointed, and the bounds of their habitation; That they should seek the Lord, if haply they might feel after him, and find him, though he be not far from every one of us: For in him we live, and move, and have our being; as certain also of your own poets have said, For we are also his offspring."

# 13. Putting it together

| Evidence                                                   | What it points to                                 |
| ---------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- |
| Something exists rather than nothing                       | A cause beyond the universe                       |
| The universe began; the Second Law shows it was "wound up" | A cause outside time, space and matter            |
| The constants of physics are finely tuned for life         | An intelligent designer                           |
| DNA is a coded language; proteins are precise machines     | A mind as the source of information               |
| Origin-of-life experiments need a scientist                | Intelligence is needed to make life's ingredients |
| The Cambrian explosion: sudden new body plans              | Sudden input of new information                   |
| Similar designs across living things                       | One designer reusing a plan                       |
| The moral law                                              | A moral lawgiver                                  |
| Reason itself                                              | A rational Creator                                |

No single line of evidence forces anyone to believe. But together they form a powerful case, and the alternative — that everything came from nothing, for no reason, and then organized itself into minds that can know right from wrong — requires far more faith.

"The fool hath said in his heart, There is no God." (Psalm 14:1)
