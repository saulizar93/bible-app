## The claim

The Roman Catholic and Orthodox churches teach that believers may, and should, pray to the Virgin Mary and to the saints, asking for their prayers and their help. The Council of Trent (1563) declared that "it is good and useful suppliantly to invoke them, and to have recourse to their prayers, aid and help" (Session 25), and the _Catechism of the Catholic Church_ calls Mary "Advocate, Helper, Benefactress, and Mediatrix" (969) and teaches the faithful to pray "in communion with" her and the saints (2683).

This page tests that teaching against Scripture and history. In short:

- **Jesus Christ** is the one Mediator and the one who "ever liveth to make intercession" for those who come to God by Him. Nobody else is needed between the believer and God.
- In the whole Bible, **prayer is addressed to God alone**. He alone is called "thou that hearest prayer," and He alone knows every heart.
- **Prayer is worship.** Scripture calls it _latreia_, the service that belongs to God only, and compares it to **incense**, which could be offered to no one but God. When Israel burned incense to the bronze serpent, an object God Himself had commanded, Hezekiah broke it in pieces.
- God is **jealous** when His people seek help anywhere else: from physicians instead of Him, from foreign kings, from other gods.
- **Nobody in the Bible prayed to a dead saint.** The only man who consulted one was Saul, and he died for it.
- **Revelation 5:8** does not show saints receiving prayers; it shows the prayers of all believers rising to God, and later being answered with judgment.
- Historically, prayer to the saints grew out of the honor given to the martyrs, became common only after the fourth century, and took over the place of the **pagan gods**, often on purpose.

The one who stands at God's right hand interceding is the Lord Jesus Christ:

> **Hebrews 7:25:** "Wherefore he is able also to save them to the uttermost that come unto God by him, seeing he ever liveth to make intercession for them."

So why would anyone go to Mary, or to anyone less than Jesus?

# 1. One Mediator, one Intercessor

The New Testament never sends believers to a chain of helpers. It sends them to Christ, and through Him straight to the throne of God, "boldly." The Spirit Himself helps us pray and intercedes within us; the Son intercedes for us at God's right hand. There is no gap left for anyone else to fill.

> **1 Timothy 2:5:** "For there is one God, and one mediator between God and men, the man Christ Jesus;"

> **Romans 8:34:** "Who is he that condemneth? It is Christ that died, yea rather, that is risen again, who is even at the right hand of God, who also maketh intercession for us."

> **1 John 2:1:** "My little children, these things write I unto you, that ye sin not. And if any man sin, we have an advocate with the Father, Jesus Christ the righteous:"

> **Hebrews 4:14-16:** "Seeing then that we have a great high priest, that is passed into the heavens, Jesus the Son of God, let us hold fast our profession. For we have not an high priest which cannot be touched with the feeling of our infirmities; but was in all points tempted like as we are, yet without sin. Let us therefore come boldly unto the throne of grace, that we may obtain mercy, and find grace to help in time of need."

> **John 14:6:** "Jesus saith unto him, I am the way, the truth, and the life: no man cometh unto the Father, but by me."

> **Romans 8:26-27:** "Likewise the Spirit also helpeth our infirmities: for we know not what we should pray for as we ought: but the Spirit itself maketh intercession for us with groanings which cannot be uttered. And he that searcheth the hearts knoweth what is the mind of the Spirit, because he maketh intercession for the saints according to the will of God."

> **Matthew 11:28:** "Come unto me, all ye that labour and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest."

From the Psalms to the Epistles, every invitation to pray points to the same person: God. "Call upon me," "seek ye the LORD," "Our Father," "ask the Father in my name," "let your requests be made known unto God."

> **Psalm 50:15:** "And call upon me in the day of trouble: I will deliver thee, and thou shalt glorify me."

> **Jeremiah 29:12:** "Then shall ye call upon me, and ye shall go and pray unto me, and I will hearken unto you."

> **Psalm 145:18:** "The LORD is nigh unto all them that call upon him, to all that call upon him in truth."

> **Matthew 6:6:** "But thou, when thou prayest, enter into thy closet, and when thou hast shut thy door, pray to thy Father which is in secret; and thy Father which seeth in secret shall reward thee openly."

> **Matthew 6:9:** "After this manner therefore pray ye: Our Father which art in heaven, Hallowed be thy name."

> **John 14:13-14:** "And whatsoever ye shall ask in my name, that will I do, that the Father may be glorified in the Son. If ye shall ask any thing in my name, I will do it."

> **John 15:16:** "Ye have not chosen me, but I have chosen you, and ordained you, that ye should go and bring forth fruit, and that your fruit should remain: that whatsoever ye shall ask of the Father in my name, he may give it you."

> **John 16:23-24:** "And in that day ye shall ask me nothing. Verily, verily, I say unto you, Whatsoever ye shall ask the Father in my name, he will give it you. Hitherto have ye asked nothing in my name: ask, and ye shall receive, that your joy may be full."

> **Philippians 4:6:** "Be careful for nothing; but in every thing by prayer and supplication with thanksgiving let your requests be made known unto God."

> **Acts 4:24:** "And when they heard that, they lifted up their voice to God with one accord, and said, Lord, thou art God, which hast made heaven, and earth, and the sea, and all that in them is:"

## "O thou that hearest prayer"

That is God's title, and the reason is simple: only God can hear every prayer, everywhere, at once, and only God knows the heart from which it comes. To pray to someone is to treat him as able to hear and answer from heaven, which is to give him what belongs to God alone.

> **Psalm 65:2:** "O thou that hearest prayer, unto thee shall all flesh come."

> **1 Kings 8:38-39:** "What prayer and supplication soever be made by any man, or by all thy people Israel, which shall know every man the plague of his own heart, and spread forth his hands toward this house: Then hear thou in heaven thy dwelling place, and forgive, and do, and give to every man according to his ways, whose heart thou knowest; (for thou, even thou only, knowest the hearts of all the children of men;)"

> **Psalm 139:1-4:** "O LORD, thou hast searched me, and known me. Thou knowest my downsitting and mine uprising, thou understandest my thought afar off. Thou compassest my path and my lying down, and art acquainted with all my ways. For there is not a word in my tongue, but, lo, O LORD, thou knowest it altogether."

> **Jeremiah 17:10:** "I the LORD search the heart, I try the reins, even to give every man according to his ways, and according to the fruit of his doings."

## Calling on the name is worship

To "call upon the name of the LORD" is the Bible's way of describing worship and salvation, and Paul applies it to Jesus. "How then shall they call on him in whom they have not believed?" We call on the one we believe in.

> **Joel 2:32:** "And it shall come to pass, that whosoever shall call on the name of the LORD shall be delivered: for in mount Zion and in Jerusalem shall be deliverance, as the LORD hath said, and in the remnant whom the LORD shall call."

> **Romans 10:13-14:** "For whosoever shall call upon the name of the Lord shall be saved. How then shall they call on him in whom they have not believed? and how shall they believe in him of whom they have not heard? and how shall they hear without a preacher?"

> **Acts 7:59:** "And they stoned Stephen, calling upon God, and saying, Lord Jesus, receive my spirit."

# 3. Prayer is worship

## Prayer is _latreia_

The Greek word for the worship due to God alone is _latreia_ (verb _latreuō_, G3000): "Thou shalt worship the Lord thy God, and him only shalt thou serve" (_latreuseis_, Matthew 4:10). Luke uses the same verb for **prayer**: Anna "served God with fastings and prayers night and day," and the twelve tribes "instantly serving God day and night." Prayer is an act of _latreia_.

> **Matthew 4:10:** "Then saith Jesus unto him, Get thee hence, Satan: for it is written, Thou shalt worship the Lord thy God, and him only shalt thou serve."

> **Luke 2:37:** "And she was a widow of about fourscore and four years, which departed not from the temple, but served God with fastings and prayers night and day."

> **Acts 26:7:** "Unto which promise our twelve tribes, instantly serving God day and night, hope to come. For which hope’s sake, king Agrippa, I am accused of the Jews."

Catholic teaching answers that saints receive only _dulia_ (honor) and God alone _latria_ (worship). But the New Testament never draws that line (see the lexicon at G1401), and here Scripture itself calls prayer _latreia_. Trent, moreover, asks for the saints' "aid and help," not only their prayers.

## Prayer is incense

Scripture pictures prayer as incense rising before God. In the temple, the people prayed outside while the priest burned incense inside; in heaven, the incense is "the prayers of saints."

> **Psalm 141:2:** "Let my prayer be set forth before thee as incense; and the lifting up of my hands as the evening sacrifice."

> **Luke 1:9-10:** "According to the custom of the priest’s office, his lot was to burn incense when he went into the temple of the Lord. And the whole multitude of the people were praying without at the time of incense."

> **Revelation 5:8:** "And when he had taken the book, the four beasts and four and twenty elders fell down before the Lamb, having every one of them harps, and golden vials full of odours, which are the prayers of saints."

> **Revelation 8:3-4:** "And another angel came and stood at the altar, having a golden censer; and there was given unto him much incense, that he should offer it with the prayers of all saints upon the golden altar which was before the throne. And the smoke of the incense, which came with the prayers of the saints, ascended up before God out of the angel’s hand."

And incense was to be offered **to God alone**. God gave the exact recipe and forbade "strange incense." Nadab and Abihu died for offering "strange fire"; King Uzziah was struck with leprosy for burning incense when he was not allowed to; Israel was judged for burning incense to "the queen of heaven."

> **Exodus 30:7-9:** "And Aaron shall burn thereon sweet incense every morning: when he dresseth the lamps, he shall burn incense upon it. And when Aaron lighteth the lamps at even, he shall burn incense upon it, a perpetual incense before the LORD throughout your generations. Ye shall offer no strange incense thereon, nor burnt sacrifice, nor meat offering; neither shall ye pour drink offering thereon."

> **Exodus 30:37-38:** "And as for the perfume which thou shalt make, ye shall not make to yourselves according to the composition thereof: it shall be unto thee holy for the LORD. Whosoever shall make like unto that, to smell thereto, shall even be cut off from his people."

> **Leviticus 10:1-2:** "And Nadab and Abihu, the sons of Aaron, took either of them his censer, and put fire therein, and put incense thereon, and offered strange fire before the LORD, which he commanded them not. And there went out fire from the LORD, and devoured them, and they died before the LORD."

> **2 Chronicles 26:16-19:** "But when he was strong, his heart was lifted up to his destruction: for he transgressed against the LORD his God, and went into the temple of the LORD to burn incense upon the altar of incense. And Azariah the priest went in after him, and with him fourscore priests of the LORD, that were valiant men: And they withstood Uzziah the king, and said unto him, It appertaineth not unto thee, Uzziah, to burn incense unto the LORD, but to the priests the sons of Aaron, that are consecrated to burn incense: go out of the sanctuary; for thou hast trespassed; neither shall it be for thine honour from the LORD God. Then Uzziah was wroth, and had a censer in his hand to burn incense: and while he was wroth with the priests, the leprosy even rose up in his forehead before the priests in the house of the LORD, from beside the incense altar."

> **Jeremiah 44:17-19:** "But we will certainly do whatsoever thing goeth forth out of our own mouth, to burn incense unto the queen of heaven, and to pour out drink offerings unto her, as we have done, we, and our fathers, our kings, and our princes, in the cities of Judah, and in the streets of Jerusalem: for then had we plenty of victuals, and were well, and saw no evil. But since we left off to burn incense to the queen of heaven, and to pour out drink offerings unto her, we have wanted all things, and have been consumed by the sword and by the famine. And when we burned incense to the queen of heaven, and poured out drink offerings unto her, did we make her cakes to worship her, and pour out drink offerings unto her, without our men?"

> **Jeremiah 44:25:** "Thus saith the LORD of hosts, the God of Israel, saying; Ye and your wives have both spoken with your mouths, and fulfilled with your hand, saying, We will surely perform our vows that we have vowed, to burn incense to the queen of heaven, and to pour out drink offerings unto her: ye will surely accomplish your vows, and surely perform your vows."

(It is worth noticing that Rome gives Mary the very title "Queen of Heaven": the Easter antiphon _Regina caeli_ and Pius XII's encyclical _Ad caeli reginam_, 1954.)

## The bronze serpent

God Himself commanded Moses to make the bronze serpent, and it was used to heal (Numbers 21:8-9). Seven centuries later, Israel was burning incense to it. Hezekiah did not keep it as a holy relic or teach the people to honor it "relatively"; he broke it in pieces and called it _Nehushtan_, "a piece of brass," and God praised him for it. If an object made at God's own command had to be destroyed once it received incense, the incense of prayer cannot be given to any creature.

> **Numbers 21:8-9:** "And the LORD said unto Moses, Make thee a fiery serpent, and set it upon a pole: and it shall come to pass, that every one that is bitten, when he looketh upon it, shall live. And Moses made a serpent of brass, and put it upon a pole, and it came to pass, that if a serpent had bitten any man, when he beheld the serpent of brass, he lived."

> **2 Kings 18:3-5:** "And he did that which was right in the sight of the LORD, according to all that David his father did. He removed the high places, and brake the images, and cut down the groves, and brake in pieces the brasen serpent that Moses had made: for unto those days the children of Israel did burn incense to it: and he called it Nehushtan. He trusted in the LORD God of Israel; so that after him was none like him among all the kings of Judah, nor any that were before him."

# 4. God is jealous when we look elsewhere for help

God does not only forbid worshipping other gods. He is grieved when His people seek **help** anywhere but from Him.

## Asa: physicians instead of God

King Asa began well. But when Baasha attacked, he paid the king of Syria for help instead of relying on God, and the prophet Hanani rebuked him. Later Asa was "diseased in his feet," and "in his disease he sought not to the LORD, but to the physicians," and he died. (Doctors are not the sin; Paul calls Luke "the beloved physician." The sin was seeking them **instead of** God.)

> **2 Chronicles 16:7-9:** "And at that time Hanani the seer came to Asa king of Judah, and said unto him, Because thou hast relied on the king of Syria, and not relied on the LORD thy God, therefore is the host of the king of Syria escaped out of thine hand. Were not the Ethiopians and the Lubims a huge host, with very many chariots and horsemen? yet, because thou didst rely on the LORD, he delivered them into thine hand. For the eyes of the LORD run to and fro throughout the whole earth, to shew himself strong in the behalf of them whose heart is perfect toward him. Herein thou hast done foolishly: therefore from henceforth thou shalt have wars."

> **2 Chronicles 16:12-13:** "And Asa in the thirty and ninth year of his reign was diseased in his feet, until his disease was exceeding great: yet in his disease he sought not to the LORD, but to the physicians. And Asa slept with his fathers, and died in the one and fortieth year of his reign."

## Ahaziah: another god for help

When King Ahaziah fell and was injured, he sent to ask Baal-zebub, the god of Ekron, whether he would recover. Elijah met the messengers: "Is it not because there is not a God in Israel?" Ahaziah died.

> **2 Kings 1:2-4:** "And Ahaziah fell down through a lattice in his upper chamber that was in Samaria, and was sick: and he sent messengers, and said unto them, Go, enquire of Baal-zebub the god of Ekron whether I shall recover of this disease. But the angel of the LORD said to Elijah the Tishbite, Arise, go up to meet the messengers of the king of Samaria, and say unto them, Is it not because there is not a God in Israel, that ye go to enquire of Baal-zebub the god of Ekron? Now therefore thus saith the LORD, Thou shalt not come down from that bed on which thou art gone up, but shalt surely die. And Elijah departed."

> **2 Kings 1:16-17:** "And he said unto him, Thus saith the LORD, Forasmuch as thou hast sent messengers to enquire of Baal-zebub the god of Ekron, is it not because there is no God in Israel to enquire of his word? therefore thou shalt not come down off that bed on which thou art gone up, but shalt surely die. So he died according to the word of the LORD which Elijah had spoken. And Jehoram reigned in his stead in the second year of Jehoram the son of Jehoshaphat king of Judah; because he had no son."

## Egypt, Assyria, and the arm of flesh

Israel's kings ran to Egypt and to Assyria for help, and the prophets called it rebellion. God's verdict is the same for every age: cursed is the man who trusts in man; blessed is the man whose trust is the LORD.

> **Isaiah 30:1-2:** "Woe to the rebellious children, saith the LORD, that take counsel, but not of me; and that cover with a covering, but not of my spirit, that they may add sin to sin: That walk to go down into Egypt, and have not asked at my mouth; to strengthen themselves in the strength of Pharaoh, and to trust in the shadow of Egypt!"

> **Isaiah 31:1:** "Woe to them that go down to Egypt for help; and stay on horses, and trust in chariots, because they are many; and in horsemen, because they are very strong; but they look not unto the Holy One of Israel, neither seek the LORD!"

> **Hosea 5:13:** "When Ephraim saw his sickness, and Judah saw his wound, then went Ephraim to the Assyrian, and sent to king Jareb: yet could he not heal you, nor cure you of your wound."

> **Jeremiah 17:5-7:** "Thus saith the LORD; Cursed be the man that trusteth in man, and maketh flesh his arm, and whose heart departeth from the LORD. For he shall be like the heath in the desert, and shall not see when good cometh; but shall inhabit the parched places in the wilderness, in a salt land and not inhabited. Blessed is the man that trusteth in the LORD, and whose hope the LORD is."

> **Psalm 146:3-5:** "Put not your trust in princes, nor in the son of man, in whom there is no help. His breath goeth forth, he returneth to his earth; in that very day his thoughts perish. Happy is he that hath the God of Jacob for his help, whose hope is in the LORD his God:"

> **Psalm 121:1-2:** "I will lift up mine eyes unto the hills, from whence cometh my help. My help cometh from the LORD, which made heaven and earth."

> **Psalm 46:1:** "God is our refuge and strength, a very present help in trouble."

## A jealous God

His name is Jealous, He will not give His glory to another, and there is no Saviour beside Him.

> **Exodus 34:14:** "For thou shalt worship no other god: for the LORD, whose name is Jealous, is a jealous God:"

> **Isaiah 42:8:** "I am the LORD: that is my name: and my glory will I not give to another, neither my praise to graven images."

> **Isaiah 43:11:** "I, even I, am the LORD; and beside me there is no saviour."

> **Isaiah 45:22:** "Look unto me, and be ye saved, all the ends of the earth: for I am God, and there is none else."

# 5. Nobody in the Bible prayed to the dead

The Bible has thousands of prayers. Not one is addressed to a departed believer. Nobody prayed to Abraham, Moses, David, Elijah or Isaiah. When James was killed, the church did not pray to him to save Peter; "prayer was made without ceasing of the church **unto God** for him." When Stephen was stoned, he called on the Lord Jesus. At the Transfiguration, Moses and Elijah appeared, and the Father said, "hear **him**," and the disciples "saw no man, save Jesus only." Mary herself, in the upper room, **prayed with** the disciples; nobody prayed to her. Her last recorded words to others point away from herself: "Whatsoever **he** saith unto you, do it."

> **Acts 12:2:** "And he killed James the brother of John with the sword."

> **Acts 12:5:** "Peter therefore was kept in prison: but prayer was made without ceasing of the church unto God for him."

> **Acts 7:59-60:** "And they stoned Stephen, calling upon God, and saying, Lord Jesus, receive my spirit. And he kneeled down, and cried with a loud voice, Lord, lay not this sin to their charge. And when he had said this, he fell asleep."

> **Matthew 17:3-8:** "And, behold, there appeared unto them Moses and Elias talking with him. Then answered Peter, and said unto Jesus, Lord, it is good for us to be here: if thou wilt, let us make here three tabernacles; one for thee, and one for Moses, and one for Elias. While he yet spake, behold, a bright cloud overshadowed them: and behold a voice out of the cloud, which said, This is my beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased; hear ye him. And when the disciples heard it, they fell on their face, and were sore afraid. And Jesus came and touched them, and said, Arise, and be not afraid. And when they had lifted up their eyes, they saw no man, save Jesus only."

> **Acts 1:14:** "These all continued with one accord in prayer and supplication, with the women, and Mary the mother of Jesus, and with his brethren."

> **John 2:5:** "His mother saith unto the servants, Whatsoever he saith unto you, do it."

## The only man who consulted a dead saint

Saul, cut off from God, went to the witch of Endor to call up the prophet Samuel. Scripture's verdict: "So Saul died for his transgression... for asking counsel of one that had a familiar spirit, to enquire of it; and enquired not of the LORD." The Law forbade every attempt to consult the dead, and Isaiah asks the question that settles the matter: "should not a people seek unto their God? for the living to the dead?"

> **1 Chronicles 10:13-14:** "So Saul died for his transgression which he committed against the LORD, even against the word of the LORD, which he kept not, and also for asking counsel of one that had a familiar spirit, to enquire of it; And enquired not of the LORD: therefore he slew him, and turned the kingdom unto David the son of Jesse."

> **Deuteronomy 18:10-12:** "There shall not be found among you any one that maketh his son or his daughter to pass through the fire, or that useth divination, or an observer of times, or an enchanter, or a witch, Or a charmer, or a consulter with familiar spirits, or a wizard, or a necromancer. For all that do these things are an abomination unto the LORD: and because of these abominations the LORD thy God doth drive them out from before thee."

> **Isaiah 8:19-20:** "And when they shall say unto you, Seek unto them that have familiar spirits, and unto wizards that peep, and that mutter: should not a people seek unto their God? for the living to the dead? To the law and to the testimony: if they speak not according to this word, it is because there is no light in them."

## The only "prayer to a saint" in the Bible

There is one man in Scripture who addresses a departed saint and asks him for help: the rich man in torment, who prays to "Father Abraham" for mercy and for a messenger to his brothers. Both requests are refused, and Abraham sends him back to the written Word: "They have Moses and the prophets; let them hear them."

> **Luke 16:24-26:** "And he cried and said, Father Abraham, have mercy on me, and send Lazarus, that he may dip the tip of his finger in water, and cool my tongue; for I am tormented in this flame. But Abraham said, Son, remember that thou in thy lifetime receivedst thy good things, and likewise Lazarus evil things: but now he is comforted, and thou art tormented. And beside all this, between us and you there is a great gulf fixed: so that they which would pass from hence to you cannot; neither can they pass to us, that would come from thence."

> **Luke 16:29-31:** "Abraham saith unto him, They have Moses and the prophets; let them hear them. And he said, Nay, father Abraham: but if one went unto them from the dead, they will repent. And he said unto him, If they hear not Moses and the prophets, neither will they be persuaded, though one rose from the dead."

## The Saints do not know us

Even the greatest of the dead do not hear God's people. Isaiah prays, "Doubtless thou art our father, though Abraham be ignorant of us, and Israel acknowledge us not: thou, O LORD, art our father."

> **Isaiah 63:16:** "Doubtless thou art our father, though Abraham be ignorant of us, and Israel acknowledge us not: thou, O LORD, art our father, our redeemer; thy name is from everlasting."

> **Job 14:21:** "His sons come to honour, and he knoweth it not; and they are brought low, but he perceiveth it not of them."

> **Ecclesiastes 9:5-6:** "For the living know that they shall die: but the dead know not any thing, neither have they any more a reward; for the memory of them is forgotten. Also their love, and their hatred, and their envy, is now perished; neither have they any more a portion for ever in any thing that is done under the sun."

## Paul had to stay alive to help the church

Paul longed to die and be with Christ, which he called "far better." Yet he chose to remain in the flesh, because staying alive was "more needful for you," "for your furtherance and joy of faith." If departed saints could help believers from heaven, Paul's reasoning would make no sense: he could have gone to be with the Lord and gone on helping the Philippians from there. Instead he says plainly that his usefulness to them depended on his remaining **alive**.

> **Philippians 1:21-25:** "For to me to live is Christ, and to die is gain. But if I live in the flesh, this is the fruit of my labour: yet what I shall choose I wot not. For I am in a strait betwixt two, having a desire to depart, and to be with Christ; which is far better: Nevertheless to abide in the flesh is more needful for you. And having this confidence, I know that I shall abide and continue with you all for your furtherance and joy of faith;"

## "But we ask our friends to pray for us"

We do, and Scripture tells us to: Paul asked the churches to pray for him, and James says, "pray one for another." But that is speaking to a living brother face to face and asking him to pray **to God** with us. It is not calling on someone in heaven, believing he hears our thoughts from anywhere on earth, and asking him for "aid and help." Scripture commands the first and never once shows the second.

> **Romans 15:30:** "Now I beseech you, brethren, for the Lord Jesus Christ’s sake, and for the love of the Spirit, that ye strive together with me in your prayers to God for me;"

> **James 5:16:** "Confess your faults one to another, and pray one for another, that ye may be healed. The effectual fervent prayer of a righteous man availeth much."

> **1 Thessalonians 5:25:** "Brethren, pray for us."

# 6. God's servants refuse what belongs to God

When Cornelius bowed to Peter, Peter lifted him up: "I myself also am a man." At Lystra, Paul and Barnabas tore their clothes when the crowd tried to honor them. When John fell before the angel, the angel said, "See thou do it not... worship God." Paul warns against the "worshipping of angels" as a false humility. If the apostles and angels would not accept such honor on earth, they would not want it from heaven.

> **Acts 10:25-26:** "And as Peter was coming in, Cornelius met him, and fell down at his feet, and worshipped him. But Peter took him up, saying, Stand up; I myself also am a man."

> **Acts 14:14-15:** "Which when the apostles, Barnabas and Paul, heard of, they rent their clothes, and ran in among the people, crying out, And saying, Sirs, why do ye these things? We also are men of like passions with you, and preach unto you that ye should turn from these vanities unto the living God, which made heaven, and earth, and the sea, and all things that are therein:"

> **Revelation 19:10:** "And I fell at his feet to worship him. And he said unto me, See thou do it not: I am thy fellowservant, and of thy brethren that have the testimony of Jesus: worship God: for the testimony of Jesus is the spirit of prophecy."

> **Revelation 22:8-9:** "And I John saw these things, and heard them. And when I had heard and seen, I fell down to worship before the feet of the angel which shewed me these things. Then saith he unto me, See thou do it not: for I am thy fellowservant, and of thy brethren the prophets, and of them which keep the sayings of this book: worship God."

> **Colossians 2:18-19:** "Let no man beguile you of your reward in a voluntary humility and worshipping of angels, intruding into those things which he hath not seen, vainly puffed up by his fleshly mind, And not holding the Head, from which all the body by joints and bands having nourishment ministered, and knit together, increaseth with the increase of God."

# 7. Revelation 5:8

This verse is the main text offered to prove that the saints in heaven receive and present our prayers:

> **Revelation 5:8:** "And when he had taken the book, the four beasts and four and twenty elders fell down before the Lamb, having every one of them harps, and golden vials full of odours, which are the prayers of saints."

Look at what it actually says.

1. **The ones holding the prayers are not the saints.** They are "the four beasts and four and twenty elders." The prayers are "the prayers **of** saints," and in the New Testament "saints" means all God's people, living believers on earth (Romans 1:7; 1 Corinthians 1:2; Philippians 1:1). The saints are the ones who pray, not the ones prayed to.
2. **Nobody is praying to the elders.** The prayers are offered **before the Lamb**, and the elders fall down and worship Him. The scene is worship given to God.
3. **It is a symbolic vision.** If it proved that we should pray to those who hold the prayers, it would prove we should pray to **twenty-four elders and four living creatures**, not to Mary, Joseph or any named saint. No church teaches that.
4. **Who are the elders and the creatures?** Revelation itself suggests the meaning. The holy city has twelve gates with the names of the **twelve tribes of Israel** and twelve foundations with the names of the **twelve apostles** (Revelation 21:12-14), so the twenty-four elders are commonly read as the whole people of God, Old and New Testament together. Others note the twenty-four courses of priests in the temple (1 Chronicles 24): the elders picture God's people as a priesthood. The four living creatures, with the faces of a lion, an ox, a man and an eagle, come from Ezekiel's cherubim and Isaiah's seraphim (Ezekiel 1:10; Isaiah 6:2-3): they are commonly read as the highest of God's creatures, or as all creation (wild animals, cattle, mankind, birds) worshipping its Creator; from the second century they were also used as symbols of the four Gospels (Irenaeus, _Against Heresies_ 3.11.8). On no reading are they departed saints receiving requests.
5. **These prayers are answered with judgment.** The martyrs under the altar cry, "How long, O Lord... dost thou not judge and avenge our blood?" In chapter 8 the incense "with the prayers of all saints" rises before God, and then the same censer is filled with fire from the altar and **cast into the earth**, and the trumpets of judgment begin. The prayers in these golden vials are the cries of God's people for justice, answered by God Himself.

> **Revelation 6:9-10:** "And when he had opened the fifth seal, I saw under the altar the souls of them that were slain for the word of God, and for the testimony which they held: And they cried with a loud voice, saying, How long, O Lord, holy and true, dost thou not judge and avenge our blood on them that dwell on the earth?"

> **Revelation 8:3-5:** "And another angel came and stood at the altar, having a golden censer; and there was given unto him much incense, that he should offer it with the prayers of all saints upon the golden altar which was before the throne. And the smoke of the incense, which came with the prayers of the saints, ascended up before God out of the angel’s hand. And the angel took the censer, and filled it with fire of the altar, and cast it into the earth: and there were voices, and thunderings, and lightnings, and an earthquake."

Hebrews 12:1, the "cloud of witnesses," is sometimes offered too, but the witnesses are the men and women of faith in Hebrews 11, examples who **testified** by their lives; the verse says to look "unto Jesus" (12:2), not to them.

# 8. How the saints replaced the gods

## Ephesus: Paul put the image-makers out of business

In Ephesus, the city of the great goddess Artemis (Diana), Paul's preaching made new converts burn their books of magic, and the silversmith Demetrius gathered the craftsmen: "this Paul hath persuaded and turned away much people, saying that they be no gods, which are made with hands: so that not only this our craft is in danger to be set at nought..."

> **Acts 19:18-19:** "And many that believed came, and confessed, and shewed their deeds. Many of them also which used curious arts brought their books together, and burned them before all men: and they counted the price of them, and found it fifty thousand pieces of silver."

> **Acts 19:24-27:** "For a certain man named Demetrius, a silversmith, which made silver shrines for Diana, brought no small gain unto the craftsmen; Whom he called together with the workmen of like occupation, and said, Sirs, ye know that by this craft we have our wealth. Moreover ye see and hear, that not alone at Ephesus, but almost throughout all Asia, this Paul hath persuaded and turned away much people, saying that they be no gods, which are made with hands: So that not only this our craft is in danger to be set at nought; but also that the temple of the great goddess Diana should be despised, and her magnificence should be destroyed, whom all Asia and the world worshippeth."

If the apostles had taught Christians to honor holy images and pray to saints, the silversmiths would have had nothing to fear: they could simply have made shrines of the Virgin and statues of the apostles instead. Their trade was in danger because the gospel left no place for sacred images or heavenly patrons at all. (It is a striking irony that the council which proclaimed Mary _Theotokos_, the Council of 431, met in **Ephesus**, the old city of the great mother goddess.)

## A deliberate strategy

The change was not only an accident of popular devotion. Church leaders adopted it on purpose:

- **Pope Gregory the Great** (601) advised that the temples of the English pagans should not be destroyed but sprinkled with holy water and turned into churches, and that their festivals of sacrifice be replaced with feasts of the saints whose relics were placed there, so that, keeping some of their outward joys, the people would more easily accept the new faith (letter to Abbot Mellitus, in Bede, _Ecclesiastical History_ 1.30).
- **The Pantheon** in Rome, "the temple of all the gods," was rededicated by Pope Boniface IV in 609 as the church of **Saint Mary and All the Martyrs**.
- **Theodoret of Cyrus** (c. 430) boasted to the pagans that "the Lord has brought in His dead in place of your gods" (_Cure of the Greek Maladies_ 8).

## Gods with departments, saints with patronages

Paganism had a god for every need. Catholic and Orthodox devotion now has a patron saint for the same needs:

# 9. A later development

## Nothing in the first two centuries

The New Testament contains no prayer to any saint, and neither do the earliest Christian writings after it: Clement of Rome, Ignatius, Polycarp, the _Didache_, Justin Martyr. They honored the martyrs and remembered them; the church of Smyrna gathered at Polycarp's grave to celebrate "the birthday of his martyrdom" (_Martyrdom of Polycarp_ 18). Remembering is not praying.

## "Rest in peace" is a wish, not a prayer

Early Christian graves say things like "in peace," "may you live in God" (_vivas in Deo_), or "may your spirit rest well." Those are wishes and blessings for the dead, exactly as we say "Rest in peace" at a funeral today. Nobody thinks a mourner who says "Rest in peace" is praying to the deceased.

## When it began

By the middle of the third century some Christians did begin to ask the martyrs for prayers. Graffiti scratched by pilgrims at the shrine of the apostles on the Appian Way in Rome (after c. 250) read, for example, "Paul and Peter, pray for Victor." The oldest known prayer addressed to Mary, the _Sub tuum praesidium_ ("We fly to thy protection"), survives on a papyrus that scholars date anywhere from the third century to the seventh. That is two hundred years after the apostles, in the age of the martyr cult, not in the New Testament.

## Voices against it

As the practice spread, it was resisted:

- **Epiphanius of Salamis** (c. 375) condemned the Collyridians, women who offered cakes to Mary: "Let Mary be held in honor, but let the Lord be worshipped... let no one worship Mary" (_Panarion_ 79).
- **Augustine** (c. 420) insisted that Christians build no temples to the martyrs and offer them no sacrifice, but honor them as men; worship belongs to God alone (_City of God_ 8.27).
- **Vigilantius**, a priest of Gaul (c. 406), objected to the cult of relics, the vigils at martyrs' tombs and the belief that the dead hear and pray for us; Jerome's furious reply, _Against Vigilantius_, is the record of how such objections were silenced.

Only in 1563 did the Council of Trent define invoking the saints as "good and useful," declaring that those who deny it "think impiously" (Session 25).

# 10. Idolatry is easier than we think

Idolatry is not only bowing to a statue. It is anything that takes the place that belongs to God: money, pleasure, appetite, self. Paul calls covetousness idolatry; Jesus says no one can serve God and mammon; Ezekiel speaks of men who "set up their idols in their heart."

> **Colossians 3:5:** "Mortify therefore your members which are upon the earth; fornication, uncleanness, inordinate affection, evil concupiscence, and covetousness, which is idolatry:"

> **Ephesians 5:5:** "For this ye know, that no whoremonger, nor unclean person, nor covetous man, who is an idolater, hath any inheritance in the kingdom of Christ and of God."

> **Matthew 6:24:** "No man can serve two masters: for either he will hate the one, and love the other; or else he will hold to the one, and despise the other. Ye cannot serve God and mammon."

> **Philippians 3:18-19:** "(For many walk, of whom I have told you often, and now tell you even weeping, that they are the enemies of the cross of Christ: Whose end is destruction, whose God is their belly, and whose glory is in their shame, who mind earthly things.)"

> **2 Timothy 3:2:** "For men shall be lovers of their own selves, covetous, boasters, proud, blasphemers, disobedient to parents, unthankful, unholy,"

> **Ezekiel 14:3:** "Son of man, these men have set up their idols in their heart, and put the stumblingblock of their iniquity before their face: should I be enquired of at all by them?"

> **1 John 5:21:** "Little children, keep yourselves from idols. Amen."

The question is not whether we call it worship but whom we treat as our **Saviour** and our **Helper**. God claims both titles. Whoever we run to for rescue, protection and answers, apart from Him, is sitting in His place, however holy that person was on earth.

John the Baptist, the greatest of the prophets, understood his place: "He must increase, but I must decrease." Paul feared that the church's devotion might be drawn away from "the simplicity that is in Christ," and wanted believers to "attend upon the Lord without distraction." Any saint, however honored, who stands between the believer and Christ is exactly that distraction.

> **John 3:28-30:** "Ye yourselves bear me witness, that I said, I am not the Christ, but that I am sent before him. He that hath the bride is the bridegroom: but the friend of the bridegroom, which standeth and heareth him, rejoiceth greatly because of the bridegroom’s voice: this my joy therefore is fulfilled. He must increase, but I must decrease."

> **2 Corinthians 11:2-3:** "For I am jealous over you with godly jealousy: for I have espoused you to one husband, that I may present you as a chaste virgin to Christ. But I fear, lest by any means, as the serpent beguiled Eve through his subtilty, so your minds should be corrupted from the simplicity that is in Christ."

> **1 Corinthians 7:35:** "And this I speak for your own profit; not that I may cast a snare upon you, but for that which is comely, and that ye may attend upon the Lord without distraction."

# Conclusion

Mary was blessed, and the apostles and martyrs are examples worth following. But not one of them is our Mediator, our Intercessor, our Helper or our Saviour. Jesus is, and He invites us to come to Him directly.

> **Hebrews 4:16:** "Let us therefore come boldly unto the throne of grace, that we may obtain mercy, and find grace to help in time of need."

> **Matthew 11:28-29:** "Come unto me, all ye that labour and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest. Take my yoke upon you, and learn of me; for I am meek and lowly in heart: and ye shall find rest unto your souls."

> **Hebrews 7:25:** "Wherefore he is able also to save them to the uttermost that come unto God by him, seeing he ever liveth to make intercession for them."
