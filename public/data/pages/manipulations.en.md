Every translation involves choices, and honest translators sometimes disagree. But some choices are not about style. They change what the Hebrew or Greek actually says, and they always lean in the same direction: toward a doctrine the translator's church already holds. This page compares those passages side by side with the original-language words and with translations that render them faithfully — the **KJV** in English and the **RVG** in Spanish — so you can judge for yourself.

Two things to keep in mind:

- **Translation choice vs. textual variant.** Sometimes a Bible differs from the KJV because it follows a different Greek manuscript tradition (the Critical Text), not because the translator bent the wording. Where that is the case, this page says so; and I also made it a priority to point those differences out in the Study Notes of this app. A manipulation is when the same Hebrew or Greek word is rendered in a way the word does not mean.
- **Quoted texts.** In order to avoid Copyright infringements, I try to stick to citations from the translations in this app: Torres Amat, Douay-Rheims (Challoner), Straubinger, KJV, RVG and LSV; the New World Translation quotations are from the current edition on jw.org. Where a Bible is not in the app, its manipulated verses are listed in the table at the end.

# 1. "Do penance" instead of "Repent"

The Greek verb **_metanoeō_** means "to change one's mind," "to repent" — a turning of the heart and mind. In the Old Testament the usual words are **_shuv_** ("to turn back") and **_nacham_** ("to be sorry, to relent"). None of them means performing acts of satisfaction for sin.

The Latin Vulgate rendered _metanoeite_ as _paenitentiam agite_ — literally "do penance." Already in 1516 the Catholic scholar **Erasmus** pointed out that the Greek means a change of mind, not a penitential act, and Luther built the first of his 95 Theses on the same point. Yet the Catholic Bibles translated from the Latin kept "do penance," because it supports the sacrament of Penance: confession to a priest followed by works of satisfaction.

> **KJV — Matthew 3:2:** "Repent ye: for the kingdom of heaven is at hand."
> **Douay-Rheims:** "**Do penance**: for the kingdom of heaven is at hand."
> **Torres Amat:** "**Haced penitencia**: porque está cerca el reino de los cielos."
> **RVG:** "Arrepentíos, porque el reino del cielo se ha acercado."

The most serious case is Peter's answer on the day of Pentecost, the passage that tells sinners how to be saved:

> **KJV — Acts 2:38:** "**Repent**, and be baptized every one of you in the name of Jesus Christ for the remission of sins…"
> **Douay-Rheims:** "**Do penance**: and be baptized every one of you in the name of Jesus Christ…"
> **Torres Amat:** "**Haced penitencia**, y sea bautizado cada uno de vosotros en el nombre de Jesucristo…"

The pattern runs through the whole Bible, and in the Old Testament it even replaces words that have nothing to do with repentance:

> **Job 42:10 — KJV:** "And the LORD **turned the captivity of Job**, when he prayed for his friends."
> **Douay-Rheims:** "The Lord also was turned at **the penance of Job**, when he prayed for his friends."

> **Job 21:2 — KJV:** "Hear diligently my speech, and **let this be your consolations**."
> **Douay-Rheims:** "Hear, I beseech you, my words, and **do penance**."

> **Leviticus 5:5 — KJV:** "…he shall **confess** that he hath sinned in that thing."
> **Douay-Rheims:** "Let him **do penance** for his sin." · **Torres Amat:** "**haga penitencia** por el pecado."

> **2 Chronicles 33:12 — KJV:** "…and **humbled himself greatly** before the God of his fathers."
> **Douay-Rheims:** "…and **did penance exceedingly** before the God of his fathers."

> **Job 24:23 — KJV:** "Though it be given him **to be in safety**, whereon he resteth…"
> **Douay-Rheims:** "God hath given him **place for penance**…" · **Torres Amat:** "Dale Dios **lugar de penitencia**…"

Repentance is a gift of God received by faith (Acts 11:18); "penance" turns it into a work the sinner performs. The difference is the whole Gospel.

# 2. "Priest" instead of "Elder"

The New Testament has two different words. **_Hiereus_** means "priest" — a man who offers sacrifices, like the Levitical priests. **_Presbyteros_** means "elder" — an older, mature man who oversees a congregation. The New Testament **never** calls a church leader a _hiereus_; the only priests in the church are **all believers** (1 Peter 2:5, 9; Revelation 1:6), and the only High Priest is Christ (Hebrews 4:14). Translating _presbyteros_ as "priest" plants the Catholic priesthood in texts that describe elders.

> **KJV — Acts 14:23:** "And when they had ordained them **elders** in every church…"
> **Douay-Rheims:** "And when they had ordained to them **priests** in every church…"
> **Torres Amat:** "habiendo ordenado **sacerdotes** en cada una de las iglesias…"
> **RVG:** "Y cuando les ordenaron **ancianos** en cada iglesia…"

> **KJV — James 5:14:** "Is any sick among you? let him call for the **elders** of the church…"
> **Douay-Rheims:** "Let him bring in the **priests** of the church…"

> **KJV — Titus 1:5:** "…and ordain **elders** in every city, as I had appointed thee."
> **Douay-Rheims:** "…and shouldest ordain **priests** in every city…"

The same happens in 1 Timothy 5:17 ("Let the **priests** that rule well…"). Ironically, the Torres Amat usually writes "presbíteros" — the Greek word in Spanish letters — but in Acts 14:23 it writes "sacerdotes."

# 3. YHWH, the only Rock

One of the great titles of God in the Old Testament is **_tsur_**, "**Rock**" — the God who does not move, the only refuge and our **ONLY ROCK**. "He is the Rock, his work is perfect" (Deuteronomy 32:4). The title matters for the identity of Jesus: Paul says of Israel in the wilderness, "they drank of that spiritual Rock that followed them: **and that Rock was Christ**" (1 Corinthians 10:4). If YHWH alone is the Rock, and the Rock was Christ, then Jesus is YHWH.

In Catholicism, **the Rock** is Peter, and coincidentally, the Latin Vulgate avoids using **Rock** for YHWH and wrote _Deus_ ("God") or _fortis_ ("strong"). The Catholic Bibles made from the Latin followed it, and the Rock disappears from the very passages that say there is **no other Rock**:

> **Deuteronomy 32:4 — KJV:** "**He is the Rock**, his work is perfect."
> **Douay-Rheims:** "**The works of God** are perfect." · **Torres Amat:** "Perfectas son todas **las obras de Dios**."

> **Deuteronomy 32:31 — KJV:** "For **their rock is not as our Rock**, even our enemies themselves being judges."
> **Douay-Rheims:** "For **our God is not as their gods**." · **Torres Amat:** "Porque no es **nuestro Dios como los dioses de ellos**."

> **1 Samuel 2:2 — KJV:** "…neither is there **any rock like our God**."
> **Douay-Rheims:** "…there is **none strong** like our God." · **Torres Amat:** "**ninguno es fuerte** como nuestro Dios."

> **2 Samuel 22:32 — KJV:** "For who is God, save the LORD? and **who is a rock**, save our God?"
> **Douay-Rheims:** "…and **who is strong** but our God?" · **Torres Amat:** "¿Y **quién es fuerte** sino nuestro Dios?"

> **Psalm 18:2 — KJV:** "The LORD is **my rock**, and my fortress, and my deliverer."
> **Douay-Rheims (Ps 17:3):** "The Lord is **my firmament**…" · **Torres Amat:** "El Señor es **mi firme apoyo**…"

> **Psalm 62:2 — KJV:** "**He ONLY is my rock** and my salvation."
> **Douay-Rheims (Ps 61:3):** "For **he is my God** and my saviour." · **Torres Amat:** "**Él es mi Dios** y mi salvador."

The same happens in Deuteronomy 32:15 and 32:18, Psalm 18:31, Psalm 62:6 and Isaiah 17:10. Modern dynamic translations do something similar for readability: the Good News Translation turns "Rock" into "defender" or "protector," and the Biblia de América renders it "apoyo" in Isaiah 44:8.

**A note of honesty on Isaiah 44:8.** While Catholic Bibles used to completely remove YHWH **our Rock** from the entire Old Testament, there are a couple of places where even the KJV and the RVG translate _tsur_ as "God": "Is there a God beside me? yea, **there is no God**; I know not any" (KJV); "No hay otro Dios" (RVG). The literal reading is kept by the LSV ("I have not known another **Rock**") and, notably, by the Catholic **Straubinger** ("¿O hay acaso (otra) **Roca**? No la conozco"). Straubinger, translating from the Hebrew in the 1950's (more than 400 years since honest Protestant Bibles were published in vernacular languages), finally restores "Él es la **Roca**" in Deuteronomy 32:4 — proof that the problem is the Latin, not the Hebrew.

# 4. Mary: "she shall crush," "full of grace," and the perpetual virginity

### Genesis 3:15 — "he," not "she"

The first promise of the Redeemer says the woman's **seed** will crush the serpent's head. The Hebrew pronoun is **_hu'_** — masculine, "he" (the Seed). Jerome's Vulgate read _ipsa_, "she," and so the verse became a text about Mary crushing the serpent — the image behind countless statues of Mary standing on a snake.

> **RVG — Genesis 3:15:** "…entre tu simiente y su simiente; **Él** te herirá en la cabeza, y tú le herirás en el calcañar."
> **Douay-Rheims:** "…**she** shall crush thy head, and thou shalt lie in wait for **her** heel."
> **Torres Amat:** "…**ella** quebrantará tu cabeza, y tú andarás acechando a **su** calcañar."

The KJV reads "**it** shall bruise thy head," referring to the seed, and the Catholic Church's own official Latin revision, the _Nova Vulgata_ (1979), corrected _ipsa_ to _ipsum_. Some modern Catholic translations (NABRE) make the seed plural — "**they** will strike at your head" — which hides the single Messiah the verse promises in order to include Mary.

### Luke 1:28 — "full of grace" or "highly favoured"?

The angel calls Mary **_kecharitōmenē_**, a passive participle of _charitoō_, "to grace, to favour": "you who have been favoured." The same verb appears in only one other place, Ephesians 1:6, where it describes **every believer**: "he hath made us accepted (_echaritōsen_) in the beloved." No one translates Ephesians 1:6 as "full of grace," yet the Vulgate's _gratia plena_ ("full of grace") in Luke 1:28 became the foundation of the Hail Mary and of the doctrine that Mary is free of sin, since a person **full of grace** cannot have sin.

> **KJV:** "Hail, thou that art **highly favoured**, the Lord is with thee."
> **RVG:** "¡Salve, **muy favorecida**! El Señor es contigo."
> **Douay-Rheims:** "Hail, **full of grace**, the Lord is with thee." · **Torres Amat:** "Dios te salve, **llena de gracia**…"

**"But it's a perfect passive participle."** Catholic apologists often argue that the grammar itself proves Mary's sinlessness: _kecharitōmenē_ is a **perfect passive participle**, which describes an action completed in the past whose result continues — so, they say, Mary was "graced" fully and permanently from the first moment of her existence, which they take as the Immaculate Conception. But the Greek Bible itself shows that the form proves no such thing. The same verb, in the **same tense, voice and mood** — perfect passive participle — appears in the Septuagint in **Sirach 18:17** (a book the Catholic Church accepts as Scripture), describing an ordinary person:

> **Sirach 18:17 (Greek):** "οὐκ ἰδοὺ λόγος ὑπὲρ δόμα ἀγαθόν; καὶ ἀμφότερα παρὰ ἀνδρὶ **κεχαριτωμένῳ**."
> **KJV (Apocrypha):** "Lo, is not a word better than a gift? but both are with **a gracious man**."
> **RSV-CE (Catholic):** "Indeed, does not a word surpass a good gift? Both are to be found in **a gracious man**."
> **Latin Vulgate:** "…sed utraque cum homine **justificato**" ("…with a justified man").

_Kecharitōmenē_ (Luke 1:28) and _kecharitōmenō_ (Sirach 18:17) are the same word in the same form; they differ only in gender and case (feminine vocative for Mary, masculine dative for "a man"), as the grammar of each sentence requires. In Sirach it simply means a gracious man — someone kind and pleasing in his speech — and no one, not even the Catholic RSV-CE, claims that this man was conceived without sin or is "full of grace." Even the Vulgate translates it there as _justificato_, not _gratia plena_. If the perfect passive participle does not make the man of Sirach 18:17 sinless, it does not make Mary sinless either. The perfect tense simply says that God's favour had been given to her and remained on her — "thou hast found favour with God" (Luke 1:30).

Jesus is **full of grace** (πλήρης χάριτος) in John 1:14.
Acts 6:8 has a variant, so the Critical Text says that Stephen is also **full of grace** (πλήρης χάριτος). Is he also sinless?
Mary is not **full of gace** (πλήρης χάριτος), she was **highly favored** or **endued with grace** (κεχαριτωμένη).
Notice how **πλήρης χάριτος** is different from _κεχαριτωμένη_.

Even the RSV-2CE (2006), the Catholic edition of a modern Protestant translation, changed the RSV's "O favored one" back to "full of grace" and in the Ignatius Catholic Study Bible Study Note on Luke 1:28, it explains: "The expression _full of grace_ is rooted in Catholic tradition and traced to St. Jerome's tranlation of this verse in the Latin Vulgate. Although fundamentally accurate, it lacks some of the depth of the Greek original." They even admit that they translate Scripture with **tradition** rather than Greek grammar.

American Protetant pressure and information access has forced Catholicism to be more honest in their translations. Their Bibles improved significantly after Vatican II (1965). In 1966, the **Jerusalem Bible (en)** was first published, and it accurately translated Luke 1:28.

### Luke 1:28:

> **Jerusalem Bible (1966):** “Rejoice, so **highly favoured!** The Lord is with you.”

And then in its first update:

> **New Jerusalem Bible (1985):** "Rejoice, **you who enjoy God's favour!** The Lord is with you."

But then, they just got tired of honesty:

> **Revised New Jerusalem Bible (2019):** "Rejoice, **full of grace!** The Lord is with you.'"

The Spanish Biblia Jerusalén (1997) didn't even try:

> **Biblia Jerusalén (1997):** "«Alégrate, **llena de gracia** (full of grace), el Señor está contigo.»"

### Luke 1:34 — an added vow of virginity

> **KJV:** "How shall this be, seeing **I know not a man**?"
> **Torres Amat:** "¿Cómo ha de ser eso? Pues yo **no conozco ni jamás conoceré** varón alguno."

The words "ni jamás conoceré" ("nor will I ever know") are not in the Greek. They put a lifelong vow of virginity into Mary's mouth.

### Matthew 1:25 — "till"

> **KJV:** "And knew her not **till** she had brought forth her firstborn son."
> **RVG:** "Y no la conoció **hasta que** dio a luz a su hijo primogénito."
> **Jerusalem Bible** "and, **though he had not had intercourse with her**, she gave birth to a son;"
> **Torres Amat:** "Y, **sin haberla conocido** o tocado, dio a luz su hijo primogénito."
> **Straubinger:** "Y **sin que la conociera**, dio ella a luz un hijo."

The Greek **_heōs hou_** means "until." Removing "until" removes the natural implication that Joseph and Mary lived as husband and wife afterwards. (The omission of "firstborn" in Straubinger and other modern Bibles comes from the Critical Text, not from the translator.)

### "His brothers" become "relatives" and "cousins"

The Gospels speak openly of Jesus' **_adelphoi_** (brothers) and **_adelphai_** (sisters), and even name them: James, Joses, Simon and Judas (Matthew 13:55). Greek has a word for cousin — **_anepsios_** (Colossians 4:10) — and it is never used for them.

> **KJV — Matthew 13:55-56:** "…and **his brethren**, James, and Joses, and Simon, and Judas? And **his sisters**, are they not all with us?"
> **Torres Amat:** "¿No son sus **parientes** Santiago, José, Simón y Judas? Y sus **primas hermanas**, ¿no viven todas entre nosotros?"

> **KJV — Mark 6:3:** "Is not this the carpenter, the son of Mary, **the brother of** James…? and are not **his sisters** here with us?"
> **Torres Amat:** "¿No es Éste aquel artesano, hijo de María, **primo hermano** de Santiago…? ¿Y sus **primas hermanas** no moran aquí…?"

> **KJV — Galatians 1:19:** "But other of the apostles saw I none, save James **the Lord's brother**."
> **Torres Amat:** "…sino a Santiago, el **primo hermano** del Señor."

The Torres Amat does the same in Matthew 12:46, Mark 3:31, Luke 8:19, John 2:12, John 7:3 and Acts 1:14. (Mark 3:21 is sometimes added to this list, but there the Greek _hoi par' autou_ — "those belonging to him" — is genuinely ambiguous, and the KJV itself reads "his friends." What the RSV-2CE did was change the RSV's "his family" to "his friends.")

### Luke 11:27-28 — "Yea rather"

A woman blesses the womb that bore Jesus. He answers with **_menoun_**, a word that corrects: "**Yea rather**, blessed are they that hear the word of God, and keep it" (KJV; LSB "**On the contrary**"; RVG "**Antes** bienaventurados…"). Some Catholic Bibles turn the correction into comparative wording: "**still happier**" (Jerusalem Bible), "**more blessed still**" (New Jerusalem Bible). Other modern Catholic Bibles do correctly translate the corrective nature of the Greek: "**Rather, blessed are those**" (NABRE), "**Blessed rather are those**" (RSV-2CE).

### Matthew 15:23 — inventing intercession

> **KJV:** "And his disciples came and besought him, saying, **Send her away**; for she crieth after us."
> **Torres Amat:** "Y sus discípulos, acercándose, **intercedían**, diciéndole: **Concédele lo que pide**, a fin de que se vaya."

The Greek **_apolyson autēn_** means "send her away, dismiss her." The disciples wanted her gone; the Torres Amat turns them into intercessors.

# 5. Peter's wife

> **KJV — 1 Corinthians 9:5:** "Have we not power to lead about **a sister, a wife**, as well as other apostles, and as the brethren of the Lord, and Cephas?"
> **RVG:** "…traer con nosotros **una hermana, una esposa**…"
> **Douay-Rheims:** "…to carry about **a woman, a sister**…"
> **Torres Amat:** "…llevar en los viajes **alguna mujer hermana en Jesucristo, para que nos asista**…"

The Greek **_adelphēn gynaika_** means "a sister (fellow believer) as a wife." Paul is defending the apostles' right to bring their wives on their journeys, "as… **Cephas**" (Peter) did. The Gospels confirm Peter was married: Jesus healed "**his wife's mother**" (Matthew 8:14). Catholic renderings such as "alguna mujer hermana," "a Christian woman" or "una mujer cristiana" turn the wife into a female helper, which protects the claim that Peter — the first "pope" — and the apostles lived as celibates.

# 6. Other doctrinal renderings

- **Matthew 6:7.** Jesus forbids **_battalogeō_**, babbling the same words over and over: "use not **vain repetitions**" (KJV); "no uséis **vanas repeticiones**" (RVG). The Douay-Rheims has "**speak not much**," the Torres Amat "no afectéis **hablar mucho**," and the Biblia de Jerusalén (5.ª ed.) "no charléis mucho" — none of which touches repeated prayers such as the rosary.
- **Ephesians 5:32.** Paul calls marriage a "great **mystery**" (_mystērion_), referring to Christ and the church. The Vulgate's _sacramentum_ produced "a great **sacrament**" (Douay-Rheims) and "Sacramento es éste grande" (Torres Amat), a proof text for marriage as one of the seven sacraments. Straubinger, from the Greek, restores "Este **misterio** es grande."
- **Acts 9:31.** The KJV and RVG read "the **churches** had rest"; Catholic Bibles read "the **Church**," a single body under one government. To be fair, this one is a **textual variant**: the Critical Text also has the singular. It is still listed because it is used to support the idea of one universal visible Church.
- **John 1:18.** Some Catholic Bibles write "Dios Hijo" ("God the Son"). The Critical Text reads _monogenēs theos_ ("only begotten God") where the Textus Receptus reads "the only begotten **Son**"; a phrase like "God the Son," however, belongs to later Trinitarian theology, not to the text.

# 7. The New World Translation (Jehovah's Witnesses)

The New World Translation (NWT) was produced by the Watch Tower Society to fit a doctrine that denies that Jesus is God. Its alterations follow four patterns.

### "A god"

> **KJV — John 1:1:** "In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and **the Word was God**."
> **NWT:** "…and the Word was **a god**."

The NWT does not translate _theos_ without the article as "a god" elsewhere in the same chapter — in John 1:6, 1:12, 1:13 and the first half of 1:18 the very same construction is rendered "God" — but it does so here, making Jesus a lesser, second god. It renders John 1:18 "the only-begotten **god**" and Hebrews 1:8 "**God is your throne** forever," instead of "**Thy throne, O God**, is for ever and ever" (KJV), where the Father calls the Son God.

### "Jehovah" inserted into the New Testament

No Greek manuscript of the New Testament contains the name YHWH. Yet the NWT inserts "Jehovah" more than two hundred times where the Greek says _Kyrios_ ("Lord"), and it does so selectively — exactly where the "Lord" would otherwise be Jesus:

> **Romans 10:9 — NWT:** "…publicly declare with your mouth that **Jesus is Lord**…"
> **Romans 10:13 — NWT:** "For 'everyone who calls on the name of **Jehovah** will be saved.'"
> **KJV — Romans 10:13:** "For whosoever shall call upon the name of **the Lord** shall be saved."

In Paul's argument the "Lord" of verse 13 is the "Lord Jesus" of verse 9. By changing one and not the other, the NWT breaks the link — Paul applies Joel's "call on the name of YHWH" to Jesus. The same insertion appears in James 5:14 ("applying oil to him in the name of **Jehovah**"), where the apostles anointed and healed in the name of Jesus (Acts 3:6; 4:10), and in Colossians 3:23, 2 Peter 3:9-12 and Revelation 4:11, among others.

### "Other" added to make Jesus a creature

> **KJV — Colossians 1:16-17:** "For by him were **all things** created… And he is before **all things**, and by him **all things** consist."
> **NWT:** "…by means of him **all other things** were created… he is before **all other things**, and by means of him **all other things** were made to exist."

The word "other" (Greek _allos_ or _loipos_) is not in the text. It is added four times to make Jesus the first thing God created rather than the Creator of all things. The NWT adds it in verse 20 as well.

> **KJV — Philippians 2:9:** "…and given him **a name which is above every name**."
> **NWT:** "…and kindly gave him **the name that is above every other name**."

Here "other" is added so that "every name" will not include the name Jehovah. In the Greek, the name above **every** name is given to Jesus, and every knee bows "at the name of Jesus" (v. 10) — the very honour that Isaiah 45:23 reserves for YHWH.

### "In union with" instead of "in"

The NWT systematically renders the preposition **_en_** ("in") as "**in union with**" whenever it speaks of Christ in the believer, the believer in Christ, or the Father in the Son:

> **KJV — John 14:10:** "Believest thou not that **I am in the Father, and the Father in me**?"
> **NWT:** "Do you not believe that **I am in union with the Father** and the Father is **in union with me**?"

> **KJV — Galatians 2:20:** "…nevertheless I live; yet not I, but **Christ liveth in me**."
> **NWT:** "…it is Christ who is living **in union with me**."

> **KJV — Colossians 1:27:** "…which is **Christ in you**, the hope of glory."
> **NWT:** "…which is **Christ in union with you**, the hope of his glory."

"In union with" describes two separate parties in agreement. The Greek describes indwelling: the Father dwelling in the Son (John 14:10-11), and Christ dwelling in the believer by the Spirit (Romans 8:9-10). The change removes the Father's presence in Jesus and Christ's presence in the believer from dozens of verses (John 15:4-7; 17:21-26; Romans 8:1-2; 2 Corinthians 5:17; Ephesians 1-3, and the rest of the table).

### Worship becomes "obeisance"

The verb **_proskyneō_** means "to worship, to bow down in worship." The NWT translates it "worship" when it is directed to God (Matthew 4:10, "It is Jehovah your God you must worship") but "**do obeisance**" when it is directed to Jesus:

> **KJV — Matthew 28:9:** "And they came and held him by the feet, and **worshipped him**."
> **NWT:** "…took hold of his feet and **did obeisance to him**."

The same is done in Matthew 2:11, 14:33 and 28:17, Luke 24:52, John 9:38 and Hebrews 1:6 ("let all of God's angels **do obeisance** to him").

### Other changes

- **John 8:58.** "Before Abraham was, **I AM**" (KJV) — Jesus takes the name God revealed to Moses (Exodus 3:14), and the Jews pick up stones. The NWT reads "before Abraham came into existence, **I have been**."
- **Luke 23:43.** "**To day** shalt thou be with me in paradise" (KJV). The NWT moves the comma: "Truly I tell you **today**, you will be with me in Paradise," to fit its teaching that the dead are unconscious.
- **2 Peter 1:1.** "…the righteousness of **God and our Saviour Jesus Christ**" — one person, by the Greek construction with a single article (the same construction as "our Lord and Saviour Jesus Christ" in 2 Peter 1:11). The NWT reads "**our God and the Savior** Jesus Christ," inserting "the" to make two persons.

# Conclusion

None of these renderings is required by the Hebrew or the Greek. Each of them protects a doctrine that the original text does not teach: penance and the priesthood, Mary's sinlessness and perpetual virginity, a celibate papacy — or, in the New World Translation, a Jesus who is a created god, worshipped with "obeisance," who lives merely "in union with" believers and whose name is below Jehovah's. A faithful translation lets the text say what it says: that YHWH alone is the Rock, and that Rock was Christ; that the name above every name belongs to Jesus; and that Christ lives **in** you, the hope of glory.
