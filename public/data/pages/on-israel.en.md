Did God cast away the Jewish people when most of them did not receive Jesus, and is the Church now "the new Israel" that inherited their promises? That idea, called **replacement theology** or **supersessionism**, became the official view of the imperial Church, and for 1,600 years it was used to justify contempt, laws and violence against the Jews. This page lets Paul answer the question, traces how the Jewish roots were cut out of Christianity, documents what the institutional Church did to the Jews, looks at what prophecy says about Israel's future, and ends with what it means, and does not mean, for a Christian to bless Israel.

# 1. Salvation is of the Jews

Christianity did not begin as a Gentile religion that later tolerated Jews. Jesus was a Jew, born under the Law, descended from Abraham and David; He kept the feasts and went to the synagogue on the sabbath "as his custom was." His apostles were Jews, and the first church was entirely Jewish: thousands in Jerusalem believed and were "all zealous of the law." Paul, the apostle to the Gentiles, still went to the synagogue first "as his manner was," hurried to be in Jerusalem for Pentecost, and called himself "an Israelite, of the seed of Abraham."

> **Matthew 1:1:** "The book of the generation of Jesus Christ, the son of David, the son of Abraham."

> **Galatians 4:4:** "But when the fulness of the time was come, God sent forth his Son, made of a woman, made under the law,"

> **John 4:22:** "Ye worship ye know not what: we know what we worship: for salvation is of the Jews."

> **Romans 9:4-5:** "Who are Israelites; to whom pertaineth the adoption, and the glory, and the covenants, and the giving of the law, and the service of God, and the promises; Whose are the fathers, and of whom as concerning the flesh Christ came, who is over all, God blessed for ever. Amen."

> **Romans 15:8:** "Now I say that Jesus Christ was a minister of the circumcision for the truth of God, to confirm the promises made unto the fathers:"

> **Luke 4:16:** "And he came to Nazareth, where he had been brought up: and, as his custom was, he went into the synagogue on the sabbath day, and stood up for to read."

> **Acts 21:20:** "And when they heard it, they glorified the Lord, and said unto him, Thou seest, brother, how many thousands of Jews there are which believe; and they are all zealous of the law:"

> **Acts 17:2:** "And Paul, as his manner was, went in unto them, and three sabbath days reasoned with them out of the scriptures,"

> **Acts 20:16:** "For Paul had determined to sail by Ephesus, because he would not spend the time in Asia: for he hasted, if it were possible for him, to be at Jerusalem the day of Pentecost."

> **Romans 11:1:** "I say then, Hath God cast away his people? God forbid. For I also am an Israelite, of the seed of Abraham, of the tribe of Benjamin."

# 2. Paul's answer: God has not cast away His people

In Romans 9–11 Paul asks the very question this page is about. His answer is **"God forbid."** Unbelief has made Israel stumble, but not fall forever; their stumbling opened the door to the Gentiles, and their restoration will be "life from the dead."

> **Romans 11:1-2:** "I say then, Hath God cast away his people? God forbid. For I also am an Israelite, of the seed of Abraham, of the tribe of Benjamin. God hath not cast away his people which he foreknew. Wot ye not what the scripture saith of Elias? how he maketh intercession to God against Israel, saying,"

> **Romans 11:11-12:** "I say then, Have they stumbled that they should fall? God forbid: but rather through their fall salvation is come unto the Gentiles, for to provoke them to jealousy. Now if the fall of them be the riches of the world, and the diminishing of them the riches of the Gentiles; how much more their fulness?"

> **Romans 11:15:** "For if the casting away of them be the reconciling of the world, what shall the receiving of them be, but life from the dead?"

## Grafted in, not replacing

Paul's picture is one olive tree. The root is Israel's patriarchs and promises. Some natural branches were broken off for unbelief; Gentile believers, "a wild olive tree," were grafted in **among** the remaining branches and now share the root. The Gentile does not become the tree, and he does not carry the root; the root carries him. Paul warns Gentiles against exactly the pride that replacement theology later became: "Boast not against the branches."

> **Romans 11:16-18:** "For if the firstfruit be holy, the lump is also holy: and if the root be holy, so are the branches. And if some of the branches be broken off, and thou, being a wild olive tree, wert graffed in among them, and with them partakest of the root and fatness of the olive tree; Boast not against the branches. But if thou boast, thou bearest not the root, but the root thee."

> **Romans 11:19-21:** "Thou wilt say then, The branches were broken off, that I might be graffed in. Well; because of unbelief they were broken off, and thou standest by faith. Be not highminded, but fear: For if God spared not the natural branches, take heed lest he also spare not thee."

> **Romans 11:22-24:** "Behold therefore the goodness and severity of God: on them which fell, severity; but toward thee, goodness, if thou continue in his goodness: otherwise thou also shalt be cut off. And they also, if they abide not still in unbelief, shall be graffed in: for God is able to graff them in again. For if thou wert cut out of the olive tree which is wild by nature, and wert graffed contrary to nature into a good olive tree: how much more shall these, which be the natural branches, be graffed into their own olive tree?"

## "And so all Israel shall be saved"

Israel's partial blindness lasts "until the fulness of the Gentiles be come in"; then "all Israel shall be saved," because God's gifts and calling are irrevocable. In the same breath Paul says they are "enemies" concerning the gospel and "beloved" for the fathers' sakes, so "Israel" here is the Jewish people, not the Church.

> **Romans 11:25-27:** "For I would not, brethren, that ye should be ignorant of this mystery, lest ye should be wise in your own conceits; that blindness in part is happened to Israel, until the fulness of the Gentiles be come in. And so all Israel shall be saved: as it is written, There shall come out of Sion the Deliverer, and shall turn away ungodliness from Jacob: For this is my covenant unto them, when I shall take away their sins."

> **Romans 11:28-29:** "As concerning the gospel, they are enemies for your sakes: but as touching the election, they are beloved for the fathers’ sakes. For the gifts and calling of God are without repentance."

## Paul's own heart

Paul did not speak of the Jews as rejected enemies. He wished himself accursed for their sake, prayed for their salvation, and said Gentile believers owe them a debt.

> **Romans 9:2-3:** "That I have great heaviness and continual sorrow in my heart. For I could wish that myself were accursed from Christ for my brethren, my kinsmen according to the flesh:"

> **Romans 10:1:** "Brethren, my heart’s desire and prayer to God for Israel is, that they might be saved."

> **Romans 15:27:** "It hath pleased them verily; and their debtors they are. For if the Gentiles have been made partakers of their spiritual things, their duty is also to minister unto them in carnal things."

## Gentiles are brought near, into Israel's hope

Ephesians says the same thing from the Gentile side. Gentiles were once "aliens from the commonwealth of Israel" and "strangers from the covenants of promise"; in Christ they are "no more strangers," but "fellowcitizens" and "fellowheirs." They are brought into Israel's promises; Israel is not written out of them. Jew and Gentile together become "one new man" (see **Key Oneness verses**).

> **Ephesians 2:11-13:** "Wherefore remember, that ye being in time past Gentiles in the flesh, who are called Uncircumcision by that which is called the Circumcision in the flesh made by hands; That at that time ye were without Christ, being aliens from the commonwealth of Israel, and strangers from the covenants of promise, having no hope, and without God in the world: But now in Christ Jesus ye who sometimes were far off are made nigh by the blood of Christ."

> **Ephesians 2:19:** "Now therefore ye are no more strangers and foreigners, but fellowcitizens with the saints, and of the household of God;"

> **Ephesians 3:6:** "That the Gentiles should be fellowheirs, and of the same body, and partakers of his promise in Christ by the gospel:"

## What about "the Israel of God" and "a Jew inwardly"?

Replacement theology leans on a few verses:

- **Romans 2:28-29**, "he is a Jew, which is one inwardly": Paul is speaking to Jews about the heart; true covenant life is inward. In the very next verse he asks, "What advantage then hath the Jew?" and answers, "Much every way" (Romans 3:1-2).
- **Galatians 3:7, 29**, believers are "the children of Abraham" and "Abraham's seed": Gentiles become Abraham's children by faith, as God promised ("in thee shall all nations be blessed," Galatians 3:8). Sharing Abraham as father does not cancel God's word to Abraham's physical descendants, whom Paul still calls "my kinsmen according to the flesh" (Romans 9:3).
- **Galatians 6:16**, "the Israel of God": it can mean the whole Church, but it can also mean Jewish believers within it, since Paul blesses "them" **and** "the Israel of God." It is too uncertain to overturn three chapters of Romans written to answer this exact question.

> **Romans 3:1-2:** "What advantage then hath the Jew? or what profit is there of circumcision? Much every way: chiefly, because that unto them were committed the oracles of God."

> **Galatians 3:8:** "And the scripture, foreseeing that God would justify the heathen through faith, preached before the gospel unto Abraham, saying, In thee shall all nations be blessed."

> **Galatians 6:16:** "And as many as walk according to this rule, peace be on them, and mercy, and upon the Israel of God."

## The prophets agree

God tied Israel's survival as a nation to the sun, the moon and the stars. He promised to scatter them and to bring them back.

> **Jeremiah 31:35-37:** "Thus saith the LORD, which giveth the sun for a light by day, and the ordinances of the moon and of the stars for a light by night, which divideth the sea when the waves thereof roar; The LORD of hosts is his name: If those ordinances depart from before me, saith the LORD, then the seed of Israel also shall cease from being a nation before me for ever. Thus saith the LORD; If heaven above can be measured, and the foundations of the earth searched out beneath, I will also cast off all the seed of Israel for all that they have done, saith the LORD."

> **Ezekiel 36:24-28:** "For I will take you from among the heathen, and gather you out of all countries, and will bring you into your own land. Then will I sprinkle clean water upon you, and ye shall be clean: from all your filthiness, and from all your idols, will I cleanse you. A new heart also will I give you, and a new spirit will I put within you: and I will take away the stony heart out of your flesh, and I will give you an heart of flesh. And I will put my spirit within you, and cause you to walk in my statutes, and ye shall keep my judgments, and do them. And ye shall dwell in the land that I gave to your fathers; and ye shall be my people, and I will be your God."

> **Hosea 3:4-5:** "For the children of Israel shall abide many days without a king, and without a prince, and without a sacrifice, and without an image, and without an ephod, and without teraphim: Afterward shall the children of Israel return, and seek the LORD their God, and David their king; and shall fear the LORD and his goodness in the latter days."

> **Amos 9:14-15:** "And I will bring again the captivity of my people of Israel, and they shall build the waste cities, and inhabit them; and they shall plant vineyards, and drink the wine thereof; they shall also make gardens, and eat the fruit of them. And I will plant them upon their land, and they shall no more be pulled up out of their land which I have given them, saith the LORD thy God."

# 3. How the Jewish roots were cut away

The New Testament Church was Jewish, and for a generation Gentiles were the newcomers (Acts 15). Within a century, writers began to describe the Church as the replacement of Israel. The Epistle of Barnabas (c. 130) said the covenant "is ours," for the Jews "lost it" (4.6-8); Justin Martyr (c. 160) wrote, "we are the true spiritual Israel" (_Dialogue with Trypho_ 11); Ignatius had already written, "It is absurd to profess Jesus Christ and to Judaize" (_To the Magnesians_ 10.3). Melito of Sardis (c. 170) made the first known charge of **deicide**: "God has been murdered… by an Israelite right hand" (_On the Passover_ 96). Once the Empire adopted Christianity in the 300s, these ideas became law.

## Passover

The apostolic churches of Asia kept the Christian Passover on the 14th of Nisan, the Jewish date. Polycrates of Ephesus wrote that the apostle John and Philip had kept it that way; when Bishop Victor of Rome (c. 190) tried to cut those churches off, Irenaeus rebuked him and recalled that Polycarp and Bishop Anicetus had agreed to differ (Eusebius, _Church History_ 5.24). The Council of Nicaea (325) fixed a separate date so that Easter would never follow the Jewish reckoning, and Emperor Constantine explained why in his letter to the churches:

> "It appeared an unworthy thing that in the celebration of this most holy feast we should follow the practice of the Jews, who have impiously defiled their hands with enormous sin… **Let us then have nothing in common with the detestable Jewish crowd**; for we have received from our Saviour a different way."
> — Constantine, letter after Nicaea (Eusebius, _Life of Constantine_ 3.18-19)

The Council of Antioch (341, canon 1) then excommunicated anyone who kept Pascha with the Jews. Yet Paul had written, "Christ our passover is sacrificed for us: therefore let us keep the feast" (1 Corinthians 5:7-8).

## The Sabbath and the feasts

Jesus and the apostles kept the sabbath and the feasts of Israel: Passover, Unleavened Bread, Pentecost, Tabernacles, even Dedication (Hanukkah). Believers met on the first day of the week as well (Acts 20:7; 1 Corinthians 16:2), and Paul told Gentiles not to let anyone judge them over holy days or sabbaths (Colossians 2:16-17). So the New Testament leaves these days to the believer's conscience (Romans 14:5-6).

The imperial Church did not leave them free; it **forbade** them. Constantine made Sunday the legal day of rest (AD 321, _Code of Justinian_ 3.12.2). The Council of Laodicea (c. 363) ruled: "Christians must not judaize by resting on the Sabbath, but must work on that day… if any shall be found to be judaizers, let them be anathema from Christ" (canon 29); it also forbade receiving festival gifts or unleavened bread from Jews (canons 37-38). The Council of Elvira (c. 306) had already forbidden Christians to eat with Jews or let a Jew bless their fields (canons 49-50). Jewish believers who kept the Law were pushed out: Jerome said of the Nazarenes, "while they wish to be both Jews and Christians, they are neither" (_Letter_ 112.13).

> **Luke 2:41-42:** "Now his parents went to Jerusalem every year at the feast of the passover. And when he was twelve years old, they went up to Jerusalem after the custom of the feast."

> **John 7:10:** "But when his brethren were gone up, then went he also up unto the feast, not openly, but as it were in secret."

> **John 10:22-23:** "And it was at Jerusalem the feast of the dedication, and it was winter. And Jesus walked in the temple in Solomon’s porch."

> **Acts 20:6:** "And we sailed away from Philippi after the days of unleavened bread, and came unto them to Troas in five days; where we abode seven days."

> **1 Corinthians 5:7-8:** "Purge out therefore the old leaven, that ye may be a new lump, as ye are unleavened. For even Christ our passover is sacrificed for us: Therefore let us keep the feast, not with old leaven, neither with the leaven of malice and wickedness; but with the unleavened bread of sincerity and truth."

> **Colossians 2:16-17:** "Let no man therefore judge you in meat, or in drink, or in respect of an holyday, or of the new moon, or of the sabbath days: Which are a shadow of things to come; but the body is of Christ."

> **Romans 14:5-6:** "One man esteemeth one day above another: another esteemeth every day alike. Let every man be fully persuaded in his own mind. He that regardeth the day, regardeth it unto the Lord; and he that regardeth not the day, to the Lord he doth not regard it. He that eateth, eateth to the Lord, for he giveth God thanks; and he that eateth not, to the Lord he eateth not, and giveth God thanks."

## The name of Jesus in baptism

The apostles, all Jews, baptized "in the name of Jesus Christ" (Acts 2:38; 8:16; 10:48; 19:5; see **Baptism**). The name _Yeshua_ means "YHWH saves" (see the lexicon at H3068). Later practice replaced the name with a formula of titles and the single burial with three immersions, and church law made both mandatory:

- **The _Apostolic Canons_** (Syria, late 300s), canon 49: "If any bishop or presbyter does not baptize according to the Lord's institution, into the Father, the Son and the Holy Ghost… let him be deposed." Canon 50: "If any bishop or presbyter does not perform the one initiation with three immersions, but with giving one immersion only, **into the death of the Lord**, let him be deposed." The baptism being outlawed is described in Paul's own words: one immersion "into his death" (Romans 6:3), that is, into Christ, not into three names.
- **Constantinople, canon 7** (attached to the council of 381; repeated by the Council in Trullo, 692, canon 95) rejects "Eunomians, who are baptized with only **one immersion**," and "**Sabellians, who teach the identity of Father and Son**," and orders that they be received "**as heathen**," that is, baptized again as if they had never been Christians. The same list receives the **Quartodecimans**, who kept Passover on the Jewish date, only after they anathematize their practice.
- **Pope Pelagius I** (c. 560) ruled on people who said they had been baptized "in the name of the Lord" only and required them to be baptized again in the threefold name (Denzinger 445).
- **Thomas Aquinas** (1270s) admitted that "in the primitive Church the apostles baptized in the name of Christ," but held that this was "by special revelation," and that such a baptism is no longer valid (_Summa Theologiae_ III q.66 a.6).

Put together, the imperial church outlawed baptism by one immersion into Christ's death, and refused to recognize the baptism of those who held that the Father and the Son are one. The canons do not record the words the "Sabellians" said at baptism, so this is an inference, but a strong one: those who believed that the one God is Father, Son and Spirit and that His name is Jesus are the ones who baptized "in the name of Jesus Christ," as the apostles did (see **Baptism**, section 5). The documents give doctrinal reasons, not hostility to the Jews; but the effect was the same as with Passover and the sabbath: the practice of the Jewish apostles was set aside, and those who kept it were treated as outsiders.

> **Acts 2:38:** "Then Peter said unto them, Repent, and be baptized every one of you in the name of Jesus Christ for the remission of sins, and ye shall receive the gift of the Holy Ghost."

> **Acts 8:16:** "(For as yet he was fallen upon none of them: only they were baptized in the name of the Lord Jesus.)"

# 4. The Empire's religion turns on the Jews

## Emperors and bishops

- **Constantine** called Judaism a "nefarious sect" (_feralis atque nefaria secta_) and punished conversion to it (_Theodosian Code_ 16.8.1, AD 315), and forbade Jews to own or circumcise Christian slaves (16.9.1, 336).
- **John Chrysostom**, the "Golden Mouth" of Antioch, preached eight sermons _Against the Jews_ (386-387): "The synagogue is not only a brothel and a theater; it also is a den of robbers and a lodging for wild beasts" (1.3.1); he said the Jews "are no better than hogs and goats" and, "although such beasts are unfit for work, they are fit for killing… this is what happened to the Jews" (1.2.5-6). He is honored as a saint and a Doctor of the Church.
- **Ambrose of Milan** (388): when Christians burned the synagogue at Callinicum and Emperor Theodosius ordered the bishop to rebuild it, Ambrose refused communion until the order was withdrawn, writing, "I declare that I set fire to the synagogue, or at least that I ordered those who did it" (_Letter_ 40.8).
- **Cyril of Alexandria** (414-415) expelled the Jews of Alexandria and let the mob take their property (Socrates Scholasticus, _Church History_ 7.13).
- **Augustine** taught that God kept the Jews alive, scattered and humbled, as "witnesses" to Christian truth ("Slay them not," Psalm 59:11; _City of God_ 18.46): a teaching that spared their lives but fixed them in a degraded place for a thousand years.
- **Imperial law** shut Jews out of public office and the army (_Novella_ 3 of Theodosius II, 438), and Justinian (553) told synagogues which translations they could read (_Novella_ 146).

# 5. What was done to the Jews

## Massacres and expulsions

- **1096, the First Crusade.** On the way to Jerusalem, crusaders under Count Emicho massacred the Jewish communities of the Rhineland: Speyer, Worms (about 800 dead) and Mainz (more than 1,000). Many Jews killed their own children rather than be forcibly baptized.
- **1190, York.** About 150 Jews died in Clifford's Tower, by their own hand or by the mob.
- **1242, Paris.** After a forced "disputation," 24 cartloads of the Talmud were burned at the order of Pope Gregory IX and King Louis IX.
- **1290, England** expelled every Jew (Edward I); **1306, France** (Philip IV); **1492, Spain** (the Alhambra Decree); **1497, Portugal**. The Spanish Inquisition (from 1478) burned Jewish converts suspected of keeping Jewish customs, and "purity of blood" statutes (Toledo, 1449) barred people of Jewish descent from office even after baptism.
- **1348-1349, the Black Death.** Jews were accused of poisoning wells. In Strasbourg, by the chroniclers' count, about 2,000 Jews were burned on February 14, 1349; hundreds of communities across the Empire were destroyed. Pope Clement VI issued bulls declaring them innocent, but they were widely ignored.

## The blood libel

Jews were falsely accused of killing Christian children for their blood: Norwich (1144), Blois (1171, about 30 Jews burned), Trent (1475, the boy "Simon of Trent" venerated as a martyr until 1965), La Guardia (1491, months before Spain's expulsion). Popes Innocent IV (1247) and Gregory X (1272) declared the charge false; it kept spreading anyway.

## The host desecration libel

This accusation grew out of a change in teaching about the Lord's Supper. Around 831 the monk **Paschasius Radbertus** wrote _On the Body and Blood of the Lord_, teaching that the bread becomes the very flesh of Christ "born of Mary"; the monk Ratramnus answered him, and the debate ran for centuries. Berengar of Tours, who denied a physical change, was made to swear in 1059 that Christ's body is "broken by the hands of the priests and ground by the teeth of the faithful." The Fourth Lateran Council (1215), the same council that ordered the Jewish badge, defined **transubstantiation**, and the feast of Corpus Christi followed (1264).

Once the wafer was believed to be Christ's literal body, Jews were accused of stealing consecrated hosts to torture Jesus again: stabbing them, burning them, making them bleed. The first known case was at Beelitz, near Berlin (1243); then Paris (1290). In 1298 a host-desecration charge at Röttingen set off the **Rintfleisch massacres**, in which bands led by a knight named Rintfleisch destroyed about 146 Jewish communities in Franconia and killed thousands. Others followed at Deggendorf (1338), Brussels (1370), Segovia (1410) and Berlin (1510), where 38 Jews were burned and the rest expelled from Brandenburg. Confessions were obtained by torture, and the "miraculous" hosts were then displayed for pilgrims.

## Luther

The Reformation did not end this. In 1543 Martin Luther wrote _On the Jews and Their Lies_, urging that their synagogues and schools be set on fire, their houses destroyed, their prayer books and Talmud taken away, their rabbis forbidden to teach on pain of death, and the Jews put to forced labor. In November 1938, days after Kristallnacht, the Lutheran bishop Martin Sasse published excerpts as _Martin Luther on the Jews: Away with Them!_; at Nuremberg, Julius Streicher said that Luther would be in the dock in his place had the book been taken into account.

## Repudiations

Many churches have since renounced this history: the Roman Catholic Church in _Nostra Aetate_ (1965), which rejected the charge that the Jews are collectively guilty of Christ's death and quoted Romans 11:28-29, and in _The Gifts and the Calling of God Are Irrevocable_ (2015); the Evangelical Lutheran Church in America in its 1994 declaration rejecting Luther's anti-Jewish writings. That repentance is right. Scripture never blamed "the Jews" as a people for the cross: Peter said they acted "through ignorance," Jesus prayed for His killers, and the apostles named Herod, Pilate, the Gentiles and the people of Israel together, while Jesus said, "No man taketh it from me, but I lay it down of myself."

> **Acts 3:17:** "And now, brethren, I wot that through ignorance ye did it, as did also your rulers."

> **Luke 23:34:** "Then said Jesus, Father, forgive them; for they know not what they do. And they parted his raiment, and cast lots."

> **Acts 4:27-28:** "For of a truth against thy holy child Jesus, whom thou hast anointed, both Herod, and Pontius Pilate, with the Gentiles, and the people of Israel, were gathered together, For to do whatsoever thy hand and thy counsel determined before to be done."

> **John 10:17-18:** "Therefore doth my Father love me, because I lay down my life, that I might take it again. No man taketh it from me, but I lay it down of myself. I have power to lay it down, and I have power to take it again. This commandment have I received of my Father."

# 6. Replacement theology today

The doctrine did not end with Rome. It passed into the Protestant churches and is still taught, most often in the classical Reformed and the older state and liturgical churches, which read the Church as the "true Israel" and the promises to Israel as fulfilled only spiritually. Some examples:

- **John Calvin**, on Romans 11:26: "I extend the word Israel to all the people of God," Jews and Gentiles together, which makes "all Israel shall be saved" a statement about the Church (_Commentary on Romans_, 1540). Much of later Reformed teaching followed him.
- **The "Open Letter to Evangelicals" (Knox Theological Seminary, 2002)**, signed by a number of Reformed teachers, said that the inheritance promises to Abraham apply not to an ethnic group but to the Church, "the true Israel," and that no ethnic or religious group's claim to the land "can be supported by Scripture."
- **The Church of Scotland's report _The Inheritance of Abraham?_ (2013)** argued that the land promises were not meant to apply to a territory or to the Jewish people today; after protests from Jewish groups it was revised before the General Assembly, but its argument stood.

Two cautions keep this fair. First, criticizing Israeli policy, or rejecting a particular political Christian Zionism, is **not** the same as replacement theology or antisemitism (see section 8). Second, many in these same churches reject both antisemitism and replacement theology; the Presbyterian Church (USA), for instance, affirmed in 1987 that God's covenant with the Jewish people remains and that "the church has not 'replaced' the Jewish people." The test is not the denomination but the teaching: does it say God is finished with the Jewish people? Romans 11 says He is not.

# 7. Israel in prophecy

Many Bible students, this page included, read the following passages together as a picture of the last days. Others read them differently: some see them fulfilled in the destruction of the temple in AD 70, others apply them only to the Church. What follows is one careful futurist reading, offered as interpretation, not dogma.

## A temple, a covenant, and an abomination

Daniel foretells a ruler who "shall confirm the covenant with many for one week" and in the middle of it stops the sacrifices and sets up "the abomination that maketh desolate." Its first fulfilment came in 167 BC, when Antiochus IV Epiphanes set up a pagan altar in the temple (Daniel 11:31; 1 Maccabees 1:54). Jesus, speaking two centuries later, still placed "the abomination of desolation… stand in the holy place" in the future, and Paul describes "that man of sin" who "sitteth in the temple of God, shewing himself that he is God." Revelation 11 speaks of a temple and an altar to be measured while the city is trodden down. Read this way, a temple will again stand in Jerusalem.

> **Daniel 9:27:** "And he shall confirm the covenant with many for one week: and in the midst of the week he shall cause the sacrifice and the oblation to cease, and for the overspreading of abominations he shall make it desolate, even until the consummation, and that determined shall be poured upon the desolate."

> **Matthew 24:15-16:** "When ye therefore shall see the abomination of desolation, spoken of by Daniel the prophet, stand in the holy place, (whoso readeth, let him understand:) Then let them which be in Judaea flee into the mountains:"

> **Matthew 24:21:** "For then shall be great tribulation, such as was not since the beginning of the world to this time, no, nor ever shall be."

> **2 Thessalonians 2:3-4:** "Let no man deceive you by any means: for that day shall not come, except there come a falling away first, and that man of sin be revealed, the son of perdition; Who opposeth and exalteth himself above all that is called God, or that is worshipped; so that he as God sitteth in the temple of God, shewing himself that he is God."

> **Revelation 11:1-2:** "And there was given me a reed like unto a rod: and the angel stood, saying, Rise, and measure the temple of God, and the altar, and them that worship therein. But the court which is without the temple leave out, and measure it not; for it is given unto the Gentiles: and the holy city shall they tread under foot forty and two months."

## Received in his own name

Jesus warned His own nation: "I am come in my Father's name, and ye receive me not: if another shall come in his own name, him ye will receive." On this reading, many in Israel will at first welcome the coming ruler, the covenant-maker of Daniel 9:27, as a deliverer, perhaps even as messiah, while Christians recognize him from the Scriptures as the antichrist.

> **John 5:43:** "I am come in my Father’s name, and ye receive me not: if another shall come in his own name, him ye will receive."

> **1 John 2:18:** "Little children, it is the last time: and as ye have heard that antichrist shall come, even now are there many antichrists; whereby we know that it is the last time."

## The woman and her seed

In Revelation 12 the woman clothed with the sun, with the moon under her feet and a crown of twelve stars, is Israel: it is the picture of Joseph's dream of Jacob, Rachel and the twelve tribes, and she gives birth to the Messiah. When the dragon is cast down, he persecutes the woman; she is carried to the wilderness and nourished there for "a time, and times, and half a time" (three and a half years, 1,260 days). Unable to destroy her, the dragon "went to make war with the remnant of her seed, which keep the commandments of God, and have the testimony of Jesus Christ."

On this reading, the ruler who first made a covenant with Israel turns on her in the middle of the "week," as Daniel says, God protects a remnant of the Jewish people, and the devil then turns his fury on the believers in Jesus. That suffering is **the wrath of the devil**, "because he knoweth that he hath but a short time," not the wrath of God, from which believers are promised deliverance. Jesus Himself said His elect would be gathered "immediately after the tribulation of those days." (Whether the Church is taken out before this persecution or goes through it is debated among futurists; this reading follows the texts that place the saints in it.)

> **Genesis 37:9-10:** "And he dreamed yet another dream, and told it his brethren, and said, Behold, I have dreamed a dream more; and, behold, the sun and the moon and the eleven stars made obeisance to me. And he told it to his father, and to his brethren: and his father rebuked him, and said unto him, What is this dream that thou hast dreamed? Shall I and thy mother and thy brethren indeed come to bow down ourselves to thee to the earth?"

> **Revelation 12:1-2:** "And there appeared a great wonder in heaven; a woman clothed with the sun, and the moon under her feet, and upon her head a crown of twelve stars: And she being with child cried, travailing in birth, and pained to be delivered."

> **Revelation 12:5-6:** "And she brought forth a man child, who was to rule all nations with a rod of iron: and her child was caught up unto God, and to his throne. And the woman fled into the wilderness, where she hath a place prepared of God, that they should feed her there a thousand two hundred and threescore days."

> **Revelation 12:12-14:** "Therefore rejoice, ye heavens, and ye that dwell in them. Woe to the inhabiters of the earth and of the sea! for the devil is come down unto you, having great wrath, because he knoweth that he hath but a short time. And when the dragon saw that he was cast unto the earth, he persecuted the woman which brought forth the man child. And to the woman were given two wings of a great eagle, that she might fly into the wilderness, into her place, where she is nourished for a time, and times, and half a time, from the face of the serpent."

> **Revelation 12:17:** "And the dragon was wroth with the woman, and went to make war with the remnant of her seed, which keep the commandments of God, and have the testimony of Jesus Christ."

> **Revelation 13:7:** "And it was given unto him to make war with the saints, and to overcome them: and power was given him over all kindreds, and tongues, and nations."

> **1 Thessalonians 5:9:** "For God hath not appointed us to wrath, but to obtain salvation by our Lord Jesus Christ,"

> **Matthew 24:29-31:** "Immediately after the tribulation of those days shall the sun be darkened, and the moon shall not give her light, and the stars shall fall from heaven, and the powers of the heavens shall be shaken: And then shall appear the sign of the Son of man in heaven: and then shall all the tribes of the earth mourn, and they shall see the Son of man coming in the clouds of heaven with power and great glory. And he shall send his angels with a great sound of a trumpet, and they shall gather together his elect from the four winds, from one end of heaven to the other."

## The time of Jacob's trouble, and the turning

The prophets speak of a time of trouble for Israel such as never was, worse than any before it, from which God's people are nevertheless delivered. Zechariah says two thirds will perish and a third be "brought through the fire" and refined; then they will look upon the One "whom they have pierced," and mourn for him as for an only son. Jesus told Jerusalem it would not see Him again until it says, "Blessed is he that cometh in the name of the Lord." Paul says the Deliverer will come out of Sion and turn away ungodliness from Jacob. After the worst persecution in their history, the Jewish people will at last receive Jesus as their Messiah and Lord.

> **Jeremiah 30:7:** "Alas! for that day is great, so that none is like it: it is even the time of Jacob’s trouble; but he shall be saved out of it."

> **Daniel 12:1:** "And at that time shall Michael stand up, the great prince which standeth for the children of thy people: and there shall be a time of trouble, such as never was since there was a nation even to that same time: and at that time thy people shall be delivered, every one that shall be found written in the book."

> **Zechariah 13:8-9:** "And it shall come to pass, that in all the land, saith the LORD, two parts therein shall be cut off and die; but the third shall be left therein. And I will bring the third part through the fire, and will refine them as silver is refined, and will try them as gold is tried: they shall call on my name, and I will hear them: I will say, It is my people: and they shall say, The LORD is my God."

> **Zechariah 12:10:** "And I will pour upon the house of David, and upon the inhabitants of Jerusalem, the spirit of grace and of supplications: and they shall look upon me whom they have pierced, and they shall mourn for him, as one mourneth for his only son, and shall be in bitterness for him, as one that is in bitterness for his firstborn."

> **Zechariah 14:3-4:** "Then shall the LORD go forth, and fight against those nations, as when he fought in the day of battle. And his feet shall stand in that day upon the mount of Olives, which is before Jerusalem on the east, and the mount of Olives shall cleave in the midst thereof toward the east and toward the west, and there shall be a very great valley; and half of the mountain shall remove toward the north, and half of it toward the south."

> **Matthew 23:37-39:** "O Jerusalem, Jerusalem, thou that killest the prophets, and stonest them which are sent unto thee, how often would I have gathered thy children together, even as a hen gathereth her chickens under her wings, and ye would not! Behold, your house is left unto you desolate. For I say unto you, Ye shall not see me henceforth, till ye shall say, Blessed is he that cometh in the name of the Lord."

> **Romans 11:26-27:** "And so all Israel shall be saved: as it is written, There shall come out of Sion the Deliverer, and shall turn away ungodliness from Jacob: For this is my covenant unto them, when I shall take away their sins."

> **Acts 3:19-21:** "Repent ye therefore, and be converted, that your sins may be blotted out, when the times of refreshing shall come from the presence of the Lord; And he shall send Jesus Christ, which before was preached unto you: Whom the heaven must receive until the times of restitution of all things, which God hath spoken by the mouth of all his holy prophets since the world began."

# 8. Blessing Israel is not a blank check

God said to Abraham, "I will bless them that bless thee, and curse him that curseth thee." Christians are to pray for the peace of Jerusalem, to reject antisemitism in every form, and to desire the safety, sovereignty and well-being of the Jewish people, as Paul did.

> **Genesis 12:3:** "And I will bless them that bless thee, and curse him that curseth thee: and in thee shall all families of the earth be blessed."

> **Psalm 122:6-7:** "Pray for the peace of Jerusalem: they shall prosper that love thee. Peace be within thy walls, and prosperity within thy palaces."

> **Romans 10:1:** "Brethren, my heart’s desire and prayer to God for Israel is, that they might be saved."

But blessing a people is not the same as approving every decision of its rulers. The Bible itself never gave Israel's kings a blank check. Its prophets loved Israel more than anyone and were the first to confront its leaders when they sinned:

- **Samuel and Saul.** "To obey is better than sacrifice… Because thou hast rejected the word of the Lord, he hath also rejected thee from being king" (1 Samuel 15:22-23).
- **Nathan and David.** After David took Bathsheba and had Uriah killed: "Thou art the man" (2 Samuel 12:7-9).
- **Elijah and Ahab.** "I have not troubled Israel; but thou, and thy father's house" (1 Kings 18:17-18). When Ahab and Jezebel had Naboth killed to seize his vineyard, Elijah announced God's judgment on the king (1 Kings 21:17-19).
- **Micaiah and Ahab.** Four hundred court prophets told the king what he wanted to hear; one prophet told him the truth, and the king hated him for it (1 Kings 22:8).
- **Manasseh**, who "shed innocent blood very much, till he had filled Jerusalem from one end to another" (2 Kings 21:16).
- **Jeremiah and Jehoiakim.** "Woe unto him that buildeth his house by unrighteousness… Did not thy father… do judgment and justice?" (Jeremiah 22:13-17).
- **Isaiah, Amos and Micah** condemned Jerusalem's rulers for bribery, oppression and building "Zion with blood" (Isaiah 1:23; 10:1-2; Amos 5:24; Micah 3:9-11).
- **John the Baptist and Herod.** "It is not lawful for thee to have her" (Matthew 14:3-4); Jesus called Herod "that fox" (Luke 13:32).

> **2 Samuel 12:7:** "And Nathan said to David, Thou art the man. Thus saith the LORD God of Israel, I anointed thee king over Israel, and I delivered thee out of the hand of Saul;"

> **1 Kings 18:17-18:** "And it came to pass, when Ahab saw Elijah, that Ahab said unto him, Art thou he that troubleth Israel? And he answered, I have not troubled Israel; but thou, and thy father’s house, in that ye have forsaken the commandments of the LORD, and thou hast followed Baalim."

> **1 Kings 21:18-19:** "Arise, go down to meet Ahab king of Israel, which is in Samaria: behold, he is in the vineyard of Naboth, whither he is gone down to possess it. And thou shalt speak unto him, saying, Thus saith the LORD, Hast thou killed, and also taken possession? And thou shalt speak unto him, saying, Thus saith the LORD, In the place where dogs licked the blood of Naboth shall dogs lick thy blood, even thine."

> **Jeremiah 22:13:** "Woe unto him that buildeth his house by unrighteousness, and his chambers by wrong; that useth his neighbour’s service without wages, and giveth him not for his work;"

> **Jeremiah 22:15-16:** "Shalt thou reign, because thou closest thyself in cedar? did not thy father eat and drink, and do judgment and justice, and then it was well with him? He judged the cause of the poor and needy; then it was well with him: was not this to know me? saith the LORD."

> **Micah 3:9-11:** "Hear this, I pray you, ye heads of the house of Jacob, and princes of the house of Israel, that abhor judgment, and pervert all equity. They build up Zion with blood, and Jerusalem with iniquity. The heads thereof judge for reward, and the priests thereof teach for hire, and the prophets thereof divine for money: yet will they lean upon the LORD, and say, Is not the LORD among us? none evil can come upon us."

> **Amos 5:24:** "But let judgment run down as waters, and righteousness as a mighty stream."

## The same standard for every nation

God held Gentile rulers to the same law. Daniel told Nebuchadnezzar, "break off thy sins by righteousness, and thine iniquities by shewing mercy to the poor." The Law forbade partiality toward the great or the poor, commanded love for the stranger, and the apostles said, "We ought to obey God rather than men." God "is no respecter of persons."

> **Daniel 4:27:** "Wherefore, O king, let my counsel be acceptable unto thee, and break off thy sins by righteousness, and thine iniquities by shewing mercy to the poor; if it may be a lengthening of thy tranquillity."

> **Leviticus 19:15:** "Ye shall do no unrighteousness in judgment: thou shalt not respect the person of the poor, nor honour the person of the mighty: but in righteousness shalt thou judge thy neighbour."

> **Leviticus 19:33-34:** "And if a stranger sojourn with thee in your land, ye shall not vex him. But the stranger that dwelleth with you shall be unto you as one born among you, and thou shalt love him as thyself; for ye were strangers in the land of Egypt: I am the LORD your God."

> **Deuteronomy 10:17-19:** "For the LORD your God is God of gods, and Lord of lords, a great God, a mighty, and a terrible, which regardeth not persons, nor taketh reward: He doth execute the judgment of the fatherless and widow, and loveth the stranger, in giving him food and raiment. Love ye therefore the stranger: for ye were strangers in the land of Egypt."

> **Proverbs 24:23-24:** "These things also belong to the wise. It is not good to have respect of persons in judgment. He that saith unto the wicked, Thou art righteous; him shall the people curse, nations shall abhor him:"

> **Acts 5:29:** "Then Peter and the other apostles answered and said, We ought to obey God rather than men."

> **Acts 10:34-35:** "Then Peter opened his mouth, and said, Of a truth I perceive that God is no respecter of persons: But in every nation he that feareth him, and worketh righteousness, is accepted with him."

> **Micah 6:8:** "He hath shewed thee, O man, what is good; and what doth the LORD require of thee, but to do justly, and to love mercy, and to walk humbly with thy God?"

So a Christian can oppose antisemitism, care deeply about the safety of Jewish people, pray for Israel's peace and salvation, and still say plainly that a government has acted unjustly when it has. That is not hostility toward the Jewish people. It is applying to their leaders the same moral standard the Bible applies to every leader, including the kings of Israel, and the same one we must apply to our own nations and our own churches. We do not give a blank check to any president, prime minister, party or state; we owe love to every people and justice to every person.

# Conclusion

God has not cast away the people He foreknew. Gentile believers stand by faith, grafted into a root they did not plant, and the history of "Christian" contempt for the Jews is the very boasting against the branches that Paul forbade. The same Scriptures that promise Israel's restoration call every ruler, Jewish or Gentile, to justice. Love the Jewish people, pray for their peace and salvation, reject antisemitism wherever it appears, and wait for the day when "all Israel shall be saved."

> **Romans 11:33-36:** "O the depth of the riches both of the wisdom and knowledge of God! how unsearchable are his judgments, and his ways past finding out! For who hath known the mind of the Lord? or who hath been his counsellor? Or who hath first given to him, and it shall be recompensed unto him again? For of him, and through him, and to him, are all things: to whom be glory for ever. Amen."
