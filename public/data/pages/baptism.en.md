## What the Bible teaches

Water baptism is part of salvation, not an optional ceremony:

> **1 Peter 3:21:** "The like figure whereunto even baptism doth also now save us (not the putting away of the filth of the flesh, but the answer of a good conscience toward God,) by the resurrection of Jesus Christ:"

> **Mark 16:16:** "He that believeth and is baptized shall be saved; but he that believeth not shall be damned."

> **Acts 2:38:** "Then Peter said unto them, Repent, and be baptized every one of you in the name of Jesus Christ for the remission of sins, and ye shall receive the gift of the Holy Ghost."

> **Acts 22:16:** "And now why tarriest thou? arise, and be baptized, and wash away thy sins, calling on the name of the Lord."

Baptism expresses faith in God by obedience to His Word, and it specifically confesses faith in **Jesus** as Saviour. In the Bible:

- **The mode is immersion** in water. Only immersion shows what baptism means: a **burial** with Christ and a rising to new life (Romans 6:4; Colossians 2:12).
- **Faith and repentance come first**, so baptism is for those who can believe and repent, not for infants (Matthew 3:6-11; Acts 2:38; 8:12; 18:8).
- **The name is Jesus.** Every baptism in the New Testament church that mentions a name or formula names Jesus (Acts 2:38; 8:16; 10:48; 19:5; 22:16), and the letters allude to the same name (Romans 6:3-4; 1 Corinthians 1:13; 6:11; Galatians 3:27; Colossians 2:12).
- **Matthew 28:19 points to the same name.** It speaks of one singular "name" of the Father, the Son and the Holy Ghost — the name that represents all of God's redemptive manifestations — and that name is Jesus (Zechariah 14:9; Matthew 1:21; John 5:43; 14:26; Revelation 22:3-4). The other accounts of the Great Commission say the same: "in my name" (Mark 16:17), "in his name" (Luke 24:47).
- So baptism should be administered **in the name of the Lord Jesus Christ**: invoking the name of Jesus **aloud** over the person baptized (Acts 22:16; James 2:7), and rebaptizing those who were baptized another way (Acts 19:1-5).

Calling on the name of Jesus expresses faith in His identity, His atoning work, His power and His authority. Jesus is the only saving name, the name for the remission of sins, the name above every name, and the name in which we are to say and do all things (Acts 4:12; 10:43; Philippians 2:9-11; Colossians 3:17). Using Jesus' name is the way to fulfil the purposes of baptism.

(This summary follows the note on baptism in Robin Johnston and Lee Ann Alexander, eds., _Apostolic Study Bible: King James Version_, Word Aflame Press, 2014, pp. 1653–1654.)

This page sets out the evidence: first the Scriptures, then the early Christian writings, then the councils and theologians, and finally the history of how immersion was replaced by pouring and sprinkling. Bible quotations are from the King James Version; the Fathers are quoted from the _Ante-Nicene Fathers_ and _Nicene and Post-Nicene Fathers_ translations.

# 1. Every baptism with a formula: the name of Jesus

The book of Acts records five baptisms that state the name or formula used. Jews, Samaritans, Gentiles and disciples of John — all were baptized in the name of Jesus.

**Jews, at Pentecost:**

> **Acts 2:38:** "Then Peter said unto them, Repent, and be baptized every one of you in the name of Jesus Christ for the remission of sins, and ye shall receive the gift of the Holy Ghost."

> **Acts 2:41:** "Then they that gladly received his word were baptized: and the same day there were added unto them about three thousand souls."

**Samaritans:**

> **Acts 8:12:** "But when they believed Philip preaching the things concerning the kingdom of God, and the name of Jesus Christ, they were baptized, both men and women."

> **Acts 8:16:** "(For as yet he was fallen upon none of them: only they were baptized in the name of the Lord Jesus.)"

**Gentiles, at the house of Cornelius** — even after they had already received the Holy Ghost, Peter "commanded" them to be baptized in the name of the Lord:

> **Acts 10:43-48:** "To him give all the prophets witness, that through his name whosoever believeth in him shall receive remission of sins. While Peter yet spake these words, the Holy Ghost fell on all them which heard the word. And they of the circumcision which believed were astonished, as many as came with Peter, because that on the Gentiles also was poured out the gift of the Holy Ghost. For they heard them speak with tongues, and magnify God. Then answered Peter, Can any man forbid water, that these should not be baptized, which have received the Holy Ghost as well as we? And he commanded them to be baptized in the name of the Lord. Then prayed they him to tarry certain days."

**The disciples of John at Ephesus** — already baptized by immersion for repentance, they were **baptized again** in the name of the Lord Jesus:

> **Acts 19:1-5:** "And it came to pass, that, while Apollos was at Corinth, Paul having passed through the upper coasts came to Ephesus: and finding certain disciples, He said unto them, Have ye received the Holy Ghost since ye believed? And they said unto him, We have not so much as heard whether there be any Holy Ghost. And he said unto them, Unto what then were ye baptized? And they said, Unto John’s baptism. Then said Paul, John verily baptized with the baptism of repentance, saying unto the people, that they should believe on him which should come after him, that is, on Christ Jesus. When they heard this, they were baptized in the name of the Lord Jesus."

**Paul himself** — baptized "calling on the name of the Lord":

> **Acts 22:16:** "And now why tarriest thou? arise, and be baptized, and wash away thy sins, calling on the name of the Lord."

### The name was spoken over the person baptized

"Calling on the name" means invoking it aloud. James speaks of "that worthy name by the which ye are called" — in Greek, literally "the good name **called upon you**" (_to kalon onoma to epiklēthen eph' hymas_). Modern translations make the point explicit: "the excellent name **that was invoked over you**" (NRSV); "that precious name by which you are distinguished and called **[the name of Christ invoked in baptism]**" (Amplified Bible, Classic Edition).

> **James 2:7:** "Do not they blaspheme that worthy name by the which ye are called?"

### The letters allude to the same baptism

> **Romans 6:3-4:** "Know ye not, that so many of us as were baptized into Jesus Christ were baptized into his death? Therefore we are buried with him by baptism into death: that like as Christ was raised up from the dead by the glory of the Father, even so we also should walk in newness of life."

> **1 Corinthians 1:13:** "Is Christ divided? was Paul crucified for you? or were ye baptized in the name of Paul?"

> **1 Corinthians 6:11:** "And such were some of you: but ye are washed, but ye are sanctified, but ye are justified in the name of the Lord Jesus, and by the Spirit of our God."

> **Galatians 3:27:** "For as many of you as have been baptized into Christ have put on Christ."

> **Colossians 2:12:** "Buried with him in baptism, wherein also ye are risen with him through the faith of the operation of God, who hath raised him from the dead."

In 1 Corinthians 1:13 Paul asks, "were ye baptized in the name of Paul?" The question only makes sense if they had been baptized in the name of the One who **was** crucified for them.

### No one in the Bible ever baptized with the words "in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Ghost"

Matthew 28:19 is a command; it is not a record of a baptism. Every time Scripture records what was said at a baptism, the name is Jesus. Not once does anyone repeat the titles "Father, Son and Holy Ghost" over a person being baptized.

### Matthew 28:19: one name

> **Matthew 28:18-20:** "And Jesus came and spake unto them, saying, All power is given unto me in heaven and in earth. Go ye therefore, and teach all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Ghost: Teaching them to observe all things whatsoever I have commanded you: and, lo, I am with you alway, even unto the end of the world. Amen."

Jesus said "the **name**," not "the names." Father, Son and Holy Ghost are titles; the name of all three is Jesus:

- **The Father's name:** "I am come in my Father's name" (John 5:43); "I have manifested thy name" (John 17:6). "Wherefore God also hath highly exalted him, and given him a name which is above every name" (Philippians 2:9). "Holy Father, protect them by Your name, the name You gave Me" (John 17:11 BSB). "I protected and preserved them by Your name, the name You gave Me" (John 17:12 BSB). "as he hath by inheritance obtained a more excellent name than they" (Hebrews 1:4). "Hosanna to the Son of David: Blessed is he that cometh in the name of the Lord" (Matthew 21:9). " I will declare thy name unto my brethren" (Hebrews 2:12). "And I have declared unto them thy name, and will declare it" (John 17:26). "the works that I do in my Father’s name, they bear witness of me" (John 10:25). "And there shall be no more curse: but the throne of God and of the Lamb shall be in it; and his servants shall serve him: And they shall see his face; and his name shall be in their foreheads" (Revelation 22:3-4).
- **The Son's name:** "thou shalt call his name JESUS: for he shall save his people from their sins" (Matthew 1:21). _Yeshua_ means "YHWH is salvation."
- **The Holy Ghost's name:** "the Comforter, which is the Holy Ghost, whom the Father will send **in my name**" (John 14:26). "After the Holy Spirit prevented them from speaking the word in the province of Asia...And when they came to the border of Mysia, they tried to enter Bithynia, but the Spirit of Jesus would not permit them" (Acts 16:6-7 BSB). "But ye are not in the flesh, but in the Spirit, if so be that the Spirit of God dwell in you. Now if any man have not the Spirit of Christ, he is none of his" (Romans 8:9). "Of which salvation the prophets have enquired...Searching what, or what manner of time the Spirit of Christ which was in them did signify, when it testified beforehand the sufferings of Christ..." (1 Peter 1:10-11). "Now the Lord is that Spirit: and where the Spirit of the Lord is, there is liberty" (2 Corinthians 3:17). "For I know that this shall turn to my salvation through your prayer, and the supply of the Spirit of Jesus Christ" (Philippians 1:19). "Teaching them to observe all things whatsoever I have commanded you: and, lo, I am with you alway, even unto the end of the world. Amen" (Matthew 28:20). "I will not leave you comfortless: I will come to you" (John 14:18). "Nevertheless I tell you the truth; It is expedient for you that I go away: for if I go not away, the Comforter will not come unto you; but if I depart, I will send him unto you" (John 16:7).
- **One name:** "Therefore my people shall know my name: therefore they shall know in that day that I am he that doth speak: behold, it is I" (Isaiah 52:6). "in that day shall there be one LORD, and his name one" (Zechariah 14:9); "He that believeth on him is not condemned: but he that believeth not is condemned already, because he hath not believed in the name of the only begotten Son of God" (John 3:18). "his name shall be in their foreheads" (Revelation 22:4).

> **John 5:43:** "I am come in my Father’s name, and ye receive me not: if another shall come in his own name, him ye will receive."

> **Matthew 1:21:** "And she shall bring forth a son, and thou shalt call his name JESUS: for he shall save his people from their sins."

> **John 14:26:** "But the Comforter, which is the Holy Ghost, whom the Father will send in my name, he shall teach you all things, and bring all things to your remembrance, whatsoever I have said unto you."

> **Zechariah 14:9:** "And the LORD shall be king over all the earth: in that day shall there be one LORD, and his name one."

> **Revelation 22:3-4:** "And there shall be no more curse: but the throne of God and of the Lamb shall be in it; and his servants shall serve him: And they shall see his face; and his name shall be in their foreheads."

Isaiah 9:6 gives the child who is born three of these very titles: He is the **Son** ("a son is given"), the **Counsellor** (the Spirit is the Comforter or Counsellor, John 14:16, 26), and **the everlasting Father**:

> **Isaiah 9:6:** "For unto us a child is born, unto us a son is given: and the government shall be upon his shoulder: and his name shall be called Wonderful, Counsellor, The mighty God, The everlasting Father, The Prince of Peace."

The other accounts of the same Great Commission name the name:

> **Mark 16:15-17:** "And he said unto them, Go ye into all the world, and preach the gospel to every creature. He that believeth and is baptized shall be saved; but he that believeth not shall be damned. And these signs shall follow them that believe; In my name shall they cast out devils; they shall speak with new tongues;"

> **Luke 24:46-47:** "And said unto them, Thus it is written, and thus it behoved Christ to suffer, and to rise from the dead the third day: And that repentance and remission of sins should be preached in his name among all nations, beginning at Jerusalem."

Peter, who heard the command, fulfilled it ten days after the ascension by commanding baptism "in the name of Jesus Christ" (Acts 2:38). The apostles did not disobey Jesus; they understood what the name was.

Baptism is not the only thing done in His name. In the New Testament, **everything** is:

**Every word and deed, and giving thanks:**

> **Colossians 3:17:** "And what soever ye do in word or deed, do all in the name of the Lord Jesus, giving thanks to God and the Father by him."

# 3. Baptism was by immersion

### What the word means

The Greek verb _baptizō_ means "to dip, to immerse, to plunge." It was used for dipping cloth in dye and for ships sinking. Naaman "dipped himself seven times in Jordan" — in the Greek Old Testament, _ebaptisato_, he **baptized** himself:

> **2 Kings 5:14:** "Then went he down, and dipped himself seven times in Jordan, according to the saying of the man of God: and his flesh came again like unto the flesh of a little child, and he was clean."

### Jewish immersions

Baptism did not come from nowhere. The Law commanded the unclean to "bathe his flesh in water" (Leviticus 15:13, 16). By the time of Jesus, Jews immersed in a _mikveh_, a pool which, according to the Mishnah, had to hold enough water to cover the whole body (forty _se'ahs_, _Mikvaot_ 1:7). Gentiles who converted to Judaism were immersed (Babylonian Talmud, _Yevamot_ 46a–47b). The Essenes of Qumran practised ritual immersion. John the Baptist chose a place with deep water:

> **John 3:23:** "And John also was baptizing in Ænon near to Salim, because there was much water there: and they came, and were baptized."

> **Mark 1:5:** "And there went out unto him all the land of Judæa, and they of Jerusalem, and were all baptized of him in the river of Jordan, confessing their sins."

> **Mark 1:9-10:** "And it came to pass in those days, that Jesus came from Nazareth of Galilee, and was baptized of John in Jordan. And straightway coming up out of the water, he saw the heavens opened, and the Spirit like a dove descending upon him:"

### In the New Testament

They went **down into** the water and came **up out of** it, because baptism is a **burial**:

> **Matthew 3:16:** "And Jesus, when he was baptized, went up straightway out of the water: and, lo, the heavens were opened unto him, and he saw the Spirit of God descending like a dove, and lighting upon him:"

> **Acts 8:36-39:** "And as they went on their way, they came unto a certain water: and the eunuch said, See, here is water; what doth hinder me to be baptized? And Philip said, If thou believest with all thine heart, thou mayest. And he answered and said, I believe that Jesus Christ is the Son of God. And he commanded the chariot to stand still: and they went down both into the water, both Philip and the eunuch; and he baptized him. And when they were come up out of the water, the Spirit of the Lord caught away Philip, that the eunuch saw him no more: and he went on his way rejoicing."

> **Romans 6:4:** "Therefore we are buried with him by baptism into death: that like as Christ was raised up from the dead by the glory of the Father, even so we also should walk in newness of life."

> **Colossians 2:12:** "Buried with him in baptism, wherein also ye are risen with him through the faith of the operation of God, who hath raised him from the dead."

> **Hebrews 10:22:** "Let us draw near with a true heart in full assurance of faith, having our hearts sprinkled from an evil conscience, and our bodies washed with pure water."

### In the earliest Christian writings

- "We go down into the water full of sins and defilement, and come up bearing fruit in our heart" (_Epistle of Barnabas_ 11.11, c. 100–130).
- "They descend into the water dead, and they arise alive" (_Shepherd of Hermas_, _Similitude_ 9.16.4, c. 140).
- The _Didache_ (see below) prefers "living" (running) water and allows pouring only when there is not enough water to immerse.

Everett Ferguson, in the most complete historical study of the subject (_Baptism in the Early Church_, 2009), concludes that the evidence gives "overwhelming support for full immersion as the normal action," with exceptions only for lack of water and especially for baptism on a sickbed.

# 4. Baptism in the name of Jesus in early Christian writings

The New Testament is clear. But did Christians after the apostles still baptize in the name of Jesus? They did — for centuries, alongside the threefold formula, until the councils of the imperial church forbade it.

### The Shepherd of Hermas (Rome, c. 140)

Hermas, brother of the bishop of Rome, speaks of those who "wish to be baptized **into the name of the Lord**" (_Vision_ 3.7.3), and says: "before a man bears the name of the Son of God he is dead; but when he receives the seal he lays aside his deadness… The seal, then, is the water" (_Similitude_ 9.16.3-4).

### The Didache (c. 100?)

The _Didache_, or _Teaching of the Twelve Apostles_, contradicts itself:

- Chapter 7 says to baptize "in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit," and, if there is not enough water, to "pour water three times upon the head."
- But chapter 9 says: "**let no one eat or drink of your Eucharist, unless they have been baptized into the name of the Lord**" (9.5).

And the _Didache_ is not Scripture, nor a reliable witness to what the apostles taught:

- It was **lost for centuries**. The only complete copy is a single Greek manuscript, Codex Hierosolymitanus, **copied in 1056**, discovered in Constantinople by Philotheos Bryennios in 1873 and published in 1883. Only small fragments and later reworkings exist besides it (a fourth-century papyrus with parts of chapters 1–3, a Coptic fragment, and a revised version inside the _Apostolic Constitutions_, c. 380).
- Its date and authorship are unknown; scholars place it anywhere from the late first to the third century.
- Scholars widely agree that it grew in layers and was edited over time, and some consider the baptismal instructions of chapter 7, with their threefold formula and threefold pouring, to be among the later additions — while chapter 9's "baptized into the name of the Lord" reflects the older practice.

### The Acts of Paul and Thecla (c. 180)

In this popular second-century story, Thecla, condemned to the beasts, throws herself into a pool of water, saying: "**In the name of Jesus Christ I baptize myself on my last day**" (_Acts of Paul and Thecla_ 34).

### The Acts of Peter (c. 180–200)

The _Acts of Peter_ shows the transition. Peter baptizes the ship captain Theon "in the name of the Father and the Son and the Holy Ghost" — yet the text immediately says that God "had accounted Theon worthy of **his name**," and Peter prays to "O God **Jesu Christ**… **in thy name** hath this man now been washed and sealed with thy holy seal" (_Acts of Peter_ 5). Even when the threefold titles were spoken, the name into which the man was baptized was understood to be the name of Jesus Christ.

### Pope Stephen of Rome (c. 256)

In the middle of the third century a great controversy arose over people baptized outside the mainstream church. **Stephen, bishop of Rome**, held that their baptism was valid, and his reason was the name of Christ. Firmilian of Caesarea, who opposed him, reports Stephen's position:

> "…the name of Christ is of great advantage to faith and the sanctification of baptism; so that whosoever is anywhere so-ever **baptized in the name of Christ**, immediately obtains the grace of Christ." (Firmilian, _Letter to Cyprian_ [Cyprian, _Letter_ 74/75], 18)

### Cyprian of Carthage (c. 256)

Cyprian opposed Stephen, and his letter shows what people were doing:

> "But some say… that **all who are baptized everywhere, and in any manner, in the name of Jesus Christ, have obtained the grace of baptism**." (Cyprian, _Letter_ 72 [73], to Jubaianus, 16)

Cyprian could not understand why the apostles had baptized in the name of Jesus, because he believed baptism had to be in the threefold titles. So he explained Acts 2:38 away: Peter mentioned Jesus Christ, he says, "not as though the Father should be omitted, but that the Son also might be joined to the Father" — because Peter was speaking to **Jews**, who already had the Father (17). Then, he says, Christ commanded the **Gentiles** to be baptized "in the full and united Trinity" (18).

But Acts refutes Cyprian. The name of Jesus was not only for Jews: **Samaritans** (Acts 8:16), **Gentiles** (Acts 10:48) and **disciples of John** in a Greek city (Acts 19:5) were all baptized in the name of Jesus — and the disciples at Ephesus were baptized **again** in that name. Cyprian's explanation was invented to make Acts fit the formula of his own day.

### A Treatise on Rebaptism (c. 256)

An anonymous bishop, writing on Stephen's side of the same controversy, defends the baptism of those baptized in the name of Jesus:

> "…the **invocation of the name of Jesus** ought not to be thought futile by us…" (_Treatise on Rebaptism_ 7)

And he explains why the apostles commanded baptism in the name of Jesus:

> "Nor, as I think, was it for any other reason that the apostles had charged those whom they addressed in the Holy Spirit, that they should be **baptized in the name of Christ Jesus**, except that **the power of the name of Jesus invoked upon any man by baptism might afford to him who should be baptized no slight advantage for the attainment of salvation**, as Peter relates in the Acts of the Apostles, saying: 'For there is none other name under heaven given among men whereby we must be saved.' As also the Apostle Paul unfolds, showing that God hath exalted our Lord Jesus, and given Him a name, that it may be above every name, that in the name of Jesus all should bow the knee, of things heavenly and earthly, and under the earth, and every tongue should confess that Jesus is Lord in the glory of God the Father. And he on whom, when he should be baptized, **invocation should be made in the name of Jesus**, although he might obtain baptism under some error, still would not be hindered from knowing the truth at some time or another, and correcting his error, and coming to the Church and to the bishop, and sincerely **confessing our Jesus** before men; so that then, when hands were laid upon him by the bishop, he might also receive the Holy Spirit, and he would not lose that former **invocation of the name of Jesus**." (_Treatise on Rebaptism_ 6)

He cites the Samaritans, who "had only been baptized **in the name of the Lord Jesus**" (3), and Peter's words at the house of Cornelius, "he commanded them to be baptized **in the name of Jesus Christ**" (5), and quotes Acts 4:12: "For there is none other name under heaven given among men whereby we must be saved" (6). He insists that "the most holy invocation of the name of Jesus Christ" must be guarded (15). In the middle of the third century, baptism in the name of Jesus was still practised widely enough to be defended by one bishop of Rome and attacked by another bishop of Carthage.

# 5. The imperial church forbids it

After Constantine, the church of the empire made the threefold formula and triple immersion a rule, and condemned those who did otherwise:

- **The Apostolic Canons** (Syria, c. 380), canon 50: "If any bishop or presbyter does not perform the one initiation with three immersions, but with giving one immersion only, **into the death of the Lord**, let him be deposed. For the Lord said not, Baptize into my death, but, Go ye and make disciples of all nations, baptizing them into the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Ghost." Someone was still baptizing with one immersion "into the death of the Lord" (Romans 6:3) — and the church deposed them for it.
- **Constantinople (381), canon 7** (an appended rule, repeated by the Council in Trullo in 692, canon 95): "Eunomians, who are baptized with only **one immersion**, and Montanists… and **Sabellians, who teach the identity of Father and Son**… all these, when they desire to turn to orthodoxy, we receive **as heathen**." The baptism of the "Sabellians" — those who believed the Father and the Son are one — was not recognized at all; they had to be baptized again as if they had never been Christians.
- **Pope Pelagius I** (c. 560) still had to rule on people who said they had been baptized "in the name of the Lord" only, and required that they be baptized in the threefold name (Denzinger 445).

The canons do not describe the baptismal words of the "Sabellians," but it is not hard to see why a church that believed in one God, who is Father, Son and Spirit, and whose name is Jesus, would baptize in the name of Jesus — and why its baptism was rejected.

It is also worth asking why the formula changed at all. The words of Matthew 28:19 can be spoken as three universal titles that say nothing of a particular name. The name the apostles invoked was a **Jewish** name — _Yeshua_, "YHWH is salvation" — the name of a crucified Jew. A church that became the religion of the Roman Empire had less and less use for it.

# 6. Even Trinitarian theologians admitted it

- **Ambrose of Milan** (c. 381): "**He who is blessed in Christ is blessed in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit, because the Name is One** and the Power One" (_On the Holy Spirit_ 1.3). Ambrose argued that baptism in the name of Christ is complete, because naming Christ includes the Father who anointed Him and the Spirit with whom He was anointed (1.3).
- **Pope Nicholas I** (866), answering the Bulgarians, ruled that those "baptized in the name of the Holy Trinity **or in the name of Christ only**, as we read in the Acts of the Apostles," are not to be rebaptized (_Responses to the Bulgarians_ 104).
- **Peter Lombard** and **Hugh of St. Victor** (twelfth century) held that baptism in the name of Christ alone was valid.
- **Thomas Aquinas** (c. 1270) admitted that "**in the primitive Church the apostles baptized in the name of Christ**," explaining it as "a special revelation from Christ" (_Summa Theologiae_ III, q. 66, a. 6).
- The **Catholic Encyclopedia** (1907) acknowledges that Aquinas, Bonaventure and Albert the Great held that the apostles baptized in the name of Christ only.
- **Martin Luther** (1520) wrote that baptism "truly saves in whatever way it is administered," called disputes about the exact words "pedantry," and said: "**it is certain the apostles used this formula in baptizing, as we read in the Acts of the Apostles**" — that is, "in the name of Jesus Christ" (_The Babylonian Captivity of the Church_).
- **Ulrich Zwingli** (1525), the Reformer of Zurich, wrote in _On Baptism, Rebaptism and Infant Baptism_ that "Christ did not, with these words, institute the form of baptism, as the theologians speak of it. For **the disciples did not use this form, but they baptized in the name of Jesus**" (citing Acts 10:48 and 19:5). So the words of Matthew 28:19 "were not instituted as a form, and **the theologians err on this point as badly as they ever have in their lives**." He added that he did not reject baptizing with the threefold words: "Not that I reject being baptized this way — no, no." (_Huldreich Zwinglis sämtliche Werke_, vol. 4, pp. 236–237; translated from his Swiss German: "die jünger habend dise gestalt oder form nit gebrucht, sunder sy habend getoufft in den namen Jesu… die theologi an dem ort als vast irrend als ir lebtag ie.")

# 7. How immersion was replaced

### Triple immersion

The New Testament speaks of being buried with Christ in baptism — one burial, one death. By about A.D. 200 Tertullian describes Christians being immersed **three times**, once at each title (_Against Praxeas_ 26), and that became the rule in East and West. But it was never universal: those who baptized with one immersion "into the death of the Lord" were deposed (Apostolic Canon 50); Pope Gregory the Great allowed the churches of Spain to keep a **single** immersion (_Letters_ 1.43, 591), and the Fourth Council of Toledo (633) made single immersion the rule there.

### Infant baptism

There is no clear evidence of infant baptism before the end of the second century. Around 200, Tertullian still argued against it: "Let them come, then, while they are growing up… let them become Christians when they have become able to know Christ" (_On Baptism_ 18). By the mid-third century it was spreading (Cyprian, _Letter_ 64 [58]).

Ferguson concludes that the most plausible origin of infant baptism is the **emergency baptism of sick children who were expected to die**. In a world of very high infant mortality, Christian burial inscriptions from the catacombs record small children baptized shortly before their death. The practice spread from there as a precaution, and became usual only in the fifth and sixth centuries — especially after Augustine taught that unbaptized infants are damned.

But an infant cannot believe, repent or call on the name of the Lord (Mark 16:16; Acts 2:38; 8:36-37; 22:16).

### Pouring and sprinkling

- Pouring began as an **exception**. The _Didache_ allowed it when there was not enough water. In the third century, people baptized on their sickbeds by having water poured over them ("clinical baptism") were looked on with suspicion: when Novatian, baptized this way, was ordained, Cornelius of Rome questioned whether he had truly received baptism at all (Eusebius, _Church History_ 6.43.14-17). Cyprian defended it as valid only for the sick (_Letter_ 75 [69], to Magnus).
- For more than a thousand years the normal baptism, in East and West, was immersion. Ancient baptisteries were pools to step down into.
- The Catholic Encyclopedia states: "**In the Latin Church, immersion seems to have prevailed until the twelfth century**," and that infusion (pouring) and aspersion (sprinkling) "were growing common in the thirteenth century and gradually prevailed in the Western Church." Thomas Aquinas still wrote that "it is safer to baptize by immersion, because this is the more ordinary fashion" (_Summa Theologiae_ III, q. 66, a. 7).
- Historians point to several reasons for the change: once nearly everyone baptized was a **newborn infant**, it was easier and seemed safer to pour water over the child's head at a small font than to immerse it, especially in the cold churches of northern Europe; the old exception for the sick became the norm.
- Even the Reformers admitted the original practice. John Calvin wrote that "**the term baptize means to immerse, and that this was the form used by the primitive Church**," though he thought the mode did not matter (_Institutes_ 4.15.19). Luther wished that those baptized were "completely immersed," because that is what the word means and what baptism signifies (_Sermon on the Holy Sacrament of Baptism_, 1519).
- The Eastern Orthodox churches still baptize by immersion today.

# Conclusion

In the New Testament church there is one baptism (Ephesians 4:5): believers who have repented are buried with Christ by immersion in water, and the name of Jesus is called over them for the remission of their sins. Jews, Samaritans and Gentiles were all baptized that way, and those baptized otherwise were baptized again. Everything else the church did — preaching, praying, healing, casting out devils, giving thanks, every word and deed — was done in the same name. After the apostles, baptism in the name of Jesus continued for centuries: in Hermas and the _Didache_'s "baptized into the name of the Lord," in Thecla's cry, in the _Acts of Peter_, in the arguments of a bishop of Rome and an anonymous bishop in the 250s, and in the "Sabellians" whom the imperial church received "as heathen." Triple immersion, infant baptism, pouring and sprinkling came later, one step at a time.

"Neither is there salvation in any other: for there is none other name under heaven given among men, whereby we must be saved." (Acts 4:12)

"And now why tarriest thou? arise, and be baptized, and wash away thy sins, calling on the name of the Lord." (Acts 22:16)
