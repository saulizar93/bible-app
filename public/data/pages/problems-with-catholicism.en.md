## The question

The Roman Catholic Church teaches that it is the one Church founded by Christ, that Christ made Peter the rock and the holder of the keys, and that the bishops of Rome inherit Peter's authority: "the Roman Pontiff, by reason of his office as Vicar of Christ… has full, supreme, and universal power over the whole Church" (_Catechism of the Catholic Church_ 882). Pope Boniface VIII went further: "it is altogether necessary to salvation for every human creature to be subject to the Roman Pontiff" (_Unam Sanctam_, 1302).

This page tests those claims against Scripture and history. Several points are covered in detail on other pages of this app — **Matthew 16:18**, **First Bishops of Rome**, **The One True Church**, **Image veneration**, **Baptism**, **Who gave us the Bible?**, **Deuterocanonical Books** and **Church History** — so here they are summarized and tied together.

Many Catholics love Jesus sincerely. The question is not their sincerity but whether these doctrines come from the apostles. The Bereans were praised for checking even an apostle against the Scriptures:

> **Acts 17:11:** "These were more noble than those in Thessalonica, in that they received the word with all readiness of mind, and searched the scriptures daily, whether those things were so."

# 1. Peter and the rock

The main text for the papacy is Matthew 16:18. Catholic apologists explain that Jesus spoke Aramaic, in which "Peter" and "rock" are the same word, _Kepha_. But the Holy Spirit gave us the Gospel in Greek, and Matthew wrote two different words: "thou art **Petros**, and upon this **petra** I will build my church." _Petros_ is a masculine name meaning "a stone"; _petra_ is the feminine word for rock, bedrock. The Greek Old Testament (the Septuagint) shows what a _petros_ was: in 2 Maccabees 1:16 the priests "threw stones" (_petrous_) down through a hidden door in the roof and struck down the king's commander, and in 2 Maccabees 4:41 the crowd "caught stones" (_petrous_) and clubs to throw at Lysimachus's men. A _petros_ is a stone you can pick up and throw, not the bedrock a house is built on. The Bible calls Christ the _petra_: the "rock of offence" (Romans 9:33; 1 Peter 2:8), "and that Rock was Christ" (1 Corinthians 10:4). In the Old Testament God is the Rock (Hebrew _tsur_), and there is no other:

> **Isaiah 44:8:** "Fear ye not, neither be afraid: have not I told thee from that time, and have declared it? ye are even my witnesses. Is there a God beside me? yea, there is no God; I know not any."

> **1 Corinthians 3:11:** "For other foundation can no man lay than that is laid, which is Jesus Christ."

> **1 Peter 2:5-7:** "Ye also, as lively stones, are built up a spiritual house, an holy priesthood, to offer up spiritual sacrifices, acceptable to God by Jesus Christ. Wherefore also it is contained in the scripture, Behold, I lay in Sion a chief corner stone, elect, precious: and he that believeth on him shall not be confounded. Unto you therefore which believe he is precious: but unto them which be disobedient, the stone which the builders disallowed, the same is made the head of the corner,"

- **The foundation belongs to all the apostles.** The church is "built upon the foundation of the apostles and prophets, Jesus Christ himself being the chief corner stone" (Ephesians 2:20), and the New Jerusalem has twelve foundations with the names of the twelve apostles (Revelation 21:14) — not one.
- **The same Peter, five verses later,** is told "Get thee behind me, Satan: **thou art an offence unto me**" (Matthew 16:23). The Greek word is _skandalon_, a stumbling block: a stone in the path that trips the one who walks. Christ is the _petra_ on which the church is built; in the same chapter Peter is a stumbling block in Christ's way. He cannot be the foundation rock that Scripture reserves for Christ. The promise that the gates of hell will not prevail rests on Christ, not on the man.
- **Peter never claimed it.** He called himself "also an elder" among elders (1 Peter 5:1). Paul "withstood him to the face, because he was to be blamed" (Galatians 2:11). At the Jerusalem council it was **James** who gave the final judgment (Acts 15:13-19).
- **Paul did not build on another man's foundation** (Romans 15:20) — yet he wrote to the church of Rome, planned to visit it, and in sixteen chapters, with greetings to more than two dozen people there (Romans 16), never mentions Peter.
- **The Fathers did not agree with Rome.** Origen (c. 245) said every disciple who confesses Christ as Peter did "becomes a Peter"; Hilary, Ambrose and Chrysostom said the rock is Peter's confession of faith; Augustine said "the rock was Christ" and left the reader to choose (_Retractations_ 1.21). The Catholic scholar Jean de Launoy counted the ancient interpreters and found that four in five did not even identify the rock with Peter. And the fifth did not agree with Rome either: Tertullian said the promise was given to Peter personally and could not be passed on (_On Modesty_ 21), and Cyprian said every apostle had "a like partnership both of honour and power." Outside Rome's own bishops, not one early Father read the verse the way the popes do today (see **Matthew 16:18**).

The word "church" (_ekklesia_) means an assembly of called-out people — the believers — not an institution with headquarters in a city.

# 2. The keys

"I will give unto thee the keys of the kingdom of heaven: and whatsoever thou shalt bind on earth shall be bound in heaven" (Matthew 16:19).

1. **The same power to bind and loose was given to all the disciples** in Matthew 18:18, and the power to forgive or retain sins to all of them in John 20:22-23. If binding and loosing require the keys, all the apostles had them.
2. **The keys are the knowledge of the way of salvation.** Jesus accused the lawyers of having "taken away the key of knowledge" (Luke 11:52), and the scribes and Pharisees of shutting up the kingdom of heaven against men (Matthew 23:13). They "sit in Moses' seat" (Matthew 23:2) — not a physical chair, but the possession and teaching of the law. A key opens by **declaring God's word**; only God admits or excludes.
3. **Peter used the keys exactly that way.** He opened the door of faith to the Jews at Pentecost (Acts 2:14-41), to the Samaritans with John (Acts 8:14-17), and to the Gentiles in the house of Cornelius (Acts 10:34-48). That was his honor: to be first. Nothing is said about passing the keys to a successor.
4. **Catholic apologists appeal to Isaiah 22:22**, where Eliakim receives the key of the house of David, as a model of a papal "office." But the New Testament applies that very verse to **Jesus Himself**: "he that hath the key of David, he that openeth, and no man shutteth" (Revelation 3:7). Christ did not hand His key to anyone; He also holds "the keys of hell and of death" (Revelation 1:18).

> **Matthew 18:18:** "Verily I say unto you, Whatsoever ye shall bind on earth shall be bound in heaven: and whatsoever ye shall loose on earth shall be loosed in heaven."

> **Luke 11:52:** "Woe unto you, lawyers! for ye have taken away the key of knowledge: ye entered not in yourselves, and them that were entering in ye hindered."

> **Revelation 3:7:** "And to the angel of the church in Philadelphia write; These things saith he that is holy, he that is true, he that hath the key of David, he that openeth, and no man shutteth; and shutteth, and no man openeth;"

# 3. How Rome rose to power

### No pope at the beginning

The earliest documents show the church in Rome led by a group of elders (presbyters), as Paul instructed in 1 Timothy 3 and Titus 1. _1 Clement_ (c. 96) is written in the name of "the church of God which sojourns at Rome," not by a bishop; _The Shepherd of Hermas_ speaks of "the elders who preside over the church." A single bishop over Rome appears only around the middle of the second century (see **First Bishops of Rome**).

When bishops of Rome tried to impose their will on others, they were resisted: Polycarp and the Asian churches over Easter (Victor, c. 190); Cyprian and the African bishops over rebaptism (Stephen, 256), when Firmilian of Caesarea called Stephen's claim "open and manifest folly."

### Rank by political importance

Rome's first place came from being the **capital of the empire**, and the councils said so:

- **Constantinople (381), canon 3:** the bishop of Constantinople shall have "the prerogative of honour after the bishop of Rome, **because Constantinople is New Rome**." Constantinople had no apostolic founder at all.
- **Chalcedon (451), canon 28:** "the Fathers rightly granted privileges to the throne of old Rome, **because it was the royal city**," and granted equal privileges to New Rome. Pope Leo I rejected the canon, but the East kept it.
- In **445** Emperor Valentinian III issued an edict ordering the bishops of Gaul to obey the bishop of Rome, and governors to compel anyone who refused — papal authority enforced by imperial law.

### The claims grow

- **385** — Siricius writes that "the blessed apostle Peter… bears them in us" (letter to Himerius), one of the first times a bishop of Rome speaks as if Peter acts through him.
- **431** — At the Council of Ephesus, the papal legate Philip declares that Peter "lives and judges in his successors."
- **c. 501** — Supporters of Pope Symmachus, accused of misconduct, circulate forged documents (the "Symmachian forgeries") to establish that "the first see is judged by no one."
- **8th–9th century** — The _Donation of Constantine_, a forgery in which Constantine supposedly hands the Western empire to Pope Sylvester, and the _Pseudo-Isidorian Decretals_, a collection of forged papal letters, are used for centuries to support papal power. The Catholic scholar Lorenzo Valla proved the Donation a forgery in 1440.

### Broken succession

From 537 to 752 a newly elected pope had to wait for the **emperor's confirmation** before he could be consecrated. Popes were installed and deposed by Roman families and German emperors; in 963 a synod deposed John XII for immorality. During the **Western Schism (1378–1417)** there were two rival popes, and after the Council of Pisa (1409) three, each excommunicating the others; the Council of Constance resolved it by deposing or accepting the resignation of all three. Which was the true link in the chain of Peter?

The **Great Schism of 1054** was between Rome and **Constantinople** (Cardinal Humbert and Patriarch Michael Cerularius), over papal authority and the _filioque_; Alexandria's main split had come earlier, after Chalcedon (451).

# 4. Paganism absorbed

In 601 Pope Gregory the Great instructed missionaries in England not to destroy pagan temples but to sprinkle them with holy water, set up altars and relics in them, and replace the pagan sacrificial feasts with Christian festivals, so that the people would keep coming to "the places to which they have been accustomed" (Bede, _Ecclesiastical History_ 1.30). In 609 the Pantheon in Rome, the temple of all the gods, was consecrated as the church of **St. Mary and the Martyrs**. Statues, processions, patron saints for every trade and city, holy water and votive offerings filled the space the old gods had occupied (see **Image veneration**).

> **Exodus 20:4-5:** "Thou shalt not make unto thee any graven image, or any likeness of any thing that is in heaven above, or that is in the earth beneath, or that is in the water under the earth: Thou shalt not bow down thyself to them, nor serve them: for I the LORD thy God am a jealous God, visiting the iniquity of the fathers upon the children unto the third and fourth generation of them that hate me;"

> **1 John 5:21:** "Little children, keep yourselves from idols. Amen."

The miracle often tied to the Eucharist is the **miracle of Bolsena** (1263), where a priest from Prague who doubted the real presence saw the host bleed; it led Pope Urban IV to establish the feast of **Corpus Christi** (1264) — after transubstantiation had already been defined.

Jesus warned against exactly this:

> **Matthew 15:9:** "But in vain they do worship me, teaching for doctrines the commandments of men."

> **Colossians 2:8:** "Beware lest any man spoil you through philosophy and vain deceit, after the tradition of men, after the rudiments of the world, and not after Christ."

# 6. Mary

Catholic teaching holds four Marian dogmas: Mother of God (Ephesus, 431), perpetual virginity ("ever-virgin," Lateran Synod, 649), Immaculate Conception (1854) and bodily Assumption (1950). Only the first rests on Scripture — and it is a statement about **Jesus**, that the child she bore was God manifest in the flesh.

### Ever-virgin?

The Catechism teaches that Mary remained a virgin "even in the act of giving birth" (CCC 499). The Gospels say Joseph "knew her not **till** she had brought forth her firstborn son" (Matthew 1:25), the normal biblical expression for marital relations (Genesis 4:1, 17, 25; 38:26; Judges 11:39; 1 Kings 1:4), and they name Jesus' brothers and mention His sisters:

> **Matthew 1:24-25:** "Then Joseph being raised from sleep did as the angel of the Lord had bidden him, and took unto him his wife: And knew her not till she had brought forth her firstborn son: and he called his name JESUS."

> **Matthew 13:55-56:** "Is not this the carpenter’s son? is not his mother called Mary? and his brethren, James, and Joses, and Simon, and Judas? And his sisters, are they not all with us? Whence then hath this man all these things?"

> **Galatians 1:19:** "But other of the apostles saw I none, save James the Lord’s brother."

See also Matthew 12:46-50; Mark 3:31-35; 6:3; John 2:12; 7:3-5; Acts 1:14; 1 Corinthians 9:5. Paul tells married couples not to deprive one another (1 Corinthians 7:3-5).

The answers to these verses come from the **Protoevangelium of James**, an anonymous writing of about 150 that claims to be by James. It invents Mary's birth to Joachim and Anna, her childhood in the temple, an elderly widower Joseph with sons from a previous marriage (so Jesus' "brothers" are step-brothers), and a midwife who examines Mary after the birth and finds her still a virgin (chapters 19–20). Origen (_Commentary on Matthew_ 10.17) and Clement of Alexandria (_Stromata_ 7.16) used it. But it was never accepted as Scripture: **Jerome** rejected its step-brother theory and called the brothers cousins (_Against Helvidius_, 383), and the _Gelasian Decree_ (6th century) listed it among the rejected apocrypha. (The story of the boy Jesus striking a child dead is from a different apocryphal book, the _Infancy Gospel of Thomas_.) Mary's parents' names, her presentation in the temple and her lifelong virginity all entered Catholic feasts and art from this book.

### Immaculate?

Forty days after the birth, Mary offered the purification sacrifice the Law required of every mother — a burnt offering and a **sin offering** (Luke 2:22-24; Leviticus 12:6-8) — and she called God "my Saviour" (Luke 1:47). When a woman blessed Mary's womb, Jesus answered:

> **Luke 11:27-28:** "And it came to pass, as he spake these things, a certain woman of the company lifted up her voice, and said unto him, Blessed is the womb that bare thee, and the paps which thou hast sucked. But he said, Yea rather, blessed are they that hear the word of God, and keep it."

> **Matthew 12:48-50:** "But he answered and said unto him that told him, Who is my mother? and who are my brethren? And he stretched forth his hand toward his disciples, and said, Behold my mother and my brethren! For whosoever shall do the will of my Father which is in heaven, the same is my brother, and sister, and mother."

### The Rosary

Since Pius V (1569), popes have attributed the Rosary to a vision of Mary to St. Dominic, who was preaching against the Albigensian heretics (early 13th century) while a crusade was being waged against them. Yet the _Catholic Encyclopedia_ (1912) itself concludes that the Dominic story is not supported by evidence; it was spread by the Dominican Alan de la Roche in the 1470s. Its repeated prayers are the kind Jesus warned about: "use not vain repetitions, as the heathen do" (Matthew 6:7).

The Church presents each new Marian title as a truth that was always there and only gradually discovered. But no one understood the faith better than the apostles, who wrote it down for us; later "development" is not discovery but addition.

> **2 Timothy 4:3-4:** "For the time will come when they will not endure sound doctrine; but after their own lusts shall they heap to themselves teachers, having itching ears; And they shall turn away their ears from the truth, and shall be turned unto fables."

# 7. The Bible and the canon

### The deuterocanonical books

Catholics say Protestants removed seven books. History shows the question was open within Catholicism itself until 1546 (see **Deuterocanonical Books** and **Who gave us the Bible?**).

- **Jesus' Bible** was the Hebrew Scriptures, which the Jews arranged from Genesis to Chronicles. He spoke of the blood of the prophets "from the blood of Abel unto the blood of Zacharias" (Luke 11:51) — the first and last martyrs in that order (Genesis 4; 2 Chronicles 24:20-22).
- **1 Maccabees itself** says there was no prophet in its day (4:46; 9:27; 14:41).
- **Jerome**, who translated the Latin Vulgate, wrote that the Church reads Judith, Tobit and Maccabees "for the edification of the people, not to confirm the authority of doctrines" (_Preface to the Books of Solomon_); he noted Greek puns in the story of Susanna that show it was not written in Hebrew. Athanasius, Cyril of Jerusalem, Rufinus and Gregory the Great also excluded them from the canon, and Cardinal Cajetan, Luther's opponent, still followed Jerome in 1532. Augustine and the North African councils (393–419) accepted them.
- At the **Leipzig Disputation** (1519), Johann Eck defended purgatory with 2 Maccabees 12:43-46 (prayer for the dead); Luther answered that the book was not canonical. In 1546 the **Council of Trent** declared these books canonical under anathema — the first time a council of the whole Western Church made it binding.

### Keeping the Bible from the people

The Council of Toulouse (1229) forbade lay people to own the Old or New Testament; the Index of Forbidden Books (1559–1966) required a bishop's permission to read a vernacular Bible; Clement XI condemned the proposition that reading Scripture is for everyone (_Unigenitus_, 1713). Since Vatican II (1965) the Church encourages Bible reading — but the Magisterium remains its only authentic interpreter (_Dei Verbum_ 10).

### The second commandment

Catholic catechisms follow Augustine's numbering, which joins "no other gods" and "no graven image" into one commandment and splits "thou shalt not covet" into two. The short form taught to children — "I am the Lord your God: you shall not have strange gods before me" — leaves out the words about images entirely.

# 8. Faith and works

The Council of Trent declared: "If any one saith, that by **faith alone** the impious is justified… let him be anathema" (Session 6, canon 9). Paul said the opposite:

> **Ephesians 2:8-9:** "For by grace are ye saved through faith; and that not of yourselves: it is the gift of God: Not of works, lest any man should boast."

> **Romans 4:4-5:** "Now to him that worketh is the reward not reckoned of grace, but of debt. But to him that worketh not, but believeth on him that justifieth the ungodly, his faith is counted for righteousness."

> **Genesis 15:6:** "And he believed in the LORD; and he counted it to him for righteousness."

Catholics cite James 2:14-26. But James's own example, Abraham, was counted righteous by faith in Genesis 15 — years before he offered Isaac in Genesis 22. James shows that true faith produces works; Paul shows that works do not produce salvation. They were not rivals: James, Peter and John gave Paul "the right hands of fellowship" after hearing his gospel (Galatians 2:9). Good works are the fruit of love — "for he that loveth not his brother whom he hath seen, how can he love God whom he hath not seen?" (1 John 4:20).

# Conclusion

Studying Catholic preaching, I examined every verse used for baptism, works, the one true church, purgatory, Mary and the saints. Again and again, simply reading the verse before or after showed that the passage was being made to say something it does not say. Rome is not the only church that does this, which is why every Christian should read the Bible for himself. The pattern of the original church is still there, in the book of Acts and the letters of the apostles. Christians may differ on secondary matters (Romans 14:1-12); but salvation is by grace through faith in Jesus Christ, and no institution holds the keys to it.

> **1 Timothy 2:5:** "For there is one God, and one mediator between God and men, the man Christ Jesus;"

> **John 5:39:** "Search the scriptures; for in them ye think ye have eternal life: and they are they which testify of me."
