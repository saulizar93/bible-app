## The question

Islam teaches that Muhammad (c. 570–632) is the last and greatest prophet, and that the Qur'an is God's final, perfectly preserved word, which corrects the Bible. This page asks a simple question: **can that claim be trusted** — especially over the Christian message, which rests on first-century eyewitnesses? We will let Islam's own sources answer: the Qur'an, the hadith collections that Muslims themselves call _sahih_ ("authentic"), and the earliest biography of Muhammad.

The aim is not to mock Muslims. Many are sincere people who fear God, and Christ died for them too. The aim is to test a message, as the Bible commands.

> **Galatians 1:8:** "But though we, or an angel from heaven, preach any other gospel unto you than that which we have preached unto you, let him be accursed."

> **1 John 4:1:** "Beloved, believe not every spirit, but try the spirits whether they are of God: because many false prophets are gone out into the world."

### A familiar pattern

After studying several religions, I noticed a pattern. Islam, Mormonism and Roman Catholicism all use the Bible's evidence, but each adds new revelation or authority on top of it: the Qur'an, the _Book of Mormon_, the Catechism and the Magisterium. Each places final authority in one man or office (Muhammad, Joseph Smith and his successors, the Pope); each teaches salvation that depends on our works; each discourages members from examining the claims for themselves; and each claims to be the only way to salvation. The New Testament warned that this would happen.

> **2 Timothy 4:3-4:** "For the time will come when they will not endure sound doctrine; but after their own lusts shall they heap to themselves teachers, having itching ears; And they shall turn away their ears from the truth, and shall be turned unto fables."

# 1. One man, no witnesses

Christianity began with **public events** before many witnesses. Jesus taught and healed in the open; he was crucified under Pontius Pilate before crowds; Paul wrote that the risen Jesus was seen by over five hundred people at once, "of whom the greater part remain unto this present" — that is, go and ask them (see _Evidence for Jesus_). The apostles were willing to die for what they had **seen**.

> **1 Corinthians 15:3-8:** "For I delivered unto you first of all that which I also received, how that Christ died for our sins according to the scriptures; And that he was buried, and that he rose again the third day according to the scriptures: And that he was seen of Cephas, then of the twelve: After that, he was seen of above five hundred brethren at once; of whom the greater part remain unto this present, but some are fallen asleep. After that, he was seen of James; then of all the apostles. And last of all he was seen of me also, as of one born out of due time."

Islam began with **one man alone in a cave**. Around 610, Muhammad said an angel appeared to him in the cave of Hira, near Mecca. No one else saw or heard anything. Everything Islam teaches rests on the word of that one witness, received over 23 years (610–632).

The Bible also says that God's final word was spoken in His Son, and that the faith was delivered **once for all** — not to be completed six centuries later.

> **Hebrews 1:1-2:** "God, who at sundry times and in divers manners spake in time past unto the fathers by the prophets, Hath in these last days spoken unto us by his Son, whom he hath appointed heir of all things, by whom also he made the worlds;"

> **Jude 1:3:** "Beloved, when I gave all diligence to write unto you of the common salvation, it was needful for me to write unto you, and exhort you that ye should earnestly contend for the faith which was once delivered unto the saints."

# 2. Very late sources

The Qur'an tells almost nothing about Muhammad's life. To know what he said and did, Muslims depend on the **hadith** (reports passed down by chains of narrators) and the **sira** (biography). The problem is how late they are.

| Source | Written | Years after Muhammad's death (632) |
|---|---|---|
| Ibn Ishaq, _Sirat Rasul Allah_ (earliest biography) | before 767 — survives only in Ibn Hisham's edited version (d. 833) | ~135 (original lost) |
| Al-Bukhari, _Sahih_ (the most trusted hadith collection) | c. 846 (al-Bukhari died 870) | ~215 |
| Muslim ibn al-Hajjaj, _Sahih Muslim_ | before 875 | ~230 |

By comparison, the Gospel of Mark was written around 65–70, about 35–40 years after Jesus' death, and Paul's letters in the 50s, some 20–25 years after it — within the lifetime of the eyewitnesses. That is like writing the first history of the American Revolution today from stories passed down by word of mouth.

Muslim scholars themselves admit the problem. Tradition says al-Bukhari examined about **600,000** reports and accepted only some **7,000** (about 2,600 without repetitions) — that is, the great majority of reports circulating in his day were judged unreliable. Ibn Hisham, who edited Ibn Ishaq, says he left out things "which would distress certain people."

# 3. The first revelation: "poet or possessed?"

The most trusted Muslim source describes Muhammad's first encounter (Sahih al-Bukhari 3 and 6982): the being in the cave seized him and squeezed him so hard that he could not bear it, three times, commanding him to "Read!" Muhammad returned home trembling, saying "Cover me!" and feared for his life. His wife **Khadijah** took him to her Christian cousin **Waraqa ibn Nawfal**, who — without having seen anything — told him it was the _Namus_ (Gabriel) who had come to Moses.

> "But after a few days Waraqa died and the Divine Inspiration was also paused for a while and the Prophet became so sad as we have heard that he intended several times to throw himself from the tops of high mountains and every time he went up the top of a mountain in order to throw himself down, Gabriel would appear before him and say, 'O Muhammad! You are indeed Allah's Apostle in truth.'" (Sahih al-Bukhari 6982)

The earliest biography is even more direct. Muhammad says that no one was more hateful to him than an ecstatic poet or a man possessed, and that after the first visit he thought: "**Woe is me, poet or possessed**… I will go to the top of the mountain and throw myself down" (Ibn Ishaq, Guillaume translation, p. 106). In the account of his childhood, his foster-father feared the boy had had a stroke, and his mother Amina asked: "**Do you fear that a demon has possessed him?**" (p. 72).

Muslim scholars answer that the suicide report in Bukhari is an addition by the narrator al-Zuhri ("as we have heard"), not part of the main chain. Even so, it is in the most trusted collection, and the same story is in the earliest biography. So the first person to doubt the source of Muhammad's revelation was **Muhammad himself** — and the person who reassured him was a relative who had seen nothing.

That is why the Qur'an has to insist that he is not mad and not inspired by a devil:

> "Thou art not, for thy Lord's favour unto thee, a madman." (Qur'an 68:2) "And your comrade is not mad… Nor is this the utterance of a devil worthy to be stoned." (81:22, 25)

Muhammad himself later said he had been bewitched for a time, so that he imagined he had done things he had not done (Sahih al-Bukhari 5763–5765), and the Qur'an admits that Satan can insert words into a prophet's message (22:52) — the background of the "Satanic verses" incident reported by Ibn Ishaq and al-Tabari, in which Muhammad briefly praised three pagan goddesses of Mecca. The Bible warns:

> **2 Corinthians 11:14:** "And no marvel; for Satan himself is transformed into an angel of light."

> **Matthew 24:24:** "For there shall arise false Christs, and false prophets, and shall shew great signs and wonders; insomuch that, if it were possible, they shall deceive the very elect."

# 4. The Qur'an confirms the Bible — and that sinks Islam

The Qur'an repeatedly says that the Torah and the Gospel (_Injil_) are God's revelation, that it came to **confirm** them, and that Jews and Christians must judge by them:

> "Say: O People of the Scripture! Ye have naught (of guidance) till ye observe the Torah and the Gospel and that which was revealed unto you from your Lord." (Qur'an 5:68)

> "And if thou (Muhammad) art in doubt concerning that which We reveal unto thee, then question those who read the Scripture (that was) before thee." (10:94)

See also 3:3, 5:46-47 and 7:157 (the Prophet "whom they will find described in the Torah and the Gospel which are with them").

Many Muslims today answer that the Bible has been corrupted. But the Qur'an says that **no one can change God's words**:

> "Perfected is the Word of thy Lord in truth and justice. There is naught that can change His words." (6:115; see also 6:34, 10:64, 18:27)

This leaves only two options, and both refute Islam — the "Islamic dilemma":

- **If the Bible in Muhammad's day was reliable**, then today's Bible is too, because we have complete manuscripts from centuries **before** Muhammad: Codex Sinaiticus (c. 350) contains the entire New Testament; Codex Vaticanus (c. 325–350) all the Gospels; the Dead Sea Scrolls contain the Old Testament books a thousand years older still. They say what our Bibles say: that Jesus is the Son of God, that He died on the cross for our sins and rose again (John 3:16; 1 Cor 15:3-4). The Qur'an would then be confirming a book that contradicts it.
- **If the Bible had already been corrupted**, then the Qur'an was wrong to tell Christians to judge by it, wrong to tell Muhammad to ask its readers, and wrong to say God's words cannot be changed.

> **John 3:16:** "For God so loved the world, that he gave his only begotten Son, that whosoever believeth in him should not perish, but have everlasting life."

> **Isaiah 40:8:** "The grass withereth, the flower fadeth: but the word of our God shall stand for ever."

# 5. Muhammad did not know what Christians believe

The Qur'an attacks a "Trinity" that no church has ever taught: God, Jesus **and Mary**.

> "And when Allah saith: O Jesus, son of Mary! Didst thou say unto mankind: Take me and my mother for two gods beside Allah?" (Qur'an 5:116)

Just before this, it argues that Jesus and Mary cannot be divine because "they both used to eat food" (5:75), and it says "Allah is the third of three" (5:73). No Christian group ever confessed Mary as part of the Godhead. A book that came down from the God who knows all things would not misunderstand the faith it claims to correct.

The Qur'an's picture of Jesus also comes, in part, from **legends written centuries after Him**:

- Jesus making clay birds and bringing them to life (3:49; 5:110) — from the _Infancy Gospel of Thomas_ (2nd century), a story the church never accepted.
- Jesus speaking from the cradle (19:29-30) — found in the _Arabic Infancy Gospel_ (5th–6th century).
- Mary giving birth under a palm tree that drops dates for her (19:23-26) — from the _Gospel of Pseudo-Matthew_ (6th–7th century).
- Mary called "sister of Aaron" and daughter of Imran (19:28; 66:12) — apparently confusing her with Miriam, the sister of Moses and Aaron, who lived about 1,400 years earlier.

The Qur'an adds Jesus' title "Messiah" and His virgin birth and miracles — but denies His death:

> "They slew him not nor crucified him, but it appeared so unto them." (4:157)

That single sentence, written about 600 years after the event by a man who was not there, contradicts **every** first-century source: the four Gospels and Paul, and also the non-Christian witnesses — Josephus, Tacitus, Mara bar Serapion, Lucian and the Talmud (see _Evidence for Jesus_). The crucifixion is one of the best-established facts of ancient history.

# 6. Was Muhammad foretold in the Bible?

Muslims point to two passages:

- **"A Prophet like unto me" (Deuteronomy 18:15-18).** Moses said God would raise up the prophet "**from the midst of thee, of thy brethren**" — from Israel. The New Testament applies it to Jesus (Acts 3:20-22), a Jew who, like Moses, mediated a covenant and worked signs before the people.
- **The "Comforter" (John 14–16).** The Qur'an says Jesus announced a messenger named Ahmad (61:6), and some identify him with the _Paraclete_. But Jesus said the Comforter is the **Spirit of truth**, whom the world cannot see, who **dwells in** the disciples, and who would come "not many days hence" (Acts 1:5) — not a man born six centuries later.

> **Deuteronomy 18:15:** "The LORD thy God will raise up unto thee a Prophet from the midst of thee, of thy brethren, like unto me; unto him ye shall hearken;"

> **John 14:16-17:** "And I will pray the Father, and he shall give you another Comforter, that he may abide with you for ever; Even the Spirit of truth; whom the world cannot receive, because it seeth him not, neither knoweth him: but ye know him; for he dwelleth with you, and shall be in you."

> **John 14:26:** "But the Comforter, which is the Holy Ghost, whom the Father will send in my name, he shall teach you all things, and bring all things to your remembrance, whatsoever I have said unto you."

# 7. Has the Qur'an been perfectly preserved?

Muslims are taught that the Qur'an has been preserved letter for letter since Muhammad. Their own sources say otherwise.

- **It was not written down as a book in Muhammad's lifetime.** After the battle of Yamama (632), where many men who had memorized the Qur'an were killed, Umar feared that "a large part of the Qur'an may be lost," and Abu Bakr had Zayd ibn Thabit collect it "from parchments, scapula, leaf-stalks of date palms and from the memories of men" (Sahih al-Bukhari 4986).
- **Muhammad forgot verses.** He heard a man reciting and said, "May Allah bestow His mercy on him, as he has reminded me of such-and-such verses of such-and-such surahs, which I had missed" (Sahih al-Bukhari 5038). The Qur'an itself speaks of revelation God "abrogates or causes to be forgotten" (2:106).
- **A whole surah was lost.** A companion of Muhammad, Abu Musa, said they used to recite a surah as long as surah 9, but he had forgotten it except for one line (Sahih Muslim 1050).
- **Verses lost or removed.** Umar said that the "verse of stoning" for adultery was revealed and recited, and feared people would one day say they did not find it in the Book (Sahih al-Bukhari 6829–6830). It is not in today's Qur'an. Aisha said a verse about breastfeeding an adult ten times was revealed, then replaced by five, and was still being recited when Muhammad died (Sahih Muslim 1452) — it is not in the Qur'an either. And in Sunan Ibn Majah 1944 (graded _hasan_), Aisha says the verses of stoning and of breastfeeding were written on a page under her bed, and while the household was busy with Muhammad's death, "**a tame sheep came in and ate it**."
- **The official version was imposed and the others burned.** Caliph Uthman (c. 650) had an official copy made, sent it to every province, and "ordered that all the other Qur'anic materials, whether written in fragmentary manuscripts or whole copies, be burnt" (Sahih al-Bukhari 4987).
- **Muhammad's own recommended teachers disagreed.** He said: "Learn the Qur'an from four: Abdullah ibn Mas'ud, Salim, Mu'adh and Ubayy ibn Ka'b" (Sahih al-Bukhari 3808). The first, **Ibn Mas'ud**, who had learned some seventy surahs from Muhammad's own mouth, objected to the official copy, refused to hand his over, and complained that a younger man had been put in charge of it (Jami' at-Tirmidhi 3104). His codex did not contain surahs 113 and 114, which he did not consider part of the Qur'an (Sahih al-Bukhari 4977 and its explanations) — 111 surahs, not 114. **Ubayy**'s codex, according to early reports, had two extra surahs (116).
- **Variant texts exist today.** The Sana'a palimpsest, one of the oldest Qur'an manuscripts (7th century), has an erased lower text that differs from the standard Qur'an in wording and verse order (Sadeghi & Goudarzi, _Der Islam_ 87, 2012). And the Qur'an printed today is not one text: most of the world reads the _Hafs_ transmission, but North and West Africa read _Warsh_, with differences in letters and wording.

The Bible's copyists never claimed a miracle of letter-perfect copying; instead, God gave us thousands of early manuscripts that can be compared, so the original wording can be recovered. Islam claims letter-perfect preservation — and burned the evidence that would let us check it.

# 8. Muhammad's wives and convenient revelations

Muhammad married **Khadijah** when he was about 25 and she about 40, and he had no other wife while she lived. After her death he took many wives — eleven at the same time, according to Sahih al-Bukhari 268 — while the Qur'an limits other men to four (4:3). A special revelation made the exception:

> "…a privilege for thee only, not for the (rest of) believers." (Qur'an 33:50)

- **Aisha** was "married to him when she was six years old and he consummated his marriage with her when she was nine years old" (Sahih al-Bukhari 5133; also 3894, Sahih Muslim 1422). Muhammad was then about 53.
- **Zaynab** was the wife of his adopted son Zayd. A revelation (33:37) approved Muhammad's marrying her after Zayd divorced her, and the next verses ended adoption as a legal relationship (33:4-5, 40).
- When such revelations came, Aisha said to him: "**I feel that your Lord hastens in fulfilling your wishes and desires**" (Sahih al-Bukhari 4788).

Jesus, by contrast, asked nothing for Himself and gave His life.

# 9. Violence and the treatment of women

The Qur'an contains commands like these, given in the context of Muhammad's wars, which groups from the early conquests until today have taken as permanent:

> "Fight against such of those who have been given the Scripture as believe not in Allah nor the Last Day… until they pay the tribute readily, being brought low." (Qur'an 9:29; see also 9:5, 8:12, 47:4, 5:33)

> "As for those from whom ye fear rebellion, admonish them and banish them to beds apart, and scourge them." (4:34)

Many Muslims live peacefully and read these verses as limited to their historical setting. But the founder of a religion is its model, and Muhammad led military campaigns, ordered executions and took captives as concubines (Qur'an 4:24; 33:50). Jesus told His followers to love their enemies and to put away the sword.

> **Matthew 5:43-44:** "Ye have heard that it hath been said, Thou shalt love thy neighbour, and hate thine enemy. But I say unto you, Love your enemies, bless them that curse you, do good to them that hate you, and pray for them which despitefully use you, and persecute you;"

> **Matthew 26:52:** "Then said Jesus unto him, Put up again thy sword into his place: for all they that take the sword shall perish with the sword."

# 10. Salvation by scales — with no assurance

In Islam, two recording angels write down every deed (Qur'an 50:17-18; 82:10-12), and on the Day of Judgment the deeds are weighed: "Then, as for him whose scales are heavy, he will live a pleasant life. But as for him whose scales are light, the Bereft and Hungry One will be his mother" (101:6-9). The pillars include obligatory almsgiving (_zakat_, 2.5% of savings). Of those who don't pay it, Muhammad said that their wealth will become "a bald-headed poisonous male snake with two black spots over the eyes," which will wind around their neck and bite their cheeks, saying "**I am your wealth, I am your treasure**" (Sahih al-Bukhari 1403; Qur'an 3:180).

No Muslim can know whether his scales will be heavy enough — not even Muhammad: "Nor know I what will be done with me or with you" (Qur'an 46:9). He said, "None of you will enter Paradise by his deeds… not even I, unless Allah bestows His favour and mercy on me" (Sahih al-Bukhari 6467). The only guaranteed way is to die fighting in Allah's cause (Qur'an 3:169; 9:111).

The gospel is the opposite: salvation is God's gift, paid in full by Jesus on the cross, received by faith — and the believer can **know** he has eternal life.

> **Ephesians 2:8-9:** "For by grace are ye saved through faith; and that not of yourselves: it is the gift of God: Not of works, lest any man should boast."

> **1 John 5:13:** "These things have I written unto you that believe on the name of the Son of God; that ye may know that ye have eternal life, and that ye may believe on the name of the Son of God."

# 11. Strange teachings in the "authentic" hadith

The following are not from weak or fringe sources. They are in collections that Sunni Islam considers the most reliable books after the Qur'an. A Muslim is expected to accept a _sahih_ hadith as true.

### Paradise

- **72 wives.** The martyr receives six rewards, including that "he is married to seventy-two wives among al-Hur al-'Ayn" — the virgins of Paradise (Jami' at-Tirmidhi 1663, graded _hasan sahih_ by at-Tirmidhi). The popular "72 virgins" comes from here.
- **The strength of a hundred men.** "The believer will be given such and such strength in Paradise for intercourse." Asked whether he would be able for that, Muhammad said: "He will be given the strength of a hundred" (Jami' at-Tirmidhi 2536, _hasan_).
- **See-through wives.** The first people to enter Paradise will each have two wives "so beautiful, pure and transparent that the marrow of the bones of their legs will be seen through the flesh" (Sahih al-Bukhari 3245, 3254).
- On earth, Muhammad visited all his eleven wives in one night, and his companions said he "was given the strength of thirty men" (Sahih al-Bukhari 268).

Jesus taught that in the resurrection people "neither marry, nor are given in marriage, but are as the angels of God" (Matt 22:30).

### Nature and science

- **The sun sets in a muddy spring.** Dhul-Qarnayn traveled "till, when he reached the setting-place of the sun, he found it setting in a muddy spring" (Qur'an 18:86).
- **The sun prostrates under God's throne every night** and asks permission to rise again; one day it will be told to rise from the west (Sahih al-Bukhari 3199).
- **A fly in your drink:** "If a fly falls in the vessel of any of you, let him dip all of it into the vessel and then throw it away, for in one of its wings there is a disease and in the other there is healing" (Sahih al-Bukhari 3320).
- **Camel urine as medicine.** Muhammad told sick men to drink camels' milk and urine; when they then killed the herdsman, he had their hands and feet cut off, their eyes branded with hot iron, and left them on the rocks to die of thirst (Sahih al-Bukhari 233).
- **Black seed** "is a healing for every disease except death" (Sahih al-Bukhari 5688).

### Satan and animals

- A man slept until morning without praying; Muhammad said, "Satan urinated in his ears" (Sahih al-Bukhari 1144).
- "Satan stays in the upper part of the nose all night," so one should wash the nose on waking (Sahih al-Bukhari 3295). Yawning is from Satan (3289).
- **Rats are a lost tribe of Israel:** "A group of Israelites were lost. Nobody knows what they did. I think they were cursed and changed into rats," because rats will not drink camel's milk but will drink sheep's milk (Sahih al-Bukhari 3305). The Qur'an also says God turned Sabbath-breakers into apes (2:65; 7:166).
- **Monkeys stoning an adulteress:** a narrator in al-Bukhari's collection says that before Islam he saw a group of monkeys stoning a she-monkey for adultery, and he joined them in stoning her (Sahih al-Bukhari 3849).

### Breastfeeding an adult

Sahla complained that Salim, a grown man who had been raised in her house, still came in to her. Muhammad told her: "**Suckle him** and you would become unlawful for him" (Sahih Muslim 1453) — the same ruling as the "verse" the sheep ate.

# Conclusion

Christianity rests on public events, many eyewitnesses who died for what they saw, and documents written within their lifetimes. Islam rests on one man's private experience, which he himself first feared came from a demon; on a book that confirms the Scriptures it then contradicts; on reports written two centuries later; and on a text whose own sources describe lost verses, burned copies and an eaten page. The Qur'an denies the cross that every first-century witness affirms.

Jesus did not point to a future prophet. He said:

> **John 14:6:** "Jesus saith unto him, I am the way, the truth, and the life: no man cometh unto the Father, but by me."

> **Acts 4:12:** "Neither is there salvation in any other: for there is none other name under heaven given among men, whereby we must be saved."
