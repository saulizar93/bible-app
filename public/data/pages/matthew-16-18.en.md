## The claim

> **Matthew 16:13-19:** "When Jesus came into the coasts of Caesarea Philippi, he asked his disciples, saying, Whom do men say that I the Son of man am? And they said, Some say that thou art John the Baptist: some, Elias; and others, Jeremias, or one of the prophets. He saith unto them, But whom say ye that I am? And Simon Peter answered and said, Thou art the Christ, the Son of the living God. And Jesus answered and said unto him, Blessed art thou, Simon Bar-jona: for flesh and blood hath not revealed it unto thee, but my Father which is in heaven. And I say also unto thee, That thou art Peter, and upon this rock I will build my church; and the gates of hell shall not prevail against it. And I will give unto thee the keys of the kingdom of heaven: and whatsoever thou shalt bind on earth shall be bound in heaven: and whatsoever thou shalt loose on earth shall be loosed in heaven."

The Roman Catholic Church reads this passage as the foundation of the papacy: Peter is "this rock," Christ gave him authority over the whole church, and that authority passes to the bishops of Rome as his successors. The First Vatican Council (1870) defined it as dogma and claimed that this is how the Church has always understood the text:

> "To this absolutely manifest teaching of the sacred Scriptures, **as it has always been understood by the Catholic Church**, are clearly opposed the distorted opinions of those who… deny that Peter… was invested by Christ with a true and proper primacy of jurisdiction." (_Pastor Aeternus_, ch. 1)

This page tests that claim against the Fathers of the early church. In short:

- The Fathers did **not** agree on what "this rock" means. Most said it is **Peter's confession of faith**, or **Christ Himself**; others said **all the apostles**; a minority said **Peter**.
- Even the Fathers who said "Peter" did **not** conclude that the bishop of Rome rules the whole church.
- A seventeenth-century French Catholic scholar who counted the patristic interpretations found that about **four out of five** Fathers did not identify the rock with Peter. A Catholic archbishop presented those figures to the bishops at the First Vatican Council.
- Rome binds itself to interpret Scripture only "according to the unanimous consent of the Fathers" — and then defines a doctrine on a verse on which the Fathers were not unanimous.

Much of the patristic material on this page is gathered in William Webster, _The Matthew 16 Controversy: Peter and the Rock_ (Christian Resources, 1996). Bible quotations are from the King James Version; the Fathers are quoted from the _Ante-Nicene Fathers_ and _Nicene and Post-Nicene Fathers_ translations.

# 1. What the text says

- **Peter and the rock.** Jesus says, "thou art **Peter** (_Petros_), and upon this **rock** (_petra_) I will build my church." He does not say "upon you." The rock is introduced right after Peter's confession, "Thou art the Christ, the Son of the living God," which Jesus says was revealed by the Father.
- **Christ is the foundation.** Throughout Scripture the rock and the foundation is God and His Christ:

> **1 Corinthians 3:11:** "For other foundation can no man lay than that is laid, which is Jesus Christ."

> **1 Corinthians 10:4:** "And did all drink the same spiritual drink: for they drank of that spiritual Rock that followed them: and that Rock was Christ."

> **Isaiah 28:16:** "Therefore thus saith the Lord GOD, Behold, I lay in Zion for a foundation a stone, a tried stone, a precious corner stone, a sure foundation: he that believeth shall not make haste."

> **1 Peter 2:4-8:** "To whom coming, as unto a living stone, disallowed indeed of men, but chosen of God, and precious, Ye also, as lively stones, are built up a spiritual house, an holy priesthood, to offer up spiritual sacrifices, acceptable to God by Jesus Christ. Wherefore also it is contained in the scripture, Behold, I lay in Sion a chief corner stone, elect, precious: and he that believeth on him shall not be confounded. Unto you therefore which believe he is precious: but unto them which be disobedient, the stone which the builders disallowed, the same is made the head of the corner, And a stone of stumbling, and a rock of offence, even to them which stumble at the word, being disobedient: whereunto also they were appointed."

> **Psalm 18:2:** "The LORD is my rock, and my fortress, and my deliverer; my God, my strength, in whom I will trust; my buckler, and the horn of my salvation, and my high tower."

- **Peter himself** applies the stone to Jesus (1 Peter 2:4-8) and calls himself "also an elder" (1 Peter 5:1). He never claims to be the rock or the ruler of the church.
- **All the apostles are foundation stones**, not Peter alone:

> **Ephesians 2:20:** "And are built upon the foundation of the apostles and prophets, Jesus Christ himself being the chief corner stone;"

> **Revelation 21:14:** "And the wall of the city had twelve foundations, and in them the names of the twelve apostles of the Lamb."

- **The keys and binding and loosing** were given to all the disciples, not to Peter alone:

> **Matthew 18:18:** "Verily I say unto you, Whatsoever ye shall bind on earth shall be bound in heaven: and whatsoever ye shall loose on earth shall be loosed in heaven."

> **John 20:22-23:** "And when he had said this, he breathed on them, and saith unto them, Receive ye the Holy Ghost: Whose soever sins ye remit, they are remitted unto them; and whose soever sins ye retain, they are retained."

- **Immediately afterwards**, Jesus rebukes Peter: "Get thee behind me, Satan: thou art an offence unto me" (Matthew 16:23). Peter is a stone that can stumble; the rock that cannot fail is Christ.
- **No successor is mentioned.** Nothing in the passage speaks of Rome, of a bishop of Rome, or of anyone succeeding Peter in any authority.

# 2. What the Fathers said

### The rock is Peter's confession of faith

- **Hilary of Poitiers** (c. 356): "This faith it is which is the foundation of the Church… **Upon this rock of confession is the Church built**." (_On the Trinity_ 6.36-37)
- **Ambrose of Milan** (c. 382): "**Faith, then, is the foundation of the Church**, for it was not said of Peter's flesh, but of his faith, that 'the gates of hell shall not prevail against it.'" (_On the Incarnation_ 5.34)
- **John Chrysostom** (c. 390): "'Upon this rock I will build my church'; **that is, on the faith of his confession**." (_Homilies on Matthew_ 54.3)
- **Cyril of Alexandria** (c. 425) calls the rock "the unshakable and firm faith of the disciple" on which the church is founded (_Dialogues on the Trinity_ 4).
- **Theodoret of Cyrus**, **Isidore of Pelusium** and many others among the Greek Fathers read it the same way (Webster gathers their texts).

### The rock is Christ

- **Augustine of Hippo** (c. 416): "For **petra** (rock) is not derived from **Peter**, but Peter from petra; just as Christ is not called so from the Christian, but the Christian from Christ. For on this very account the Lord said, 'On this rock will I build my Church,' because Peter had said, 'Thou art the Christ, the Son of the living God.' On this rock, therefore, He said, which thou hast confessed, I will build my Church. **For the Rock was Christ**." (_Tractates on John_ 124.5)
- At the end of his life Augustine reviewed his earlier writings and said that he had sometimes explained the rock as Peter, but more often as Christ: "For it was not said to him, 'Thou art the rock,' but 'Thou art Peter.' **But the rock was Christ**… Of these two meanings let the reader choose the more probable." (_Retractations_ 1.21) The greatest Father of the Latin Church left the question open — which is impossible if the papal interpretation were the constant tradition.
- **Jerome**, commenting on this verse, says that Christ gave Simon the name Peter because he "believed in **the rock, Christ**" (_Commentary on Matthew_ 16:18).

### The rock is all the apostles, or every believer

- **Origen** (c. 245): "If we too have said like Peter, 'Thou art the Christ, the Son of the living God'… **we become a Peter**… For a rock is every disciple of Christ… But if you suppose that upon that one Peter only the whole church is built by God, what would you say about John the son of thunder or each one of the Apostles?" (_Commentary on Matthew_ 12.10-11)
- **Jerome** (c. 393): "You say, the Church was founded upon Peter: although elsewhere the same is attributed to all the Apostles, and they all receive the keys of the kingdom of heaven, and **the strength of the Church depends upon them all alike**." (_Against Jovinianus_ 1.26)

### Fathers who said "Peter" — but not the papacy

Some Fathers did say that Peter is the rock. But none of the early ones drew Rome's conclusion:

- **Tertullian** (c. 220) said the promise was given to **Peter personally**, and attacked a bishop who claimed it passed to the church of bishops: "What sort of man are you, subverting and wholly changing the manifest intention of the Lord, **conferring this gift personally upon Peter**? 'On thee,' He says, 'will I build My church'… not on the church" (_On Modesty_ 21).
- **Cyprian of Carthage** (c. 251) said the church was built on Peter as a **sign of unity**, but that "**the other apostles were also the same as was Peter, endowed with a like partnership both of honour and power**" (_On the Unity of the Church_ 4). For Cyprian every bishop sits in the chair of Peter. When Bishop Stephen of Rome tried to impose his view on baptism, Cyprian and the African bishops refused, saying: "**neither does any of us set himself up as a bishop of bishops**" (_Seventh Council of Carthage_, 256). (A second version of Cyprian's chapter 4, with stronger language about a "primacy," also survives; scholars debate which is original, but even it says nothing of Rome ruling the other churches.)
- **Firmilian of Caesarea** (c. 256) mocked the same Stephen "who so boasts of the place of his episcopate, and contends that he holds **the succession from Peter**," and told him that by trying to excommunicate everyone else, "you have excluded yourself alone from all" (_Letter to Cyprian_ [Cyprian, _Letter_ 74/75], 17, 24).

### What about the bishops of Rome themselves?

Even a pope rejected a universal bishop. When the patriarch of Constantinople took the title "ecumenical (universal) patriarch," **Gregory the Great**, bishop of Rome (590–604), wrote to the emperor:

> "I confidently say that **whosoever calls himself, or desires to be called, Universal Priest, is in his elation the precursor of Antichrist**, because he proudly puts himself above all others." (_Letters_ 7.33)

Gregory refused the title for himself as well. Six centuries later Gregory VII declared "that the Roman pontiff alone can with right be called universal" (_Dictatus Papae_ 2, 1075).

### The early councils

- **Nicaea (325), canon 6**, gave the bishop of Alexandria authority over Egypt, Libya and Pentapolis, "since the like is customary for the bishop of Rome also" — treating Rome as one regional church among others, not as the ruler of all.
- **Constantinople (381), canon 3**, and **Chalcedon (451), canon 28**, gave Constantinople privileges next to Rome **because it was the imperial capital** — "because it is New Rome." The councils explained Rome's honour by the city's political rank, not by Matthew 16:18. (Rome rejected both canons.)

# 3. Jean de Launoy's count

**Jean de Launoy** (1603–1678), a French Catholic priest and doctor of the Sorbonne, set out to find how the Fathers and ancient writers had actually interpreted "this rock." He found five interpretations:

| "This rock" is… | Number of Fathers |
|---|---|
| Peter's **faith** or **confession** ("Thou art the Christ") | **44** |
| **Peter** | **17** |
| **Christ** Himself | **16** |
| **All the apostles** | **8** |
| All the faithful | (a few) |

Of the 85 who chose one of the first four answers, **68 — about 80% — did not identify the rock with Peter**. And the 17 who did say "Peter" did not say "the pope."

At the First Vatican Council (1870), **Peter Richard Kenrick**, Catholic archbishop of St. Louis, prepared a speech against the definition of papal primacy and infallibility, citing Launoy's figures. He concluded: "If we are bound to follow the greater number of the Fathers in this matter, then we must hold for certain that the word '_petra_' means **not Peter professing the faith, but the faith professed by Peter**." Kenrick never got to deliver it at the council; he had it printed in Naples (_Concio in Concilio Vaticano habenda at non habita_, 1870).

# 4. Rome does not follow the tradition it claims

The Council of Trent and the First Vatican Council declared that no one may interpret Scripture "contrary to **the unanimous consent of the Fathers**," and every Catholic priest once had to swear: "Nor will I ever take and interpret it otherwise than according to the unanimous consent of the Fathers" (Profession of the Tridentine Faith, 1564).

But on Matthew 16:18 there **is** no unanimous consent of the Fathers. Most of them read the verse differently from Rome. So when Vatican I declared that Peter is the rock with a primacy of jurisdiction passed to the bishops of Rome, "as it has always been understood by the Catholic Church," it did not follow the Fathers; it overruled them.

Even the modern _Catechism of the Catholic Church_ keeps both readings side by side. In one place it says: "**On the rock of this faith confessed by St. Peter, Christ built his Church**" (424) — the reading of Hilary, Ambrose and Chrysostom. In another it says that Christ "made him the 'rock' of his Church" (881).

# Conclusion

The Fathers did not read Matthew 16:18 the way Rome does today. Origen said every disciple who confesses Christ becomes a rock; Hilary, Ambrose and Chrysostom said the rock is Peter's confession of faith; Augustine said "the rock was Christ" and left the reader to choose; Jerome said the church rests on all the apostles alike; Tertullian and Cyprian, who called Peter the rock, denied that the promise made one bishop the ruler of the others; and a bishop of Rome called the claim to be universal bishop the mark of a forerunner of Antichrist. A Catholic scholar counted the Fathers and found four in five against the papal reading, and a Catholic archbishop tried to tell that to Vatican I. The rock on which the church is built is the truth Peter confessed — that Jesus is "the Christ, the Son of the living God" — and Christ Himself.

"For other foundation can no man lay than that is laid, which is Jesus Christ." (1 Corinthians 3:11)
